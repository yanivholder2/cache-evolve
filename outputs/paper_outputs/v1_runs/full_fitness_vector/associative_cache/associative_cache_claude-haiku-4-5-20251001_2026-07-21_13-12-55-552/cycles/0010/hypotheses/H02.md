# H02

Amortize characterization tracking overhead by batching bias recalculation every 64 operations instead of continuous per-operation tracking to reduce latency spikes while maintaining hit-rate adaptation.

## Parent

cycle/0009/v003

## Changes

Add an `operation_counter: u32` field to `AssociativeCache` to track operation-granularity progress during the characterization phase. In the `put()` and `get_and_update_item()` methods, increment the counter on every operation. Modify the tracking logic (currently executed on every get during characterization) to only execute when `operation_counter % 64 == 0`, deferring bias calculation to every 64th operation rather than every operation. Specifically, in `get_and_update_item()`, wrap the timestamp comparison and hit-counting logic with `if self.operation_counter % 64 == 0 { ... }`. Keep the CHARACTERIZATION_PHASE_THRESHOLD at 512 operations; characterization still terminates after 512 puts, but tracking overhead is concentrated into 8 checkpoints (every 64 ops) instead of spread across all 512 operations. This directly addresses cycle 0009's finding that continuous per-operation tracking creates latency degradation, particularly p99 spikes.

## Rationale

Cycle 0009 v001's amortization attempt failed by consolidating overhead into operation spikes (p99 doubled). Cycle 0009 v003 showed that continuous per-operation tracking (timestamp comparisons) adds 2–3× latency. The hypothesis differs from v001 by targeting the tracking logic itself (not the eviction buffer), batching at a coarser granularity (64 ops) than v001's 32-op deferral, and skipping expensive per-operation comparisons rather than merely deferring them. If per-operation tracking cost is the primary latency driver, reducing frequency of comparisons proportionally should reduce p99 latency; batching at 64-op boundaries matches CPU cache-flush cycles and may avoid consolidation spikes.
