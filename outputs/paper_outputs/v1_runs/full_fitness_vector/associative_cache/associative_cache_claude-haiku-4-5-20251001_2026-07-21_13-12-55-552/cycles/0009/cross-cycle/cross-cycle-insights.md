# Cross-Cycle Insights

## Recurring wins

- **Implementation-level micro-optimization on the hot path** (cycle 0004 v003: early-exit + cache-line alignment) remains the only variant across nine cycles to achieve net fitness improvement. Optimization targeting frequent operations (hit detection) succeeds; optimization targeting rare paths (eviction) fails.
- **Preserved baseline structure resists regression**: ClassicLRU's 128×16 geometry and vector-based movement operations remain competitive despite all direct policy/geometry variants regressing, suggesting the baseline is finely tuned to modern CPU memory hierarchies.
- **Simple policies outperform complex ones**: FIFO's zero-cost bookkeeping and ClassicLRU's recency-only eviction consistently match or exceed hit-rate of more sophisticated policies (LFU, frequency-aware, adaptive), confirming that Zipfian access patterns are well-served by recency alone.

## Recurring failures

- **Policy changes add per-hit bookkeeping overhead that latency-dominates hit-rate gains**: TimestampLRU (cycle 0002), segment-based LRU (cycle 0004 v002), FIFO with timestamp victim selection (cycle 0004 v001), probabilistic frequency tracking (cycle 0009 v002), and characterization-based insertion bias (cycle 0009 v003) all incurred 30–300% latency regression despite sound algorithmic motivation. Modern CPUs penalize any per-operation conditional logic or field access more than they benefit from improved eviction decisions.
- **Geometry scaling in either direction fails**: Narrower buckets (256×8, cycle 0003) regressed both hit-rate and latency; wider buckets (64×32, 32×64, 16×128) improved hit-rate but caused tail latencies to spike or degraded latency at geometric extremes. Early-exit + cache-line alignment optimizations (cycle 0004 v003's success at 32×64) do not generalize to other bucket configurations (cycle 0005, 0008).
- **Metadata expansion and per-item tracking fields are invariably detrimental**: Every variant adding tracking fields (epochs, frequency counters, insertion-bias buffers) to enable algorithmic refinement paid structural overhead that exceeded any hit-rate or latency benefit. Cycles 0006–0009 confirm that bookkeeping overhead is unavoidable regardless of where it is placed (hot path, deferred, amortized).
- **Amortization consolidates rather than smooths latency**: Deferred buffer updates (cycle 0009 v001) intended to spread cost across operations instead produced p99 latency spikes, with concentrated overhead hitting specific operations (32nd, 64th, etc.). The pattern suggests load imbalance at operation granularity undermines amortization as a latency-reduction strategy.
- **Hit-rate improvements are Pareto-dominated by latency cost**: Cycles 0008–0009 confirm a consistent pattern: any variant achieving measurable hit-rate gain (0.43%–8.1%) incurs latency regression (40–300%) that makes the improvement infeasible. Within the Zipfian, single-threaded design space, hit-rate and latency are tightly coupled and cannot both improve.

## Open questions

- **Fitness validation of cycle 0004 v003 and cycle 0006 v003**: Are these variants true Pareto leaders relative to the full evolution history and across different workload distributions (non-Zipfian access patterns, cache sizes, temporal locality shifts)? Testing robustness is prerequisite to determining whether design space is saturated or opportunity remains.
- **Why does ClassicLRU's O(n) vector manipulation outperform O(1) asymptotic alternatives?** Timestamp updates (cycle 0002), segment-based tracking (cycle 0004 v002), and probabilistic counters (cycle 0009 v002) all provide asymptotic complexity advantages yet regress in practice. Is the answer cache-line coherency, branch prediction, compiler optimization, or memory-layout effects?
- **Is CPU memory hierarchy a fundamental barrier to further optimization?** Every cycle reveals that latency is the binding constraint, and any per-operation bookkeeping is heavily penalized. Have we reached the limits of what single-threaded, fixed-size cache design can achieve, or are there unexamined implementation strategies (vectorization, batching, cache-oblivious layouts) that could amortize overhead?
- **Are learned insertion heuristics viable with lower per-operation cost?** Cycle 0009 v003 achieved +8.1% hit-rate improvement through characterization-based bias, proving algorithmic adaptivity works. Could vectorized or amortized history tracking reduce the 2–3× latency overhead to acceptable levels?
- **Should the next phase pivot to orthogonal directions?** Alternative data structures (cuckoo hashing, Robin Hood variants with amortization), cache-oblivious designs, multi-core efficiency, or learned indexing may offer escape routes from the current Pareto frontier.

## Per-policy notes

- **ClassicLRU (baseline)**: Remains unbeaten across nine cycles despite all variants attempting to improve it. Simple recency-only eviction achieves 0.6611–0.6665 hit-rate on Zipfian workloads; appears to be the policy-efficiency frontier for this access pattern.
- **LFU and frequency-aware variants**: Cycles 0001, 0006 v003 (frequency-based promotion), and 0009 v002 (probabilistic counters) all aimed to exploit Zipfian hot/cold structure. Result: flat hit-rate (no improvement over recency) combined with latency penalties, confirming that frequency awareness provides no discriminator advantage for this workload's access patterns.
- **FIFO**: Cycle 0002 v002 and cycle 0004 v001 showed FIFO maintains baseline hit-rate (0.6611) with zero per-hit bookkeeping, yet latency still regressed due to architectural mismatch or implementation artifact. When FIFO is paired with policy changes elsewhere (timestamp victim selection, cycle 0004 v001), overhead multiplies.
- **Timestamp and deferred-cost variants**: Cycles 0002 v001 (TimestampLRU), 0004 v001 (FIFO + timestamp victim selection), 0009 v001 (amortized buffer updates) all failed to reduce per-hit bookkeeping cost. Deferral does not eliminate cost; it concentrates it into operation spikes.
- **Adaptive and learned insertion**: Cycles 0006 v003 (hot/cold partitioning) and 0009 v003 (characterization-based bias) proved that adaptive policies can improve hit-rate (+0.327% and +8.1% respectively) but at prohibitive latency cost (2–3×). Algorithmic innovation is available but latency-infeasible within current architecture.
- **Geometry-coupled policies**: Cycle 0004 v003's micro-optimizations (early-exit, cache-line alignment) succeeded at 32×64 but do not generalize (cycles 0005, 0008). The implication is that policies and geometry are tightly coupled—optimizations for one configuration become liabilities at others.
