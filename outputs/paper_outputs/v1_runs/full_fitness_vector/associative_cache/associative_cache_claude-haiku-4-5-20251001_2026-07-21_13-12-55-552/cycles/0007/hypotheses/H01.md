# H01

Reduce the hot-segment partition size in cycle/0006/v003 to isolate whether the 0.6988 hit-rate improvement is driven by frequency-aware promotion or by having a larger working-set buffer.

## Parent

cycle/0006/v003

## Changes

Modify the hot/cold partition threshold in v003's dual-index adaptive partitioning. The current implementation splits the 2048-entry cache into approximately two segments (estimated 512+ entries hot, remainder cold). Reduce the hot segment to approximately 1/8 of total capacity (256 entries) while keeping the cold segment at 1792 entries. Preserve all frequency-tracking logic, promotion thresholds, and decay intervals from v003 unchanged. This ablation isolates the algorithmic contribution of frequency-aware promotion from the side effect of a larger working-set buffer. Re-measure hit-rate on the same Zipfian workload to determine if the gain is robust or an artifact of partition size.

## Rationale

Cycle 0006 v003 achieved the best hit-rate (0.6988) but added significant per-operation overhead (dual-index searches, frequency tracking, promotion logic). The cycle-insight questioned whether this gain is a true algorithmic win or a consequence of having more space reserved for "hot" items. By reducing the hot partition, this hypothesis tests whether the frequency-aware promotion itself is driving the hit-rate improvement or whether simply having a larger buffer for recently-accessed items explains the gain. This validates Path A (robustness of recent high performers) and answers the core question: does v003's complexity deliver real benefit or is it over-fitted to the current cache size?
