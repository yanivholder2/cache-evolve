# H03

Implement Robin Hood hashing with backward-shift deletion to reduce probe-distance variance and flatten per-hit latency percentiles without affecting hit-rate or expanding per-item metadata.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Modify the item insertion and deletion logic within bucket operations to implement Robin Hood hashing invariants. Track a small probe distance counter (reusing or adding minimally) for each item in the bucket, representing how far it is from its ideal hash position. During insertion, if a new item has a shorter probe distance than an existing item, swap them (shift the existing item further along the probe sequence). During deletion, perform backward-shift: if an item after a deleted slot has a non-zero probe distance, move it backward to fill the gap and decrement its distance counter. Preserve the 128×16 bucket geometry, ClassicLRU eviction policy, and overall structure. Robin Hood hashing directly addresses probe-distance variance, which manifests as high p99 latency in bucket scans; by equalizing distances, scan cost becomes more predictable.

## Rationale

Cycles 0004–0006 identify recurring latency variance (especially p99 tail latencies) as a persistent bottleneck, even when hit-rate improves. Robin Hood hashing is a classic technique for variance reduction in hash tables that does not require per-item metadata expansion (unlike v001, v002, v003 which all added tracking fields and regressed). This approach redistributes existing data to reduce search cost variance, orthogonal to policy (which is invariant on Zipfian). By flattening latency variance without changing hit-rate or structure, H03 pursues Path B (alternative data structures) and tests whether the variance problem is addressable through smarter probe-distance management rather than policy or geometry changes.
