# H03

Implement saturation-aware eviction-strategy switching: use simple FIFO at low saturation (<70%) and deferred-promotion bulk flushing at high saturation (≥70%), to combine latency efficiency at light load with proven get p99 gains at heavy load.

## Parent

cycle/0003/v003

## Changes

Cycle 0003's v003 demonstrated saturation-aware activation works in principle: conditional logic that activates expensive features only under load reduces overhead on underutilized caches. However, Cycle 0005's v003 failed because the tested workload never saturated, leaving conditional logic unused. Here, implement a mode-switch architecture with measurable benefits at both extremes:

1. Add a saturation threshold flag to LruCache: track saturation (size/capacity) and switch modes at 70% boundary (adjust empirically if needed).
2. In **low-saturation mode** (<70%): use simple FIFO eviction with bounded window (e.g., min(capacity/4, 256) items), no frequency tracking, no deferral—minimal metadata and per-operation overhead.
3. In **high-saturation mode** (≥70%): activate the complete deferred-promotion strategy from Cycle 0006 v002—maintain pending_promotions queue, defer all promotion work from get() to put(), bulk flush at put()—combined with frequency bucketing for selectivity.
4. On mode transition from low→high: allocate promotion-queue metadata and frequency structures.
5. On mode transition from high→low: flush any pending queue, deallocate promotion structures (or keep allocated for zero-cost switching on re-entry).
6. Preserve bounded-window structure in both modes to ensure latency predictability; the change is strategic (which operations to perform), not structural.

This targets per-op-time by minimizing overhead at light load (where frequency tracking and deferral are unnecessary) while preserving the proven deferred-promotion approach at high saturation (where get p99 gains are proven valuable).

## Rationale

Cycle 0003/v003 showed that saturation-aware activation is architecturally sound. Cycle 0005/v003 and 0006/v002 revealed the fundamental asymmetry: at light saturation, frequency tracking and deferred-work mechanics are pure overhead; at high saturation, they provide measurable gain. Unlike Cycle 0005/v003 which added conditional logic to a single strategy, this switches between fundamentally different eviction families: FIFO (cheap, light load) vs. deferred-promotion (expensive, high load). This addresses the cross-cycle open question "How much of per-op-time overhead is workload-saturation-dependent?" If the tested workload stays light, this achieves better per-op-time than the heavyweight deferred-promotion baseline. If saturation increases (e.g., in write-heavy variants), the mode activates proven improvements. This is a strict superset of baseline's capabilities: it performs at least as well at low saturation (matching simple FIFO) and at least as well at high saturation (matching Cycle 0006 v002), with explicit mode boundaries validated empirically.
