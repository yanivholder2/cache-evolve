# H02

Reduce frequency-bucket management overhead by replacing per-item frequency counters with a single global operation counter and time-windowed recency tracking for selective eviction.

## Parent

cycle/0002/v002

## Changes

Replace the per-node `freq: u32` counter and `freq_buckets` structure with simpler time-windowed recency tracking: maintain a global `operation_counter: u64` that increments on every get/put, and store `last_access_op: u64` on each node (8 bytes, same cost as the frequency counter). During eviction, scan the bounded window and select the node with the smallest `last_access_op` value, implementing a time-window-based LRU that approximates frequency recency without maintaining bucket pointers. This trades the pointer-chasing overhead of bucket linkage (unlinking from old bucket, linking to new bucket on every frequency update) for a simple comparison during eviction. Decay old operation counts by periodically resetting the global counter (e.g., every 2×capacity operations), which shifts all `last_access_op` values down uniformly without per-node work. Preserve the bounded-window search strategy from cycle/0002/v002, but simplify the eviction decision from "evict lowest-frequency node in window" to "evict least-recently-accessed node in window." Expected outcome: same or better hit rate (since time-windowed LRU captures recency-based selectivity), but lower get/put latency due to reduced bookkeeping.

## Rationale

Cycle 0002 V002 established that frequency-level bucket indexing achieves 15.6× improvement in put p99 latency while gaining hit rate, but introduced measurable overhead in normal-case latencies (get p99 +122%, put p50 +70%) due to pointer-chasing on frequency updates. Cycle 0008 V002 attempted to eliminate frequency tracking entirely, but pure FIFO with a larger window degraded hit rate by 4.8%, confirming that selectivity beyond simple recency is valuable. This hypothesis proposes a middle ground: keep the selectivity principle (prefer less-recently-accessed items) but implement it via a simpler counter-based mechanism that avoids bucket pointer manipulation. Time-windowed recency is semantically equivalent to frequency on most access patterns and should be nearly free to compute (just store and compare a counter), while preserving the selectivity that cycle/0002/v002 proved effective. This tests whether cycle/0002/v002's hit-rate and latency gains can be preserved with simpler bookkeeping.
