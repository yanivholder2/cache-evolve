## Recurring wins

- **Amortised sampling on the eviction/gate path beats eager probing.** The 1-in-8 fire rate proved durable across cycles 4–6: it preserves latency neutrality while retaining a meaningful hit-rate signal. Anything that fires new sketch probes more often than 1-in-8 on the common put path regresses p50 or p99.
- **Eliminating serial hash data dependencies pays off.** The bijective multiply-xorshift remix (cycle 5 winner) beat a full second SipHash by removing an intra-iteration dependency rather than just reducing hash count. The principle: ILP loss from chained hash data dominates the arithmetic savings of fewer invocations.
- **Sketch row independence is a genuine accuracy lever, not a theoretical nicety.** Both the two-seeded hash split (cycle 4) and the 8× width doubling (cycle 7) improved hit rate by reducing collision noise, confirming that the 4× baseline was collision-limited on this Zipfian trace.
- **Structural policy changes (SLRU segment split) opened a new, orthogonal axis.** The protected-segment variant (cycle 8) was the first non-sketch, non-hash change to deliver a hit-rate gain; it compounded cleanly with prior sketch and gate tuning, proving policy structure and estimation accuracy are independent levers.
- **Compact flag representation (bitset over Vec) is a safe, cost-neutral improvement.** Cycle 9's bitset compaction reduced backing heap 8× with no regression, establishing it as the correct default for any future flag-per-node design.

## Recurring failures

- **Sketch decay in any tested form.** Both the width/2 cadence (cycle 1) and the capacity×10 cadence (cycle 3) hurt hit rate and latency on the LIRS trace. The stable working set makes accumulated frequency a reliable admission signal; blanket half-decay degrades it uniformly and the full-table sweep pressures L2/L3. **Axis is closed.**
- **Counter-width narrowing (u16) as a latency lever.** Tested in cycles 7 and 8; per-access zero-extension overhead consistently cancels the L2 footprint savings at these sketch sizes. **Do not revisit unless per-access cast overhead is eliminated.**
- **Flat sketch allocation and reduced sketch depth.** Cycle 2 showed flat allocation creates cache-set conflicts that dwarf pointer-indirection savings; halving depth degrades collision suppression enough to cascade into eviction errors. **Both are definitively closed.**
- **Best-of-2 victim selection at the LRU cold end.** Tested at full cost (cycle 3), amortised 1-in-8 (cycle 4 v002 was eviction amortisation, not victim selection), and near-zero overhead (cycle 7 v001). The LRU head and its immediate neighbour are equally cold relative to the working set — the admission gate, not victim identity, is the operative filter. **Axis is closed.**
- **Proportional frequency amplification (double-increment on hits).** Cycle 8 v003: cold residents accumulate the boosted signal at the same relative rate as incomers accumulate misses; the `incoming_freq > victim_freq` gate is structurally unaffected and the overhead falls on the majority hit path. **Closed.**
- **Bijective remix chains replacing independent hash seeds.** Cycle 6 v003: a common hash root produces correlated row positions, raising collision noise, and the sequential multiply–rotate–XOR chain stalls out-of-order execution worse than a second independent SipHash. **Closed.**
- **Growing the main cache struct past cache-line boundaries.** Multiple cycles showed that even adding 8 bytes to `TinyLfuCache` can double p50/p99 on code paths that never execute the new logic, via cache-line boundary shift. Any new field must fit existing alignment padding or demonstrate the trade-off is worth it.

## Open questions

- **Can lazy (get-only) promotion reduce put-path dual-list overhead in the SLRU design?** Cycle 9 noted that every full-cache put currently walks and relinks two chains; deferring segment promotion to `get` only may eliminate this cost while preserving the hit-rate benefit of the segment split.
- **Is there a clock-hand or generational eviction policy that delivers SLRU-like admission filtering with lower per-operation bookkeeping than dual-list maintenance?**
- **What is the cycle-level compound ceiling?** The current stack (bijective-remix sketch + amortised admission gate + protected segment + bitset flags) has not been evaluated as a single integrated variant; each layer was added incrementally and the true combined fitness is unknown.
- **Do structural policy parameters (window/protected ratio, probationary-to-protected promotion threshold) still have unexplored headroom?** The protected fraction was pinned at ≤20% and confirmed near-optimal for a Zipfian trace, but the promotion threshold and window-to-main ratio have never been varied across any cycle.
- **Can the admission gate and the protected segment be decoupled?** Cycle 9 observed they are partially redundant — the gate already shields hot keys, so the segment adds only a marginal increment while taxing put-path latency. A cleaner separation may reveal which mechanism carries the majority of the benefit.

## Per-policy notes

- **TinyLFU / Count-Min Sketch:** Four rows are load-bearing — removing two rows (cycle 2) cascaded into eviction errors. Width is a genuine hit-rate lever; 8× outperforms 4× on this Zipfian trace. The correct hash strategy is bijective-remix for the second row pair (not a full second SipHash, not a pure single-hash bit-slice). u16 counters do not improve latency at current sketch sizes. Sketch decay in any form degrades rather than improves accuracy on this workload.
- **LRU / probationary zone:** The LIRS trace's stable working set makes raw LRU a strong baseline; frequency accumulated over time is a reliable signal that aggressive resets destroy. The 1-in-8 amortised probe rate is the empirically validated boundary between latency-safe and latency-harmful gate firing.
- **SLRU / protected segment:** 20% protected fraction is near-optimal for a Zipfian trace; 30% backfires by filling slots with lukewarm items and shrinking the probationary pool. Bitset storage is the correct representation for per-node flags. The dominant cost of the dual-list design is the put-path conditional relinking, not the eviction-path flag lookup. Compounding the protected segment with the amortised gate and 8× sketch yields a winner, but the marginal contribution of each layer diminishes as prior layers already handle the same failure modes.
- **Eviction candidate selection:** Best-of-2 victim comparison is closed — the LRU head and neighbour are fungible cold candidates. The admission gate (`incoming_freq > victim_freq`) is the operative lever, not which cold node is chosen as the victim.
