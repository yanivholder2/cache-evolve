# Cross-Cycle Insights

## Recurring wins

- **Amortised probing preserves latency while capturing directional signal.** Any mechanism that fires on only 1-in-8 evictions rather than every eviction has consistently held latency neutral or better. The amortised strict-admission gate (Cycle 0006 v002) and the amortised best-of-2 probe frequency both confirmed this — the signal survives coarse sampling.
- **Eliminating serial hash dependencies improves throughput.** The single-hash bit-slice (Cycle 0003 v002), the bijective-remix replacement of the second SipHash (Cycle 0005 v002), and the two-seeded-hash bit-slice (Cycle 0004 v001) all show that ILP-hostile data chains matter more than raw instruction count on this hot path. Removing the dependency wins; adding it loses.
- **Row independence in the count-min sketch is load-bearing.** Every attempt to derive all four row positions from a single seed (bit-slice from one hash, or remix chains) has incurred accuracy regression visible as hit-rate decline or tail latency. Two genuinely independent seeded hashes remain the minimum for collision-safe frequency estimation on this Zipfian trace.
- **Structural policy changes (segment split) yield orthogonal gains.** The protected-LRU-segment approach (Cycle 0008 v002) improved hit rate and get-path latency independently of all sketch and gate tuning, confirming that eviction-policy architecture is a separate, complementary axis from frequency estimation.

## Recurring failures

- **Sketch decay at any tested cadence degrades the stable working set.** Both the aggressive `width/2` interval (Cycle 0001) and the moderate `capacity × 10` interval (Cycle 0003) hurt hit rate and latency. The LIRS trace's long-lived hot set makes accumulated frequency a reliable signal; blanket half-decay erases it indiscriminately. This axis is definitively exhausted.
- **Flat or structurally simplified sketch allocations increase cache-set pressure.** Single-allocation flat layout (Cycle 0002) doubled latency versus the multi-segment baseline. Reducing sketch depth from four to two rows (Cycle 0002) degraded collision suppression enough to cascade into hit-rate loss. The baseline four-row, multi-allocation design is well-calibrated; do not revisit its structure.
- **Node-layout compaction below `Option<NonZeroU32>` offers no further gains.** `u32` sentinel encoding recovered latency regression but is fitness-neutral versus baseline. u16 counters (Cycles 0007–0008) consistently incur zero-extension overhead that cancels the cache-footprint savings at current sketch sizes. This axis is closed.
- **Best-of-2 victim selection at the LRU cold end is ineffective.** Both candidate nodes are equally cold relative to the working set; the admission gate comparison is the operative filter. Confirmed refuted in Cycle 0007 even under an implementation that eliminates redundant estimate calls.
- **Proportional frequency amplification (double-increment on hit) is a pure overhead tax.** Cold residents accumulate the boosted signal at the same relative rate as incomers; the `incoming > victim` ordering is unaffected, and the overhead falls on ≈74% of operations. Definitively closed (Cycle 0008 v003).
- **Remix chains cannot substitute for independent hash seeds.** A multi-step bijective chain from a single root produces correlated row positions and also stalls out-of-order execution more than a second independent hash invocation (Cycle 0006 v003). Do not revisit.
- **Combining two independently beneficial mechanisms can compound latency costs non-additively.** Applying a stricter admission gate at a high probe rate and adding extra sketch probes on the same path (Cycle 0006 v001) doubled p50 even though each mechanism individually was manageable. Structural layout changes (struct-size growth) amplify execution overhead on adjacent code paths.

## Open questions

- **Can the protected-segment flag storage be made compact enough to avoid the 47% struct-size growth seen in Cycle 0008?** A bitset or reuse of existing node metadata could preserve the segment split's hit-rate and latency gains without the compounding cost at higher capacities.
- **What is the optimal protected-segment ratio?** The 20% threshold has not been compared against 10%, 30%, or an adaptive target; the gain may peak at a different fraction.
- **Does layering the amortised admission gate on top of the protected-segment baseline compound their independent hit-rate benefits, or does the gate become redundant once hot items are shielded from the victim pool?** These mechanisms have not been combined.
- **Are structural policy parameters — window/probationary/protected ratio, promotion threshold — a further orthogonal axis?** Eight cycles have now touched sketch structure, hash work, eviction amortisation, and segment split, but the window-to-main ratio has never been varied.
- **Can two seeded hashes cover all four rows with only one extra invocation by having each seed cover two rows via the bit-slice approach?** This specific combination (independence + reduced hash calls) was predicted in Cycle 0003 but never cleanly evaluated against the bijective-remix winner that became the subsequent baseline.

## Per-policy notes

- **TinyLFU admission gate:** The `incoming_freq > victim_freq` comparison is the decisive eviction filter. Tightening it (e.g., `> victim_freq + 1`) reliably improves hit rate but causes residency churn at full fire rate; amortising to 1-in-8 recovers latency neutrality. 1-in-4 is the latency break point. No further amortisation ratio is worth testing unless pre-computed estimates can be reused on the probe path.
- **Count-min sketch:** Four rows, multi-allocation, with two independently seeded hashes covering pairs of rows via bit-slicing is the current best-known configuration. Sketch width at 4× capacity is slightly collision-heavy (Cycle 0007 v003 confirmed a hit-rate gain at 8× width), but u16 counters at 8× width do not recover the per-access cost. A u16 + 8× compound at the same 64 KB heap is plausible but has not produced a win.
- **LRU segment split (protected/probationary):** Newly confirmed as a live axis in Cycle 0008. Re-positioning hot-chain hits within the protected segment (rather than relinking through probationary) reduces get-path link operations and removes proven-hot nodes from the victim pool. Struct-size growth is the main risk; compact flag representation is the next hypothesis.
- **Eviction candidate selection:** Best-of-2 victim comparison is closed — the LRU cold end presents candidates of equivalent frequency. All hit-rate leverage on the eviction side now resides in the admission gate comparison and the protected-segment filter, not in multi-candidate sampling.
