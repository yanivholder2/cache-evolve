## Recurring wins

- **Amortised-rate sampling (1-in-8) makes expensive per-eviction operations latency-neutral.** Both the best-of-2 eviction probe (cycle 4) and the strict admission gate (cycle 6) won only at the 1-in-8 fire rate; 1-in-4 broke latency in cycle 5. The pattern is reliable: low-frequency gating preserves hot-path timing while still accumulating a real, stable signal.
- **Eliminating serial hash dependencies on the put path is a durable gain.** Single-hash bit-slicing (cycle 3) and the bijective multiply-xorshift remix (cycle 5) both won by removing intra-iteration data dependencies rather than simply reducing hash count. The compiler's ILP-friendly codegen for independent operations outperforms hand-chained serial arithmetic every time this trade-off has been tested.
- **Idiomatic Rust abstractions beat hand-rolled arithmetic optimisations.** `Option<NonZeroU32>` with standard match arms outperformed explicit sentinel arithmetic (cycle 2). Standard container abstractions preserved ILP that explicit arithmetic collapsed. Prefer idiomatic code; profile before optimising.
- **Sketch width is a genuine hit-rate lever.** Doubling the width factor (4× → 8×) reduced collision noise enough to lift hit rate measurably on the Zipfian trace (cycle 7 v003), confirming that collision suppression — not depth — is the binding accuracy constraint at current capacity.

## Recurring failures

- **Sketch decay at any tested interval degrades performance on stable working sets.** Both an aggressive interval (`width/2`, cycle 1) and a moderate interval (`capacity × 10`, cycle 3) hurt both hit rate and latency. The LIRS trace's long-lived hot set makes accumulated frequency a reliable admission signal; periodic half-decay destroys that signal faster than accesses can rebuild it. This axis is definitively closed.
- **Flat/unified sketch allocation cannot substitute for per-row heap segments.** A single contiguous allocation introduced cache-set conflicts that more than doubled latency (cycle 2), showing that the baseline's pointer-indirection cost is lower than the stride-induced conflict pressure in a flat layout.
- **Serial bijective remix chains do not substitute for independent hash seeds.** A multi-step multiply-rotate-XOR chain rooted at a single seed produces correlated row positions (raising collision noise) and stalls out-of-order execution more than a second independent SipHash call (cycle 6 v003). Hash independence requires genuine seed diversity, not chain length.
- **Aggressive eviction rates and strict admission gates both cause residency churn.** Firing best-of-2 probes at 1-in-4 (cycle 5) or applying a strict frequency comparison on every eviction (cycle 5 v003, cycle 6 v001) reshuffles which nodes occupy cache lines; the latency cost from residency churn dominates any hit-rate gain. Adding extra sketch probes on an already-amortised path also re-introduces this cost (cycle 6 v001 even at 1-in-8 rate).
- **Best-of-2 LRU-end victim selection offers no discrimination.** The LRU head and its immediate neighbour are both equally cold relative to the working set; the admission gate comparison against the incoming key is the operative filter (cycle 7 v001). This axis is closed.
- **u32 → u16 counter without width compensation regresses p99.** Per-access zero-extension overhead outweighs the cache-footprint reduction at current sketch sizes (cycle 7 v002). Counter narrowing is only sensible when width doubles to keep total heap footprint constant.
- **Node compaction beyond NonZeroU32 yields nothing.** Compact layout improved memory density but degraded latency until idiomatic NonZeroU32 match arms recovered parity with baseline; further layout changes show no measurable benefit (exhausted by cycle 2).

## Open questions

- **Does a u16 + 8× width sketch (same 64 KB total heap as the 4× u32 baseline) combine v003's hit-rate gain with v002's footprint without v002's per-access widening cost?** This is the highest-priority untested compound; neither cycle 7 variant tested it.
- **Can the amortised strict admission gate (cycle 6 winner) be combined with best-of-2 victim selection without growing the struct?** If the eviction counter fits into existing alignment padding, the cache-line boundary shift that amplified cycle 6 v001's probe overhead may disappear.
- **Are structural policy parameters (window ratio, protected/probationary segment balance, promotion threshold) a genuinely independent axis?** No cycle has touched these levers; they may interact with sketch accuracy and should be explored only after the sketch configuration stabilises.
- **Is there a softer admission-gate variant — e.g., applied only on the amortised-probe path rather than every eviction — that limits residency churn while still capturing some hit-rate benefit?**

## Per-policy notes

- **CountMinSketch configuration:** Four-row depth is load-bearing on this Zipfian trace; removing rows cascades into eviction errors (cycle 2). Eight-row expansion lifts hit rate but doubles p99 (cycle 7) — the u16 compound is the next test. Bit-slicing a single hash into four 16-bit windows weakens tail accuracy relative to four independent hashes (cycle 3 p99 signal). Two genuinely independent seeds preserve accuracy; remix chains do not.
- **TinyLFU admission gate:** Strict threshold (`incoming > victim + 1`) on every eviction causes residency churn regardless of threshold value (cycle 5). The 1-in-8 amortised gate without extra probes is the current best baseline (cycle 6 winner). Any variant that adds sketch probes on the amortised path re-introduces the latency penalty even at the low fire rate.
- **Eviction victim selection:** The amortised 1-in-8 best-of-2 sketch probe improved hit rate stably (cycle 4 winner). Higher rates (1-in-4) break latency (cycle 5). Cold-end LRU victim discrimination is zero — the two candidates are indistinguishable on frequency (cycle 7 confirmed closed).
- **Node encoding:** `Option<NonZeroU32>` with idiomatic match arms is the settled encoding (cycle 2). The axis is exhausted; do not revisit.
- **Hash computation:** Bijective multiply-xorshift remix of a single SipHash output is the current baseline (cycle 5 winner). Two independent seeded hashes preserve row independence better but add one full hash invocation; the cost concentrates at p99, not p50.
