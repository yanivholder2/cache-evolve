# H02

Apply occupancy-responsive boost scaling at ghost-hit re-admission time to adapt the re-admission signal without hot-path overhead.

## Parent

cycle/0005/v002

## Changes

Enhance the ghost-hit re-admission logic (lines 120-128 in cycle 0005 v002) to compute occupancy ratio at ghost-hit time and scale the re-admission frequency boost accordingly. Currently, ghost hits promote directly to main queue with `freq=0`. Instead, compute `occupancy_ratio = self.main.len() as f64 / self.main_size as f64` and set the entry's frequency to `boost = (2.0 + occupancy_ratio * occupancy_ratio).min(MAX_FREQ as f64) as u8`, where the quadratic scaling favors higher boost under high occupancy (e.g., at 0.9 occupancy, boost ≈ 2.81; at 0.3 occupancy, boost ≈ 2.09). This defers the expensive occupancy computation to ghost-hit time (a cold path, infrequent), avoiding the per-operation overhead that plagued structural occupancy-aware mechanics in cycles 0004–0008. The signal adapts to queue state without adding hot-path branching.

## Rationale

Cycles 0006–0008 demonstrated that occupancy-responsive mechanisms unlock hit-rate improvements but incur per-operation overhead when computed on hot paths (puts, evictions). However, ghost-hit re-admission is a cold path (infrequent relative to puts), making it an ideal location for signal adaptation. Cycle 0008/v002 tested multi-signal re-admission but suffered from suboptimal weighting; this hypothesis applies occupancy scaling to single-signal re-admission (frequency-only, like v002's baseline) to recover the adaptive benefit without the formula complexity or per-operation cost. This exploits the architectural insight that "eviction-time computation is zero-cost" by moving occupancy computation to an already-infrequent decision point.
