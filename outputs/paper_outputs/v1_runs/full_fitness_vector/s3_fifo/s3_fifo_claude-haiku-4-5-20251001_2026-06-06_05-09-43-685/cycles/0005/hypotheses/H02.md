# H02

Reduce per-operation overhead in occupancy-aware queue sizing by scaling down adjustment magnitude.

## Parent

cycle/0004/v003

## Changes

Scale down the occupancy-responsive queue-sizing adjustment to isolate whether latency overhead is proportional to adjustment magnitude. In `src/s3_fifo/mod.rs`:

1. Replace the sizing bounds:
   - Current (v003): `small_size_min = 10%`, `small_size_max = 25%`
   - New: `small_size_min = 15%`, `small_size_max = 20%` (tighter bounds reducing adjustment range from 15% to 5%)

2. Reduce the per-operation adjustment delta:
   - Current (v003): adjust by 1 unit when crossing occupancy thresholds (0.5, 0.75)
   - New: adjust by 1 unit only when occupancy crosses 0.75 boundary, skip the 0.5 adjustment to reduce frequency of resizing

3. Keep the core sizing logic intact:
   - Check occupancy only on `put()` before evict_small()
   - Shrink when occupancy >= 0.75 to `small_size_min`
   - Grow when occupancy <= 0.5 to `small_size_max`

This tests whether the latency cost is proportional to adjustment magnitude, allowing hit-rate recovery without the overhead penalty that v003 incurred.

## Rationale

Cycle 0004/v003 showed occupancy-aware sizing boosts hit-rate (+1.2%) but degrades latency (get p50 +36ns, put p99 +86ns) due to per-operation occupancy tracking and resizing. The magnitude of adjustment (±15%) and frequency of re-sizing may be over-tuned. By reducing adjustment range to ±5% and re-sizing less frequently, we test the hypothesis that overhead is proportional to adjustment magnitude. Expected outcome: retain most of v003's hit-rate gains while reducing the latency penalty closer to v003's baseline parent.
