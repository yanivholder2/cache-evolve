# H01

Loosen ghost-queue frequency-decay retention to recover hit-rate from cycle 0004/v001's over-aggressive filtering.

## Parent

cycle/0003/v003

## Changes

Modify the frequency-decay logic in the ghost queue to preserve more candidates, addressing v001's over-aggressive filtering. In `src/s3_fifo/mod.rs`:

1. Change the ghost-queue type to `VecDeque<(K, i8)>` to support frequency values below 0, allowing tracking of decayed items that may still have re-access potential.

2. In `evict_main()`, when evicting items from main to ghost:
   - Decrement frequency: `ghost_freq = tail.freq.saturating_sub(1)` (no change)
   - But change the retention logic: instead of discarding when `ghost_freq <= 0`, retain entries with `ghost_freq >= -1` (allowing one level of further decay before discard)
   - This gives items one additional opportunity to be re-accessed before expiring from the ghost queue

3. In `put()`, when a ghost entry is re-accessed:
   - Preserve the ghost frequency as-is (including negative values)
   - Re-admit with preserved frequency + 2 (to quickly recover from the decayed state) instead of + 1
   - Clamp to range [0, MAX_FREQ]

4. Keep v003's occupancy-responsive thresholds intact (freq > 1/2/3 based on occupancy).

## Rationale

Cycle 0004/v001 demonstrated that frequency decay in the ghost queue can reduce overhead and improve latency, but the retention threshold (freq > 0 after decrement) was too strict. Items were ejected before accumulating sufficient re-access probability, destroying hit-rate. By loosening the retention threshold to allow decay down to -1 before final discard, and boosting re-admission weight (freq + 2 instead of + 1), we preserve more candidates without the per-operation overhead of dynamic structural sizing. This targets hit-rate recovery while maintaining v001's latency efficiency.
