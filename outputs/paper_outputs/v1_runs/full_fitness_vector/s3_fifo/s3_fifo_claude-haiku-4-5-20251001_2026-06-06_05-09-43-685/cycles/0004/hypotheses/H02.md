# H02

Use frequency distribution percentiles to set dynamic promotion thresholds, aligning retention to actual workload hotness patterns rather than queue occupancy alone.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

In `src/s3_fifo/mod.rs`, replace occupancy-based threshold logic with frequency-distribution-based thresholds:

1. Add fields to the `S3Fifo` struct to track frequency distribution:
   - `freq_samples: VecDeque<u8>` to maintain a recent moving window of frequencies observed during eviction (capacity = 100)
   - `threshold_from_distribution: u8` initialized to 2

2. In `evict_small()`, before making the promotion decision:
   - Compute the 75th percentile of frequencies in `freq_samples` (this represents the threshold for keeping top-quartile hottest items)
   - Update `threshold_from_distribution` to this percentile
   - Use `threshold_from_distribution` as the promotion threshold instead of occupancy-based levels

3. In `evict_small()` and `evict_main()`, whenever an entry's frequency is sampled or decremented:
   - Add that frequency to the `freq_samples` queue
   - If `freq_samples` exceeds capacity, remove oldest samples to maintain the moving window

4. Initialize `threshold_from_distribution` to 2 and only use frequency-percentile logic once 50+ samples have been collected; before that, fall back to a simple fixed threshold of 2.

This approach makes promotion thresholds responsive to actual workload access patterns (similar to v003's state-responsiveness) but using the frequency distribution signal instead of queue occupancy. Items at or above the 75th percentile frequency will be promoted, ensuring the hottest 25% of items by frequency stay in main queue regardless of occupancy state.

## Rationale

v003 proved that state-responsive thresholds unlock better performance than fixed gates. However, it uses occupancy (queue fullness) as the signal. This hypothesis explores frequency distribution as an alternative signal: workloads may have varying hotness distributions (e.g., some Zipfian with clear top-1%, others more uniform), and aligning the threshold to the actual frequency distribution of items flowing through the cache should be more robust. Expected outcome: hit-rate improvement by focusing retention on empirically hot items, with comparable or better per-op-time by reducing unnecessary main-queue evictions of warm items.
