# H01

Extend v003's occupancy-responsive thresholds with ghost-queue frequency tracking to preserve hotness signals and improve hit-rate without structural overhead.

## Parent

cycle/0003/v003

## Changes

In `src/s3_fifo/mod.rs`, enhance ghost-queue tracking to preserve frequency information while keeping v003's occupancy-responsive thresholds:

1. Modify the `ghost` field type from `VecDeque<K>` to `VecDeque<(K, u8)>` to track frequency alongside keys.

2. In `put()`, when checking for ghost hits:
   - Extract both the key and its preserved frequency from the ghost entry
   - Remove the entry from ghost queue
   - When re-admitting to main queue, initialize the entry with the preserved frequency (min of preserved freq + 1, MAX_FREQ) instead of always starting at frequency 0
   - This allows hot items to retain their hotness signal across eviction boundaries

3. In `evict_main()`, when evicting ghost entries (the `self.ghost.pop_back()` and `push_front()` lines):
   - Before pushing to ghost, decrement the frequency of the evicted tail entry
   - This implements frequency decay in the ghost queue, allowing warm items to age out gracefully rather than lose frequency abruptly
   - Only push to ghost if frequency remains > 0 after decrement; otherwise discard entirely to bound ghost-queue size

4. Keep v003's occupancy-responsive threshold logic intact (freq > 1 at low occupancy, freq > 2 at medium, freq > 3 at high occupancy).

This combines v003's occupancy-awareness (which successfully decoupled latency from hit-rate) with frequency preservation in the ghost critical path (addressing v001's failure where ghost-queue separation lost frequency information due to overflow).

## Rationale

Cycle 0003's v001 failed because structural ghost-queue separation lost frequency information through overflow before items could be re-accessed. v003 succeeded by embedding occupancy awareness directly in the eviction path. This hypothesis unifies both insights: preserve frequency alongside occupancy by keeping frequency in the ghost queue itself (embedded, not structural), with decay logic that prevents overflow while retaining hotness information. The expected outcome is recovery of hit-rate gains from better frequency preservation, while maintaining v003's occupancy-responsive latency protection.
