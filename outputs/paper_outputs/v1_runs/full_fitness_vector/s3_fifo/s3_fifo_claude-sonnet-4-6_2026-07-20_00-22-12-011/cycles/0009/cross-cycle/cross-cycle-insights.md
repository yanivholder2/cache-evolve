## Recurring wins

- **O(n) → O(1) substitutions were the highest-leverage moves**: Every cycle that eliminated a linear scan — ghost-set membership (cycle 1), `ghost.retain()` → lazy tombstoning (cycle 2), `position()` scan → `resident_map` hash lookup (cycles 4–5) — delivered large, durable gains. Algorithmic complexity reduction has been the single most reliable predictor of improvement.
- **Lazy/amortized cleanup compounds well**: Deferring ghost-deque cleanup via tombstoning (cycle 2) was a pure win with no observed downside at current workload scales; amortizing per-hit work across future eviction capacity guards is a stable pattern.
- **Structural consolidation beats decomposition**: The unified `resident_map<K,(V,u8)>` (cycle 5) outperformed every split-map and multi-structure alternative. Combining value + frequency into a single hash probe eliminated redundant lookups and shed metadata; attempts to decompose it in either direction have all regressed (cycle 6, cycle 9).
- **Orthogonal changes compose cleanly**: The three cycle-1 wins (queue sizing, O(1) ghost check, freq-deque separation) stacked without interaction penalty when merged in cycle 2. Compositional safety held throughout the early improvement phase.
- **Small-queue fraction reduction paid off monotonically through 1/20**: Halving from 1/4 → 1/10 → 1/20 improved both hit-rate and latency at every step on Zipfian workloads, with no downside until the fraction was pushed below that floor.

## Recurring failures

- **Shrinking the small queue below 1/20**: At 1/40 the latency crossover first appeared (cycle 4, driven by `position()` overhead); at 1/80 the crossover recurred independently of that scan (cycle 6) due to `evict_small()` invocation rate. Floor is confirmed at 1/20; do not revisit smaller fractions.
- **Bounded second-chance scan cap in `evict_main()`**: Falsified in cycle 4 — on Zipfian workloads cold entries naturally accumulate at the deque tail, so the unbounded scan already terminates in the first few iterations. A hard cap only forces eviction of still-warm entries beyond the budget without reducing churn.
- **Ghost promotion threshold below `freq > 1`**: Promoting at `freq > 0` or initialising ghost-hit entries at `freq=1` was harmful in every test (cycles 3, 7, 8). The `freq > 1` gate functions as a demand-confirmation filter, not bookkeeping; relaxing it floods main with once-warm cold-tail entries.
- **Eviction batching**: Concentrating multiple `evict_small()` calls into one `put()` made both p50 and p99 worse (cycle 8). Eviction overhead is super-linear in burst depth; one-at-a-time is the correct policy.
- **Frequency step acceleration and `MAX_FREQ` widening**: Step=2 decrement increased total eviction work rather than reducing it (cycle 4). `MAX_FREQ=5` deepened `evict_main()` loops without proportional hit-rate gain (cycle 7). Both levers are near-exhausted on Zipfian at the current baseline.
- **Ghost capacity beyond `main_size`**: Doubling the ghost window accumulated useless tombstones from the Zipfian cold tail, hurting put latency via the lazy-deletion drain loop (cycle 6). `main_size` is the effectively tight bound.
- **Get-path micro-optimisations on the hot set**: Probe reduction, hasher substitution (SipHash → FNV-1a), and write-skip guards for saturated entries were all invisible to get p50 (cycle 9). The Zipfian hot set's L1 residency means these cache-miss-reduction approaches find no misses to eliminate; they are structurally unreachable on this workload at this scale.

## Open questions

- **Is get p50 movable at all on this workload?** Four consecutive falsification cycles (cycles 6–9) have found no lever that shifts the primary fitness signal. The L1-resident hot set may mean that any remaining get-path headroom lies in the cold or medium-frequency tiers — which are currently invisible to the benchmark — rather than in implementation details.
- **What does a workload challenge reveal?** All gains and falsifications are specific to Zipfian hot-set access. Testing with a flatter distribution, a larger working set that spills out of L1/L2, or a mixed read/write ratio may expose bottlenecks that are currently hidden.
- **Is the ghost-set `HashSet` probe on every `put()` measurable?** It is the one per-operation cost that has not been directly targeted. At high put rates with a large ghost set, collision depth in the ghost HashSet could be the remaining movable lever on the put path.
- **Does admission selectivity reduce total eviction work?** Batching was ruled out (cycle 8), but structural selectivity — e.g., probabilistic admission gates that reduce the number of entries reaching small in the first place — has not been tested and avoids both the invocation-rate penalty and the burst-cost penalty.
- **Is the Winners anomaly signal-distorting?** Net-negative variants have appeared in the Winners list since cycle 3 and in every cycle from 6 onward. If the fitness function admits regressions on primary axes, the selection pressure may not meaningfully guide the search; clarifying the weighting would help interpret future cohort outcomes.

## Per-policy notes

- **S3-FIFO small queue**: Optimal fraction is 1/20 on Zipfian. The improvement was monotone 1/4 → 1/10 → 1/20; crossover appears independently at 1/40 and 1/80 for different reasons (scan overhead vs. invocation rate). Hold at 1/20 indefinitely.
- **S3-FIFO ghost channel**: `freq > 1` promotion is load-bearing and must not be relaxed. Ghost capacity at `main_size` is tight and optimal — neither halving nor doubling it helped. Lazy tombstoning (HashSet removal on hit; deque drain on capacity guard) is stable and correct.
- **S3-FIFO `evict_main()`**: The second-chance scan is effectively short on Zipfian because cold entries accumulate at the deque tail. Unconditional freq writes on every access keep eviction-path cache lines warm — skipping writes for saturated entries backfired in cycle 9. Deque memory-access churn (random vs. sequential) was confirmed to matter (cycle 3) but pure O(1) random selection sacrificed hit-rate protection unacceptably.
- **S3-FIFO `evict_small()`**: Call-rate (not per-call cost) is the bottleneck when the small queue is very small. Hash load-factor pre-sizing had zero effect (cycle 7), ruling out probe-chain depth. Batching is super-linear and harmful (cycle 8). The path forward, if any, is admission-side reduction rather than eviction-side amortization.
- **S3-FIFO data layout**: `resident_map<K,(V,u8)>` is the confirmed optimal structure. Splitting into separate value and frequency maps adds write overhead (cycle 6); merging further or swapping hashers adds no benefit (cycle 9). This layout is stable and should not be revisited.
