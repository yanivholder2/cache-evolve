# H02

Push bucket allocation to 90/10 geometry to find the true asymptote of capacity concentration.

## Parent

cycle/0003/v003

## Changes

In `src/shared/geometry.rs`, modify the `BaselineGeometry::for_settings()` function to allocate buckets even more aggressively toward the normal tier:

- Change the normal bucket allocation to `num_of_normal_buckets = (total_buckets * 9) / 10` (9/10 = 90%)
- Change stash allocation to `num_of_stash_buckets = total_buckets - num_of_normal_buckets`
- Clamp `num_of_normal_buckets` to at least 2 (required for probing) using `.max(2)` to ensure structural validity on small bucket counts

For baseline's total of 32 buckets, this yields 29 normal + 3 stash (90%/10%), pushing further from cycle 0003's v003 (75/25), cycle 0004's v002 (85/15), and baseline's conservative 25/75.

Keep `bucket_size` and `segment_size` unchanged. The total capacity remains constant; capacity is reallocated from the stash to the hash-indexed normal tier.

## Rationale

The progression from baseline (25/75) through 50/50 (cycle 0001), 62.5/37.5 (cycle 0002), 75/25 (cycle 0003), to 85/15 (cycle 0004) shows sustained improvement across both hit-rate and latency with no saturation observed. Cycle 0004's hypothesis that 85/15 might be approaching asymptote was disproven—the geometry asymptote remains unblocked with further gains available. Pushing to 90/10 tests whether the unbroken trend continues or if diminishing returns finally emerge. Pure-geometry variants have consistently outperformed complex compositions across all prior cycles, making this the most reliable approach to identify the true saturation point. On Zipfian workloads where a small hot-set dominates, reducing the stash from 5 buckets (in 85/15) to 3 buckets (in 90/10) further concentrates capacity where hash-indexed lookups are fastest, trading minimally impactful demotions for reliability in the primary tier.
