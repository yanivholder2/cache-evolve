# H02

Pure geometry allocation at 80/20 identifies the optimal hit-rate/latency trade-off frontier within the saturation zone.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

In `src/shared/geometry.rs`, modify the `BaselineGeometry::for_settings()` function to allocate buckets at the 80/20 point, midway between the proven-optimal 75/25 and the saturating 90/10:

- Change the normal bucket allocation to `num_of_normal_buckets = (total_buckets * 4) / 5` (80%)
- Change stash allocation to `num_of_stash_buckets = total_buckets - num_of_normal_buckets`
- Clamp `num_of_normal_buckets` to at least 2 using `.max(2)` for small bucket counts
- For baseline's total of 32 buckets, this yields 26 normal + 6 stash (80/20)

Keep `bucket_size`, `segment_size`, and all promotion logic unchanged. No frequency gating or other modifications.

## Rationale

Cycle 0005 definitively identified saturation at 90/10: v002's aggressive 90/10 geometry improves latency (105 ns, matching v003's best-latency performance) but regresses hit-rate to 0.7099, below the prior incumbents. This establishes a saturation boundary: the progression 75/25 (good) → 85/15 (good) → 90/10 (saturated) reveals that capacity constraints become binding somewhere in the 85/15–90/10 range. The open question asks whether an optimal geometry exists between 75/25 and 90/10. Testing 80/20 (midway between 75/25 and 85/15, approaching the saturation zone) provides a direct measurement of the hit-rate/latency frontier within this critical region. On pure-geometry variants, which have consistently outperformed complex compositions, this test will reveal whether the optimal allocation point is closer to the conservative 75/25 or the aggressive 90/10 extreme.
