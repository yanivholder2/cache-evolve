# H01

Replace the incumbent's position-based LRU tiebreak with a live last-access timestamp and drop the per-hit vector reorder, aiming to hold or lift hit-rate while cutting per-op-time.

## Parent

cycle/0001/v002

## Changes

Modify the bucket overrides in `src/associative_cache/associative_cache_bucket.rs` that implement v002's frequency-first rule.

Today v002, on every hit (`get_from_position`), increments `lfu_counter` **and** does a ClassicLRU move-to-back (`items.remove(position)` + `items.push(item)`); its `eviction_candidate_index` picks the global minimum `lfu_counter`, breaking ties by lowest vector index (i.e. by recency/position). The move-to-back is an O(bucket_size) shuffle on every hit, and position-tiebreak is only a coarse proxy for recency.

Make two coupled changes:

1. In `get_from_position`, on a hit: increment `lfu_counter` and set `items[position].timestamp = Instant::now()`, then return `&self.items[position]` **without** removing/re-pushing. Items keep their slots (no reorder).
2. In `eviction_candidate_index`, select the victim by `min_by_key` over the composite key `(item.lfu_counter, item.timestamp)` — i.e. fewest hits first, and among equal-frequency items the one with the **oldest last-access timestamp** (true recency-aware LFU, no decay).

Preserve all invariants: capacity (`max_size`) and resident set are untouched, new keys still insert with `lfu_counter == 0`, "once-hot immortality" is preserved (counters are never aged or capped), and the `EvictionPolicyKind` enum / JNI ordinal mapping stay pristine. The hypothesis is that true last-access recency is a better equal-frequency tiebreak than vector position, and that removing the per-hit reorder lowers per-op-time at no hit-rate cost.

## Rationale (optional)

v002 is the global best on a collision-bound trace; its only soft spots are the coarse position-tiebreak and an unmeasured O(n) per-hit shuffle. This isolates both without disturbing the frequency signal that drives the win.
