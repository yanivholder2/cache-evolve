# H03

Use random sampling of victim candidates instead of full-cache linear scan to improve hit-rate by discovering locally-stable eviction equilibria without the bias of deterministic scan order.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

In the victim-selection loop within the `put()` method (where capacity is full), replace the full-cache scan with random sampling:

1. Instead of iterating through all cached items starting from head, randomly sample K candidate items, where `K = min((capacity / 10).max(5), 30)`. Use a simple random index selection within the nodes vector to draw the sample.
2. For each sampled item, compute the eviction score `(age as f64) / (frequency + 2.0)` (same formula as baseline).
3. Select and evict the sampled item with the highest score.
4. Keep the admission gate unchanged: `incoming_freq > victim_freq`.
5. Sketch parameters, counter saturation, and all other logic remain identical to baseline.

## Rationale

The incumbent policy scans all cached items linearly from head (oldest) to tail, which may bias eviction toward items at the head of the LRU list regardless of their frequency-age score. This hypothesis tests whether randomizing the victim candidate pool can discover eviction equilibria that the deterministic scan misses. Randomization may reduce order-dependent biases and allow the frequency-age formula to operate on a less-constrained candidate distribution, potentially unlocking different hit-rate characteristics. This is a fundamentally different algorithm (sampling-based, O(K) instead of O(N)), not a formula tweak or signal addition, and explores whether the incumbent's optimality is dependent on the deterministic head-to-tail scan order or intrinsic to the scoring formula itself.
