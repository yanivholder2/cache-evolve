# H02

Differentiating cold stash admissions from warm demotions by stash insert position will raise hit-rate by evicting one-hit-wonders before the working-set tail that has been demoted back into the stash.

## Parent

cycle/0004/v001

## Changes

Today both paths land at the stash MRU end: a brand-new cold key enters
via the default `Segment::put` (`Slot::Stash`, `InsertPosition::Back`)
and a warm key demoted down the cascade also lands at the stash via
`InsertPosition::Back` (v001's hit-path `insert_pos`). The two
populations are therefore indistinguishable, so when a stash bucket
overflows the recency policy cannot tell a transient cold key from a
genuine working-set key that was just demoted.

Make cold admissions the preferred eviction victims while protecting
warm demotions:

- In `src/dash/segments/dash_segment.rs`, override `put` in the
  `impl Segment<K, V> for DashSegment` block (instead of inheriting the
  trait default). Route the cold insert to `Slot::Stash` at
  `InsertPosition::Front` (the LRU end for recency policies), preserving
  the existing `debug_assert!(!core.contains_key(...))` uniqueness guard
  and the debug logging.
- Leave the demotion-to-stash inserts in `demote_from_normal_bucket`
  exactly as v001 has them (`insert_pos == Back`, i.e. MRU), so a
  demoted warm key outranks freshly admitted cold keys in the same stash
  bucket.

Net effect: under ClassicLRU-style stash eviction (candidate = front /
LRU), a cold one-hit-wonder sits at the front and is the first to be
evicted on the next collision, whereas a warm demoted key sits at the
back and survives — protecting the oscillating LIRS working-set tail
without removing or gutting the load-bearing admission step (which cycle
0004 v002 proved must not be removed). Do not change the promotion gate,
the cascade routing, or the geometry. Keep slot-legality and
capacity-conservation invariants intact; add/adjust a unit test asserting
a cold `put` lands at stash index 0 while a demoted key lands at the back.

## Rationale (optional)

The stash is confirmed protective but currently blends cold and warm
arrivals. Position differentiation is a budget-conserving way to bias its
eviction toward true transients, a stash-dynamics lever untouched so far.
