# H02

Skew normal bucket allocation toward probing tier (18 probing, 10 target) instead of equal distribution (14 target, 14 probing) to provide more capacity for hot-tier residents and reduce eviction cascades, targeting hit rate improvement.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Modify `src/shared/geometry.rs` in the `BaselineGeometry::for_settings()` method to compute asymmetric normal bucket distribution.

**Specific implementation:**
1. In `BaselineGeometry::for_settings()`, change the fixed split from `(target=14, probing=14)` to `(target=10, probing=18)`
2. Ensure the total remains 28 normal buckets (10 + 18 = 28)
3. Adjust bucket index calculations in `src/dash/segments/segment.rs` or the bucketing logic to respect this split: buckets 0–9 are target, buckets 10–27 are probing
4. Or alternatively, use a hash-based assignment where target gets `index % 28 < 10` and probing gets `index % 28 >= 10`
5. All other parameters remain unchanged (4 stash buckets, 16 slots/bucket)

## Rationale

The current equal distribution (14 normal buckets each for target and probing) may underallocate capacity to the probing tier, where items are expected to be hottest and have the longest residency. Hot items need more space to avoid eviction cascades that demote them back to target/stash. By skewing 18 probing buckets vs. 10 target, we expand hot-tier capacity from 224 slots (14 × 16) to 288 slots (18 × 16), while target shrinks from 224 to 160 slots. This tests whether the 0.684 ceiling is constrained by probing-tier capacity saturation, causing hot items to be evicted too frequently. If hit-rate improves, it confirms that current hot-tier overflow is a bottleneck; if unchanged, it suggests the constraint lies elsewhere (e.g., stash filtering adequacy, target-tier selection discipline).
