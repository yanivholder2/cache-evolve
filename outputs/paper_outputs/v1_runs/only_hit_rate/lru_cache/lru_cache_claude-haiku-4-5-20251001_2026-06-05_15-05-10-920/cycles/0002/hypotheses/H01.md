# H01

Improve frequency-based eviction hit rate by using recency-based tie-breaking to reduce variance in victim selection on access-skewed workloads.

## Parent

cycle/0001/v001

## Changes

In the `put()` method's eviction logic (lines ~79-90), refine the victim selection algorithm to use recency as a secondary factor when breaking frequency ties. Currently, the code scans nodes from head to tail and selects the first node with minimum frequency, biasing toward the head (LRU end).

Modify this to: (1) first pass over all nodes to compute the minimum frequency value, then (2) second pass scanning from tail toward head to find the tail-most node with that minimum frequency. This ensures that among items with equal frequency, the most recently used item is selected as the victim, reducing the chance of immediately re-evicting items that were just accessed but happen to have low frequency due to early arrival.

Preserve all other invariants: the doubly-linked list structure, capacity bounds, frequency counter increments, and MRU promotion logic on get/put remain unchanged.

## Rationale

The margin between v001 (0.7255) and v002 (0.7204) suggests that tie-breaking variance may play a role. By preferring to evict older items (tail-toward-head scan) among low-frequency nodes, this variant prioritizes temporal locality alongside frequency, potentially reducing thrashing and improving cache efficiency on mixed-access patterns.
