# H02

Full 8-bit frequency precision without 3-bit bucketing will improve hit-rate above 0.7334 by preserving finer discrimination between moderately-hot items.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Replace the 3-bit quantized frequency bucket tracking from the incumbent with full 8-bit natural frequency accumulation (0-255 range). Remove the `quantize_frequency()` function and instead increment frequency on each access using standard saturating addition: `freq = freq.saturating_add(1)`. During periodic decay (at the same capacity × 16 operation threshold), apply multiplicative decay to the full range: `freq = (freq as u16 * 3 / 4) as u8` to preserve the 0.75× decay coefficient. Maintain two-pass victim selection: find minimum frequency across all items, then select the head-most (oldest) item with that minimum frequency. The hypothesis is that the incumbent's quantization loses discriminative power by collapsing multiple access counts into the same bucket; unquantized precision allows the cache to finely separate items with 4-7 accesses from those with 8-15 accesses, improving eviction decisions.

## Rationale

Cycle 0009 v002 introduced 3-bit bucketing (7 tiers) and won, but cycle 0009's insight states that "decay compresses frequencies into such a narrow operational range." This compression may be an artifact of bucketing, not a fundamental property. By using full 8-bit resolution, frequencies will remain spread across a wider range even after decay, potentially preserving finer gradient information that the cache can use to discriminate between items at similar access tiers.
