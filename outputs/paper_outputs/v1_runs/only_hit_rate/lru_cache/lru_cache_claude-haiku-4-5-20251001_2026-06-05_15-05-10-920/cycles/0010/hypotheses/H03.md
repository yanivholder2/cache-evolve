## Changes

Replace the incumbent's smooth multiplicative decay (0.75×) with threshold-based amnesia: during periodic decay at capacity × 16 operations, reset all frequency buckets below a threshold (freq_bucket < 2) to 0 rather than applying multiplicative decay to the entire distribution. Items with frequency ≥ 2 retain their exact frequencies unchanged. This creates a sharp boundary between items that have demonstrated sustained reuse (freq ≥ 2) and ephemeral accesses (freq < 2 reset to 0). Keep the 3-bit quantized bucket representation from the incumbent and maintain two-pass victim selection on the resulting frequencies. The hypothesis is that threshold-based amnesia creates a clearer separation between working-set items and one-time accesses than smooth multiplicative decay, allowing the cache to better protect the core reuse pattern while aggressively evicting ephemeral items.

## Rationale

The incumbent's smooth multiplicative decay (0.75×) gradually reduces all frequencies proportionally, which may create ambiguity in the decision boundary for borderline items. Cross-cycle analysis shows adaptive mechanisms fail, but this is not adaptation—it is a different decay *pattern*, not a different decay *trigger*. Threshold-based forgetting tests whether a sharper, discontinuous forgetting function better captures the workload's access structure (hot vs. cold) compared to the continuous decay curve. This complements H02's precision-focused approach with an orthogonal signal-shaping strategy.
