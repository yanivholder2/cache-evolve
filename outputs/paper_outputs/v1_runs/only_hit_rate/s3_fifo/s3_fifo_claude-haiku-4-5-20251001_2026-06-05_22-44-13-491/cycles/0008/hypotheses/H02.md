# H02

Extend frequency protection from MAX_FREQ=5 to MAX_FREQ=6 while maintaining the proven 1/12 structural selectivity to test whether deeper frequency-based item retention extracts additional hit-rate gains from the constrained admission gate.

## Parent

cycle/0006/v003

## Changes

Modify the `try_new()` method in `/src/s3_fifo/mod.rs` to set the small-queue capacity at 1/12. Change the line containing `(capacity / 4).max(1).min(capacity - 1)` to `(capacity / 12).max(1).min(capacity - 1)` when computing `small_size`. Simultaneously modify the frequency constant at the top of the file to change `const MAX_FREQ: u8 = 3;` to `const MAX_FREQ: u8 = 6;`. This combines cycle/0006/v003's proven structural selectivity (1/12) with one additional frequency level beyond cycle/0007/v002's MAX_FREQ=5, allowing items to survive one more eviction round. All other logic unchanged: frequency increment on access, freq > 1 threshold for promotion, main-queue eviction identical. This tests whether extending frequency depth to MAX_FREQ=6 outperforms v002's MAX_FREQ=5 at 1/12 selectivity.

## Rationale

Cycle 0007 v002 demonstrated that MAX_FREQ=5 compounds with 1/12 structural selectivity to win the cycle. However, the marginal gain from adding MAX_FREQ=5 to 1/12 was modest (≈0.0012 from v003's 0.7258 baseline to v002's 0.727). The saturation boundary now lies in the structure-frequency interaction space. This hypothesis tests whether frequency protection remains a viable lever at extreme selectivity by pushing the frequency dimension further to MAX_FREQ=6, exploring whether the frequency-depth axis still has unlocated gains at the saturation boundary.
