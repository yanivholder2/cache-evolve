# H01

Re-encode the SLRU node's recency links as `u32` NIL sentinels so the winning hit-rate/latency base also shrinks metadata-size from 56 toward 32 bytes per node.

## Parent

cycle/0002/v002

## Changes

Take v002's SLRU implementation (`src/lru_cache/mod.rs`) and narrow the
per-node links exactly as cycle/0002/v001 did for plain LRU, but applied
to the segmented node. Concretely:

- Change `Node`'s `prev: Option<usize>` and `next: Option<usize>` to
  `prev: u32` and `next: u32`, with a `const NIL: u32 = u32::MAX`
  sentinel for "no link". Keep `key`, `value`, and the `protected: bool`
  fields as-is. This drops `Node<u64,u64>` from 56 bytes to ~32 (two
  16-byte non-niche `Option`s become two 4-byte words; the `bool` plus
  padding remains).
- Also narrow the four segment endpoints on `LruCache`
  (`prot_head/prot_tail/prob_head/prob_tail`) from `Option<usize>` to
  `u32`, initialised to `NIL` in `try_new`.
- Centralise the conversions in two `#[inline]` helpers — `idx(link:
  u32) -> Option<usize>` (zero-extend, mapping `NIL` to `None`) and
  `link(index: usize) -> u32` — and follow v001's discipline: read a
  link once into a local at the top of each routine, write it back once
  per structural mutation, so `unlink`/`push_back`/`demote_protected_lru`
  and the `set_head`/`set_tail` helpers stay cast-free in their bodies.
- Preserve every behavioural invariant of v002 unchanged: probation-first
  eviction, promote-on-probationary-hit, demote-on-overflow, the
  `prot_cap` geometry, the `FastHasher`/`FastBuildHasher`, and both
  fast paths (`prot_tail == Some(idx)` get short-circuit and the
  `seg_mru != Some(idx)` put-update skip) — rewritten against the `u32`
  endpoints (e.g. compare `self.prot_tail == link(idx)` instead of
  `Some(idx)`).

The claim is that the fast paths absorb the per-access widen so hit-rate
holds at 0.7241 and per-op-time stays near 35, while metadata-size drops
well below v002's 56.

## Rationale (optional)

This is the explicit Cycle 0002 continuation: fold the narrow-link
metadata win onto the SLRU-plus-fast-hasher base and measure whether the
encoding's latency tax survives once the fast paths absorb it. It probes
the open question of whether the 24-byte-class node and the
latency-optimal hot path can coexist in one Variant.
