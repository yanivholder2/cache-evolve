# H03

Replacing IRR (inter-reference interval) as the secondary eviction signal with staleness (op_clock minus last_access) in both the protected-demotion and probationary-eviction scans will improve hit rate over cycle/0009/v001 by penalizing entries whose historical frequency is high but whose most recent access is old.

## Parent

cycle/0009/v001

## Changes

Starting from cycle/0009/v001's source (SLRU 80/20, frequency primary + IRR tie-breaker, no ghost, op_clock, last_access/prev_access timestamps, MAX_FREQ=8):

1. **demote_prot_head()**: Replace the `(freq, irr)` composite with `(freq, staleness)`. Change the scan variables: rename `victim_irr: u64` to `victim_staleness: u64`, initialized to `0`. Compute `let staleness = self.op_clock - self.nodes[idx].last_access;` for each candidate. Update `is_better_victim` to: `freq < victim_freq || (freq == victim_freq && staleness > victim_staleness)`. Remove the `let irr = self.irr(idx);` call from this function.

2. **Probationary eviction scan in put()**: Apply the same substitution — replace `let irr = self.irr(idx);` with `let staleness = self.op_clock - self.nodes[idx].last_access;`. Rename `victim_irr: u64` to `victim_staleness: u64`, initialized to `0`. Update `is_better_victim` to `freq < victim_freq || (freq == victim_freq && staleness > victim_staleness)`.

3. **Node struct**: Remove the `prev_access: u64` field — it is no longer used for scoring. Update all initialization and reset sites to remove `prev_access: 0` assignments (in `put()` when allocating a fresh slot and when recycling an evicted slot).

4. **irr() helper**: Remove the `fn irr(&self, idx: usize) -> u64` function entirely — it is now dead code.

5. **op_clock and last_access**: Keep both fields and their advancement logic unchanged. In `get()`, remove `self.nodes[idx].prev_access = self.nodes[idx].last_access;` (prev_access field is gone); keep `self.nodes[idx].last_access = self.op_clock; self.op_clock += 1;`.

6. **Demotion behavior**: Keep freq-halving on demotion unchanged. The `last_access` timestamp is preserved across demotion (not reset), so a demoted entry's staleness correctly reflects time since it was last actually accessed, not time since demotion. This is the correct interpretation: a recently-accessed entry demoted for frequency reasons is less stale than a cold newcomer, and should be harder to evict.

7. **Unit tests**: 
   - Remove or rename `irr_timestamps_preserved_across_demotion` — adapt it to verify `last_access` (not `prev_access`) is preserved across demotion. The key observable invariant is that a demoted key's `last_access` is not reset.
   - Remove the `freq_dominates_irr_in_eviction` test and replace it with a `freq_dominates_staleness_in_eviction` test with the same structure but without references to `irr` or `prev_access`.
   - Keep all other tests unchanged; they do not depend on IRR internals.

## Rationale

Cycle/0009/v001 confirmed that IRR as a secondary signal over frequency ties is inert — equal-frequency candidates are rare enough in the narrow scan window that the tie-breaker never activates consequentially. Staleness (op_clock - last_access) is a different secondary: it does not require two prior accesses to be meaningful. Any entry with a single prior access — including every cold new arrival and every demoted entry — has a finite staleness that increases monotonically. On a LIRS-structured trace, entries that were frequently accessed in an older working set but have since fallen out of the hot loop will accumulate high staleness; the frequency counter (halved on each demotion but never cleared) may still be nonzero while the entry is functionally cold. Staleness can distinguish these "stale burst" entries from genuinely active entries at the same current frequency level. Cycle/0009/v003 validated a related idea (IRR times staleness as the sole eviction score) and produced a small but real gain on the IRR baseline; this hypothesis tests whether staleness as a secondary to frequency primary produces comparable or greater benefit.
