# H03

Combine occupancy-responsive thresholds with lightweight frequency-decay ghost tracking to recover dual-metric balance.

## Parent

cycle/0003/v003

## Changes

Integrate frequency-decay ghost-queue tracking (from v001) with reduced magnitude and overhead into v003's proven occupancy-responsive threshold design. In `src/s3_fifo/mod.rs`:

1. Change ghost-queue type from `VecDeque<K>` to `VecDeque<(K, u8)>` to track frequency alongside keys.

2. In `evict_main()`, when moving items to ghost:
   - Preserve the entry's current frequency (no decay on entry to ghost, unlike v001's immediate subtract)
   - Only discard entries that already have freq = 0 (items with no re-access signal)
   - Items with freq > 0 enter ghost with preserved frequency intact

3. In `put()`, when a ghost entry is re-accessed:
   - Extract preserved frequency from ghost entry
   - Re-admit with frequency = min(preserved_freq + 1, MAX_FREQ)
   - This preserves hotness signals without adding decay complexity

4. Keep v003's occupancy-responsive thresholds intact (freq > 1/2/3 based on occupancy), which operate on main-queue entries.

This avoids both v001's over-aggressive decay (which hurt hit-rate) and v003's structural sizing overhead (which hurt latency), instead adding minimal ghost-queue frequency tracking that preserves information without per-operation cost.

## Rationale

Cycle 0004 revealed that occupancy-responsive thresholds (v003) successfully balance metrics, but v001's frequency-decay ghost filtering was over-aggressive and v003's dynamic sizing added overhead. This hypothesis targets dual-metric improvement by preserving frequency information in ghost without decay-induced premature ejection, and without structural per-operation overhead. Ghost entries retain their frequency value, allowing re-accessed items to graduate with their hotness preserved, while occupancy-responsive thresholds continue to manage main-queue admission. Expected outcome: recover hit-rate improvement from better frequency preservation while maintaining v003's latency-efficient threshold adaptation.
