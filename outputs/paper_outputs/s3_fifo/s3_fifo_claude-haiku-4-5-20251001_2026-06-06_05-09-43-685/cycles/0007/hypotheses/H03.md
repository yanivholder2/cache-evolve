# H03

Implement recency-aware ghost re-admission to prioritize recently-evicted items and recover hit-rate by coupling frequency and age signals.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

In `src/s3_fifo/mod.rs`, enhance the ghost queue to track both frequency decay and entry age, using recency to scale re-admission boost:

1. Keep the ghost structure as `VecDeque<(K, i8)>` to track frequency decay like v006/v002.

2. In `evict_main()` and `evict_small()`, implement frequency decay identical to v006/v002:
   - Compute `ghost_freq = (tail.freq as i8).saturating_sub(1)`
   - Push to ghost only if `ghost_freq >= -2`
   - Ghost items are pushed to the front (push_front), older items toward the back (pop_back)

3. In `put()`, when a ghost hit is detected:
   - Find the position of the ghost entry in the deque: `let pos = self.ghost.iter().position(|(ghost_key, _)| ghost_key == &key)`
   - Extract the entry and compute its recency ratio: `recency_ratio = 1.0 - (pos as f64 / self.ghost.len() as f64)`
     - Recently evicted items (pos near 0, at front) have recency_ratio near 1.0
     - Aged items (pos near back) have recency_ratio near 0.0
   - Use recency-scaled boost: `boost = 2 + (recency_ratio * 2.0).floor() as i8`
     - Recently evicted items: boost = 3–4 (strong re-admission signal)
     - Aged ghost items: boost = 2 (weak re-admission signal)
   - Compute final frequency: `boosted_freq = (ghost_freq + boost).max(0) as u8`, clamped to MAX_FREQ

4. When re-admitting from ghost, create entry with the computed frequency and push to main queue (same as v006/v002 baseline behavior).

5. Use fixed promotion threshold `freq > 2` in `evict_small()` (no occupancy-responsive variation).

This tests a fundamentally different signal (recency in ghost, not main-queue occupancy or frequency decay alone) to recover hit-rate by capturing items that were genuinely hot but aged out of the cache due to capacity constraints. Recently evicted items are more likely to be re-accessed than items that have been ghosted for many operations, improving the signal fidelity of the re-admission mechanism.

## Rationale

Cycles 0003–0006 relied primarily on occupancy and frequency as signals; recency is unexplored and offers complementary information. Items evicted from small queue recently likely still have memory locality and re-access potential, even if their frequency count is low due to small-queue constraints. By weighting ghost re-admission toward recently evicted items, we recover a class of warm-but-cold-counted entries that frequency-only or occupancy-only approaches miss. Expected outcome: hit-rate improvement toward 0.720+ by better capturing workload phases where frequent items temporarily leave cache, coupled with low per-operation overhead since recency computation happens only on ghost hits (infrequent operations compared to main-queue traffic).
