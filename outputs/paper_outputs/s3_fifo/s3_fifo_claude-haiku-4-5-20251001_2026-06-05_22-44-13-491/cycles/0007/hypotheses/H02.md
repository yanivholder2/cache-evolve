# H02

Pair v003's proven 1/12 small-queue selectivity with MAX_FREQ=5 to test whether deeper frequency protection compounds synergistically at the tightest admission boundary yet explored.

## Parent

cycle/0006/v003

## Changes

Modify the frequency constant at the top of `/src/s3_fifo/mod.rs`. Change `const MAX_FREQ: u8 = 3;` to `const MAX_FREQ: u8 = 5;`. Keep the small-queue ratio at 1/12 (do not change the `(capacity / 12)` line in `try_new()`). This extends the maximum frequency counter by two levels, allowing items in the main queue to survive two additional eviction rounds before permanent discard. Items with freq > 0 are decremented and re-inserted; with MAX_FREQ=5, they persist longer. All other logic remains unchanged: frequency increments on access, freq > 1 for small-to-main promotion, main-queue eviction strategy identical. This tests whether deeper frequency protection delivers larger compounding gains when paired with the tightest structural selectivity (1/12) achieved so far.

## Rationale

The cross-cycle insights note that "frequency-depth and structural selectivity are orthogonal but unequally weighted" and explicitly raise the open question: "Does 1/12 pair synergistically with frequency-depth scaling? Could tighter structural selectivity unlock greater compounding with frequency-depth?" Cycles 0005 v003 (1/8+MAX_FREQ=5) and 0006 v002 (1/10+MAX_FREQ=4) tested frequency compounding at less-tight selectivity boundaries. This hypothesis tests whether the extreme tightness of 1/12 changes the compounding dynamic by pairing it with the maximum frequency depth tested to date (MAX_FREQ=5), allowing items that survived the stringent small-queue filtering to get maximum protection in the main queue.
