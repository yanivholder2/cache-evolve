# H01

Push extreme structural selectivity to 1/15 while maintaining frequency protection at MAX_FREQ=5 to test whether the structure-frequency compounding effect extends beyond the current winner's 1/14 selectivity.

## Parent

cycle/0008/v003

## Changes

Modify the `try_new()` method in `/src/s3_fifo/mod.rs` to reduce small-queue capacity from the current 1/14 ratio to 1/15. Change the line `(capacity / 14).max(1).min(capacity - 1)` to `(capacity / 15).max(1).min(capacity - 1)` when computing `small_size`. Keep `const MAX_FREQ: u8 = 5;` unchanged to maintain the frequency protection level that enabled v003's success. All other eviction logic remains identical: frequency increment on access, freq > 1 threshold for small-to-main promotion, main-queue second-chance eviction with frequency decrement.

## Rationale

Cycle 0008's v003 demonstrated that extreme structural selectivity (1/14) paired with moderate frequency protection (MAX_FREQ=5) outperforms less aggressive structure with deeper frequency (v002's 1/12+MAX_FREQ=6). The open question directly asks: can even tighter structural selectivity (1/15, 1/16) sustain performance if paired with sufficient frequency protection? Cycle 0007 showed 1/14 alone yielding marginal returns, but v003 proved that pairing it with MAX_FREQ=5 unlocks measurable gains. This hypothesis aggressively explores whether the admission gate can be tightened further while the frequency mechanism continues to separate signal from noise, potentially locating a higher peak in the structure-frequency interaction space beyond 1/14.
