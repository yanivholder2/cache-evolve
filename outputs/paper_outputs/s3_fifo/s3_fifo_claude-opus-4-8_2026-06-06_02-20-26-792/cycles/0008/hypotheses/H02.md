# H02

Stop spending ghost capacity on weak one-hit-wonder records and devote the entire ghost budget to proven main-evicted keys, raising hit-rate by catching more cooled-then-reused strong keys within the binding capacity constraint.

## Parent

cycle/0007/v002

## Changes

Cross-cycle analysis named *raw ghost capacity* the binding constraint and
proved ghost *depth* cannot relax it. The unexplored lever is reallocating the
fixed capacity by *what* it stores. Today both populations share the
`main_size`-bounded ghost: strong main-evicted records (`from_main: true`) and
weak small-evicted one-hit-wonders (`from_main: false`), with source-aware
trimming merely dropping weak ones first.

Push this to its extreme: do not ghost weak records at all.

- In `evict_small()`, delete the `else` branch that pushes
  `GhostKey { from_main: false }`. A small-queue entry with `freq <= 1` is
  simply dropped on eviction (no ghost record). The `freq > 1` promotion path
  to main is unchanged.
- The ghost queue now holds only `from_main: true` records, so the full
  `main_size` capacity is available to proven keys. `evict_ghost_one`'s
  weak-first `rposition` scan becomes a no-op fallback that always hits
  `pop_back`; leave the function as-is (still correct) or simplify it to
  `pop_back()`.
- Preserve the `put()` in-ghost re-admission path exactly. It now fires only
  for strong keys; a one-hit-wonder that is reaccessed after eviction re-enters
  the small queue and must again earn `freq > 1` to promote — a deliberately
  stricter admission filter, which prior cycles found load-bearing on this
  trace.

Keep partition sizing, main move-to-front, and the `evict_main` rotation
untouched.

## Rationale (optional)

Weak records are documented as low value, yet they still consume capacity that
the strong population is starved for. Removing them entirely tests whether the
capacity constraint relaxes for proven keys without leaking one-hit-wonders —
the explicit open question left by cycle 0007.
