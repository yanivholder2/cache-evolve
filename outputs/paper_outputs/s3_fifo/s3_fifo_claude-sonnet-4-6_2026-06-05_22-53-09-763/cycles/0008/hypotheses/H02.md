# H02

Reducing the small queue from `capacity / 10` (~10%) to `capacity / 14` (~7%) transfers additional resident slots to main, further limiting cache pollution from one-time-access traffic on a Zipfian workload.

## Parent

cycle/0007/v001

## Changes

In `try_new()`, change the `small_size` calculation from:

```
(capacity / 10).max(1).min(capacity - 1)
```

to:

```
(capacity / 14).max(1).min(capacity - 1)
```

`main_size = capacity - small_size` is derived automatically and will grow by approximately 3 percentage points. `ghost_size = (main_size / 2).max(1)` is also derived from `main_size` and will scale up proportionally — no explicit change needed.

All other parameters are inherited unchanged from the parent: `MAX_FREQ = 5`, the `evict_main()` second-chance threshold `freq > 1`, the `evict_small()` promotion threshold `freq > 1`, and the eager-promotion logic in `get()` (small-queue items with freq >= 2 are immediately moved to the front of main).

The capacity-bound unit tests use small fixed-size caches (capacity 4 and 8) where integer division may cause `capacity / 14` to equal `capacity / 10` — verify that those tests still pass without modification and that the behavioural difference is measurable at the benchmark capacity of 2048.

## Rationale

The largest single gain in this research was the 25% → 10% small-queue reduction in cycle 0001. The hypothesis is that the benefit is monotone over a wider range than has been tested: if small-queue pollution is the dominant remaining loss mechanism, a further ~3-percentage-point shift to main should yield another increment. The cross-cycle note characterises 10% as the "near-optimal floor" but no cycle has explicitly measured sub-10% performance; this variant provides the empirical data point.
