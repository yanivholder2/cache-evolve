# H01

Raising the main-queue second-chance threshold from `freq > 1` to `freq > 2` concentrates main capacity on the most-accessed items, improving hit rate on a Zipfian workload by accelerating eviction of medium-frequency residents.

## Parent

cycle/0007/v001

## Changes

In `evict_main()`, change the second-chance condition from `if tail.freq > 1` to `if tail.freq > 2`. Items whose frequency counter has fallen to 0, 1, or 2 when they reach the eviction tail are evicted immediately without a recycle to the front of the queue. Only items with freq >= 3 receive the decrement-and-requeue treatment (freq is decremented by 1 and the item is pushed to the front).

The prior evolution tightened the second-chance gate once already (freq > 0 → freq > 1 in cycle/0006), and that change was a winner. This hypothesis takes the next step on the same axis: reserving the main queue for items that have accumulated at least three accesses, which on a Zipfian distribution corresponds to a materially higher-demand tier.

No other changes are required. `evict_small()` promotion threshold (`freq > 1`), `MAX_FREQ = 5`, `small_size = capacity / 10`, `ghost_size = main_size / 2`, and the eager-promotion logic in `get()` are all inherited unchanged from the parent. The existing unit test `main_eviction_uses_frequency_second_chance` constructs entries with specific freq values; verify that any entry with freq == 2 that previously survived by one recycle is now treated as an eviction candidate, and update the test's assertions accordingly.

## Rationale

The progression freq > 0 → freq > 1 yielded a measurable gain in cycle 0006 by keeping genuinely re-used items in main longer. The same logic applies to the next increment: on a Zipfian trace, items accessed exactly twice occupy valuable main slots but have weaker recency evidence than items accessed three or more times. Evicting them immediately frees space for the denser hot-item population and removes one round of second-chance churn per eviction cycle.
