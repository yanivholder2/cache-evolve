## Recurring wins

- **O(n) → O(1) bottleneck elimination drives the largest gains**: Every era's dominant win was identifying and collapsing a linear scan — ghost-queue membership check (cycles 0001–0002), `position()` in get/put (cycles 0004–0005), two-probe put pattern via unified `state_map` (cycle 0010). Each resolved the binding bottleneck for that era and unlocked downstream improvements.
- **Structural consolidation outperforms decomposition**: Unified data structures (`resident_map<K,(V,u8)>`, then `state_map` collapsing ghost state) consistently compounded gains by eliminating redundant probes and shedding parallel structures. Every attempt to split maps into parallel hashmaps regressed on write overhead.
- **Small-queue fraction 1/20 is the confirmed Zipfian optimum**: Monotone improvement from 1/4 → 1/10 → 1/20 across cycles 0001–0003. The lever is exhausted: 1/40 marginal with latency trade-off, 1/80 net negative due to `evict_small()` invocation-rate explosion.
- **Lazy/amortized cleanup reliably beats eager removal**: Deferring ghost tombstone cleanup to the capacity guard (cycle 0002) cut put tail latency by 5× and has held as the correct policy across all subsequent cycles.
- **`freq > 1` ghost promotion threshold is load-bearing**: The paper's threshold survived every challenge. Promoting at `freq > 0` floods main with once-warm entries lacking recency evidence; the ghost channel is a dual gate (frequency *and* demand confirmation), not a bookkeeping conservative.
- **`MAX_FREQ=4` is the Zipfian sweet spot**: Better than 3 (free hit-rate gain confirmed in cycle 0010) but worse than 5 (threshold regression confirmed in cycle 0007). The curve is non-smooth — 4 sits in a measurable plateau.

## Recurring failures

- **Bounded second-chance scan cap**: Proposed twice (cycles 0002–0003), falsified in cycle 0004. Zipfian cold entries cluster naturally at the main-queue tail, so the unbounded scan already terminates early; a cap only forces premature eviction of still-hot entries beyond the budget.
- **Eviction batching**: Burst-draining multiple evictions per `put()` (cycle 0008) made per-call cost super-linear, not amortized. One-at-a-time eviction is the correct policy for this implementation.
- **Map decomposition**: Splitting `resident_map` into parallel hashmaps (cycles 0006, 0009) doubled write overhead on every admission and eviction without recovering cache-density benefit — the hot set stays L1-resident regardless of layout.
- **Ghost capacity beyond `main_size`**: Extended ghost window (cycle 0006) accumulates useless tombstones; the Zipfian cold tail does not re-request within the wider window. `main_size` is confirmed tight.
- **Frequency step acceleration (step=2)**: Draining frequency faster (cycle 0004) amplified total eviction work by increasing ghost-hit-driven `evict_main()` calls, even as per-scan length shortened. Scan-length reduction and total-eviction-work reduction are decoupled and opposed here.
- **Custom hasher / probe-count reduction**: FNV-1a swap and map-merge to halve probes (cycle 0009) produced no measurable benefit. The Zipfian hot set's L1 residency means hash probe chain length is not on the measured critical path.
- **Write-skip guards for saturated entries**: Skipping freq writes on max-freq entries (cycle 0009) let those cache lines cool between `evict_main()` calls, introducing cold-miss penalties during eviction. Unconditional writes are a net benefit.
- **`freq=1` ghost admission**: Granting re-promoted entries a free second-chance pass (cycle 0007) extended `evict_main()` scan depth and displaced hotter entries. `freq=0` on ghost admission is confirmed correct.

## Open questions

- **Is get p50 at the implementation floor?** Four consecutive falsification cycles targeting the get path (cycles 0006–0009) suggest the Zipfian hot set's L1 residency may have saturated achievable get latency. A workload probing the cold or medium-frequency tier might reveal structural headroom that the hot-path focus cannot surface.
- **Admission-sketch threshold calibration**: Cycle 0010 v002 confirmed the direction is valid — sketch filtering reduces `evict_small()` invocation rate — but first-seen rejection over-filters workload-a's warm-cold keys. A two-visit requirement or faster sketch decay may recover hit-rate without sacrificing the eviction-rate reduction.
- **Winners anomaly in the fitness function**: Net-negative variants have landed in the Winners list in at least seven of ten cycles. The fitness function appears to weight a dimension (possibly a specific latency percentile or aggregate score) that lets hit-rate regressions be offset. If this weighting does not reflect the intended optimisation target, it may be distorting the search signal in ways that make falsification cycles indistinguishable from wins.
- **What is the current dominant per-operation cost?** With O(n) scans eliminated, ghost probes collapsed into `state_map`, and `MAX_FREQ` optimized, no bottleneck has been positively identified for cycles beyond 0010. `evict_main()` deque memory-access pattern and hash-collision behavior under load are uncharacterized candidates.

## Per-policy notes

- **Small queue (admission)**: The 1/20 fraction is the exhausted optimum on Zipfian. `evict_small()` invocation rate — not per-call overhead or hash efficiency — is the binding cost when the fraction tightens below this point. Hash pre-sizing and per-call optimization are both confirmed irrelevant to this cost.
- **Main queue (second-chance clock)**: The unbounded second-chance scan is naturally efficient on Zipfian because cold entries cluster at the tail. Artificial scan caps, two-random-choice victim selection, and `MAX_FREQ` values above 4 are all ruled out. The eviction loop is near its practical floor for this workload.
- **Ghost queue (demand confirmation)**: Capacity at `main_size` is confirmed tight and correct. Lazy tombstone cleanup (deferred to the capacity guard) is the correct deletion policy. The `freq > 1` promotion threshold is load-bearing as a demand-confirmation gate, not just frequency bookkeeping. `freq=0` on ghost admission is confirmed correct.
- **Data structure (unified state map)**: The single `state_map` encoding value, frequency, and ghost-state in one hash probe is the confirmed optimal layout across all tested alternatives. Splitting, merging, or swapping its hasher all regressed or had no effect. Unconditional freq writes on every access keep hot-set cache lines warm for subsequent eviction scans and are a net benefit despite the write cost.
- **Admission selectivity**: Sketch-based filtering is the one remaining open lever — it demonstrably reduces `evict_small()` call rate, but the rejection threshold must be tuned to avoid over-filtering the warm-cold segment that workload-a contains.
