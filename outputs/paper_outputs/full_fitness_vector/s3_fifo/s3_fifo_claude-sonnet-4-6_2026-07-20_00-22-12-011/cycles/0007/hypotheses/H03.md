# H03

Raising `MAX_FREQ` from `3` to `5` extends the second-chance eviction window for highly-accessed entries in the main queue, allowing the Zipfian hot set to accumulate more protection credits and remain resident longer, improving hit-rate.

## Parent

cycle/0005/v001

## Changes

In `src/s3_fifo/mod.rs`, change the constant declaration from:

```rust
const MAX_FREQ: u8 = 3;
```

to:

```rust
const MAX_FREQ: u8 = 5;
```

This is the only change. The constant is referenced in exactly two places:
1. `get()`: `*freq = freq.saturating_add(1).min(MAX_FREQ)` — caps the frequency increment on each cache hit.
2. `evict_main()`: used implicitly via the frequency values stored in `freq_map`; an entry with `freq=5` now requires five `evict_main()` passes (each decrementing by one) before its frequency reaches 0 and it is evicted.

All other logic in cycle/0005/v001 is preserved unchanged: the 1/40 small-queue fraction, the `freq > 1` small-queue promotion threshold in `evict_small()`, the split `data`+`freq_map` layout, the ghost lazy-deletion pattern, and the `ghost_set` HashSet membership test.

## Rationale

`MAX_FREQ` has been fixed at `3` since cycle/0001 and has never been varied across any of the six completed cycles. On a Zipfian workload a small fraction of keys accounts for nearly all accesses; those keys are requested many times before the cache reaches steady state, and many of them accumulate the `MAX_FREQ` cap quickly. A higher cap of `5` means the hottest entries receive up to five second-chance passes in `evict_main()` rather than three — roughly a 67% longer protection window — before they can be displaced. The expected outcome is improved main-queue retention of the Zipfian hot set and a small but measurable hit-rate gain. The tradeoff is that entries with moderate recency (freq between 3 and 5) will also take longer to age out, potentially delaying eviction of entries that are no longer hot; whether this effect dominates or is outweighed by the hot-set retention benefit is what this hypothesis tests.
