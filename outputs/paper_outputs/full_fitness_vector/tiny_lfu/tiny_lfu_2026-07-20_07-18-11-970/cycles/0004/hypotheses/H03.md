# H03

Requiring an incoming item to exceed the victim's estimated frequency by at least two counts before admission will reduce noise-driven eviction churn on the stable LIRS working set and improve hit rate.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

In `src/tiny_lfu/mod.rs`, change the admission check in `TinyLfuCache::put` — the condition that guards replacing the victim — from:

```rust
if incoming_freq > victim_freq {
```

to:

```rust
if incoming_freq > victim_freq.saturating_add(1) {
```

`saturating_add(1)` is used rather than plain `victim_freq + 1` to avoid the edge case where `victim_freq == u32::MAX`; in practice counters on this workload will never reach saturation, but the saturating form is correct in all cases.

No other changes: the struct layout, sketch, hash logic, LRU operations, tests, and all remaining logic in `put` and `get` remain exactly as in the baseline.

The threshold of 2 (i.e., `incoming_freq > victim_freq + 1`) means:
- A new item with frequency equal to or one greater than the victim is now **rejected** (previously it was admitted when strictly greater).
- A new item with frequency at least two greater than the victim is **admitted**, unchanged.

This tightens admission in the borderline zone where sketch estimation noise is most misleading: two items with near-identical true frequencies may have CMS estimates that differ by ±1 due to hash collisions, causing the cache to flip the victim unnecessarily. Requiring a margin of 2 makes the admission decision robust to single-count estimation noise.

## Rationale

On the LIRS trace — a stable Zipfian working set — the cache reaches a steady state where most items in the cache are hot and most eviction candidates are cold. The admission check (`incoming_freq > victim_freq`) is correct in most cases, but the marginal case (incoming_freq == victim_freq + 1) is ambiguous: the true frequencies might be equal or even reversed, and the sketch's min-estimate adds noise in both directions. Strengthening the admission condition to require a gap of at least 2 gives incumbents a one-count advantage, stabilising the resident set without blocking genuinely hot newcomers (which exceed any threshold that matters). This tests whether admission stability — an unexplored policy axis across all three prior cycles — can independently lift hit rate on this trace.
