# Cycle 0010 Insight

## Cohort outcome

This cycle tested three refinements to admission and eviction policies following cycle 0009's success with asymmetric admission and extended victim selection. **All three variants advanced**, but revealed that mechanisms previously assumed orthogonal are tightly coupled through admission pressure. v001 composed asymmetric admission with N=3 victim selection, delivering dominant latency improvements (23.5% get p99, 16.7% put p99) at the cost of hit-rate decline. v002 extended victim selection from N=3 to N=4, confirming N=3 is the optimal sweet spot as returns collapse from 0.37% hit-rate gain to 0.03%. v003 isolated asymmetric admission with a symmetric threshold on baseline, showing that update-preferential logic alone drives substantial latency gains (~16% on median operations) without hit-rate change. **The cohort establishes that asymmetric admission and extended victim selection are not independent levers but interact through eviction/admission pressure balance.**

## Patterns

- **Asymmetric admission is a latency optimization, not a hit-rate mechanism**: v003 proves that distinguishing updates (always admitted) from new entries (gated by frequency) delivers ~16% latency improvement on median operations without affecting hit-rate (0.7362 vs. baseline). This directly contradicts cycle 0009's continuation assumption that asymmetry would compound v001's hit-rate gains. Instead, asymmetry's benefit derives from streamlining the hot path (existing-key updates skip the frequency comparison), not from semantic insight into frequency estimation. The stricter gate for new arrivals is a latency-reducing side effect (anti-pollution, fewer evictions), not the primary mechanism.

- **N=3 is the definitive sweet spot for victim selection; N=4 enters diminishing-returns regime**: v002's extension from N=3 to N=4 collapses hit-rate gains from 0.37% (cycle 0009 v001) to 0.03%, a 90% reduction in marginal benefit. Simultaneously, get p99 latency increases by 25ns, indicating scanning overhead now dominates frequency-diversity gains. This tightly brackets the optimum: N=3 captures meaningful frequency signals at acceptable cost; N=4 is clearly over-provisioned. The boundary is sharp rather than gradual, confirming N=3 aligns with the sketch's collision-probability characteristics (similar to the depth=3, width=3 boundary from cycle 0008).

- **Admission and eviction policies interact through shared pressure, not independently**: Cycle 0009's continuation hypothesized that v003's asymmetric admission and v001's N=3 victim selection were orthogonal levers targeting different decision points. v001 disproves this: composing the two mechanisms does not compound hit-rate gains as predicted. Instead, the stricter admission gate for new items—when combined with deeper victim selection—constrains admission pressure, reducing the pool of items eligible for insertion. The result is fewer evictions (latency win) but also fewer admissions of marginal entries (hit-rate loss). The mechanisms trade off through the same resource—eviction slots—rather than scaling independently.

- **Composition amplifies latency benefits but reverses hit-rate improvements**: v001 achieves the most dominant latency improvements of the cycle (get p99 +23.5%, put p99 -16.7%), but this comes with hit-rate decline (-0.096%), a reversal of v001's prior behavior in cycle 0009. The stricter admission gate (requiring `incoming_freq > victim_freq + 1` for new items) acts as a pollution filter, reducing admission pressure and eviction storms. In isolation (cycle 0009), this was acceptable because it enabled deeper victim scanning; in composition with N=3, it prevents borderline-hot entries from admitting, causing hit-rate loss despite fewer evictions.

- **The policy space is tightly constrained; further composition appears counterproductive**: This cycle tested two orthogonality hypotheses (H01 composition, H03 isolation). H01 showed composition breaks assumptions; H03 showed isolation preserves the asymmetry benefit without contaminating hit-rate. The implication is that combining multiple policy changes in hopes of compounding gains is risky—mechanisms interact through admission/eviction pressure in ways that are not additive. Each mechanism must be evaluated independently or within narrow constraints to avoid inadvertent reversals.

## Open questions

- Why does the composition of v003's asymmetric gate with N=3 victim selection reverse v001's expected hit-rate improvement from cycle 0009? Is the N+1 threshold (stricter by design) or the interaction with deeper victim scanning the culprit?

- Does N=4 ever become viable with asymmetric admission (v001's policy)? Could the stricter gate's pollution filtering make deeper victim scanning tolerable?

- Could the asymmetric gate's threshold be tuned dynamically or phase-adaptively to preserve hit-rate improvements while retaining latency gains, or is the tradeoff fundamental?

## Continuation

**Freeze N=3 as the optimal victim pool size and deprioritize further victim-selection tuning.** v002 definitively establishes that N=4 enters diminishing-returns territory; returns collapse to 0.03% hit-rate gain with latency cost. N=3 was established as superior in cycle 0009 and is now validated as a stable frontier. **Adopt asymmetric admission as a latency optimization for median-case performance, separate from hit-rate objectives.** v003 shows that distinguishing updates (always admitted) from new entries improves latency by streamlining the hot path, without hit-rate side effects when applied to baseline. The strict gate (`incoming_freq > victim_freq + 1` for new items) is orthogonal to this latency benefit and introduces hit-rate tradeoffs when composed with extended victim selection; if adopted, apply it only if tail-latency improvements justify hit-rate loss. **Treat admission and eviction policies as a coupled system, not independent levers.** v001's unexpected hit-rate decline reveals that changes to admission thresholds interact with victim-selection depth through shared eviction pressure. Future optimizations should model the two together in sensitivity analysis before testing compositions. **Shift focus to orthogonal mechanisms orthogonal to the admission-eviction balance**: e.g., frequency estimation efficiency (hash function cost, sketch implementation), workload-adaptive parameter scheduling, or alternative admission criteria (e.g., recency heuristics, cost-aware policies). The admission-eviction space has reached a point where marginal improvements require tight coupling and sensitivity; the next frontier likely lies in mechanisms that do not perturb this balance.
