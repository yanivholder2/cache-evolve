# H01

Reduce promotion selectivity on 75/25 geometry by tuning the adaptive divisor formula from stash_buckets/4 to stash_buckets/3, thereby increasing promotion candidate throughput and reducing per-op latency while preserving hit-rate on the current best allocation.

## Parent

cycle/0009/v003

## Changes

In `src/dash/segments/mod.rs`, replace the `compute_promotion_threshold` function's divisor constant. Change the formula from `stash_buckets / 4` to `stash_buckets / 3`. This increases the promotion threshold (higher threshold = stricter gating becomes more permissive), allowing more items to transition from target to probing tier with fewer access-frequency requirements. On v003's 75/25 geometry (8 stash buckets), this changes the threshold from 2 to 3, reducing false-negatives where hot items remain trapped in the target tier and must be repeatedly probed. Preserve all other tier-crossing logic, demotion cascade mechanics, and the geometry allocation itself.

The inverse-coupling principle observed in cycles 0007–0009 suggests that larger stash capacity (25% vs 20%) can sustain a more permissive divisor: items promoted more eagerly will have more stash space before triggering eviction cascades. The change directly targets latency improvement (fewer LRU climbs in target, faster transition to probing) while maintaining hit-rate by exploiting v003's proven stash-capacity envelope.

## Rationale

v003 achieved the highest hit-rate (0.7169) but per-op-time (79) lags v002's efficiency (77). The latency gap is attributable to higher promotion selectivity: threshold=2 on 8 stash buckets filters aggressively, leaving frequent accesses repeated in target tier. Increasing divisor from 4 to 3 reduces the filtering pressure without destabilizing the tier allocation, because the 25% stash capacity provides buffer room that stricter geometries (80/20, 85/15) do not. This refinement tests the inverse-coupling hypothesis at mid-range allocation while staying within the proven local optimum band (75/25–80/20) identified in cycle 0009.
