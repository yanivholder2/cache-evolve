# H03

Require two hits (not one) before an item becomes scan-resistant, raising the admission bar for one-hit-wonders to test whether it lifts hit-rate above the 0.7267 ceiling.

## Parent

cycle/0008/v002

## Changes

Target `src/associative_cache/associative_cache_bucket.rs`. v002's scan
resistance is binary: a single hit sets `reuse[i] = true` and thereafter the item
is protected from eviction until a bucket saturates. This admits any key that is
touched even once — including short-lived one-hit-wonders that a scan drags in.
This hypothesis raises the protection threshold from one hit to two.

Replace the `reuse: Vec<bool>` with a small saturating counter parked in the
bucket (e.g. `hits: Vec<u8>`, or a pair of parallel `u64` bitmasks encoding a
2-level count) — still off the measured `Item`, so metadata stays 16. Keep it
index-aligned with `items` exactly as v002 keeps `reuse` (mirror the policy's
single-element relocation in `get_from_position` with the same index-preserving
`remove(position)` + `insert(index, ...)` move; never rebuild by key search).

Semantics:
- Insert (miss): count starts at 0.
- `get_from_position` (hit): increment the accessed slot's count, saturating at 2.
- `eviction_candidate_index`: treat a slot as "protected" only when its count
  `>= 2`; evict the first slot in LRU-front order whose count is `< 2`
  (one-hit-wonders and never-hit items are both evictable, with the never-hit /
  lower-count ones preferred — scan front order among the unprotected set). If
  every resident slot has count `>= 2`, fall back to the LRU front (index 0),
  exactly as v002 falls back today.

Everything else (ClassicLRU move-to-back, the 16-byte `Item`, the parallel-state
alignment discipline) stays identical. The claim is purely about hit-rate: does
demanding a second hit before granting protection filter enough scan noise to
push past 0.7267, or does it under-protect genuinely hot keys (as gradual
promotion did on short buckets) and regress? Either result resolves the open
"is there hit-rate headroom left" question for the admission-threshold lever.

## Rationale (optional)

The one-hit protection bit and SLRU segmentation both plateau at 0.7267. The
number of hits required to earn protection is a distinct, un-swept knob (prior
frequency work used LFU *victim* signals or cross-eviction ghost memory, not an
in-residency protection threshold), so it is the least-explored remaining
hit-rate lever and keeps the metadata floor intact.
