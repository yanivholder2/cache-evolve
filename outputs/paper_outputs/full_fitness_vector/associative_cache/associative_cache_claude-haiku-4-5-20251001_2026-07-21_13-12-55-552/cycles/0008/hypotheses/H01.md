# H01

Scale cycle/0004/v003's micro-optimized 32×64 geometry to a larger cache (4096 entries) to validate whether its latency efficiency is a generalizable principle or narrowly tuned to 2048-entry workload scale.

## Parent

cycle/0004/v003

## Changes

Increase the cache capacity from 2048 to 4096 entries and scale the bucket geometry from 32×64 to 64×64 (maintaining the same items-per-bucket count). Preserve all implementation details: early-exit on key match in bucket scanning, cache-line-alignment (64-byte boundaries) of bucket memory layout, and ClassicLRU eviction policy with positional tracking. Measure hit-rate on the same Zipfian workload and compare per-operation latency (get p50/p99, put p50/p99) to baseline at the larger cache size.

## Rationale

Cycle 0004 v003 is the only variant across all seven cycles to achieve net fitness improvement through micro-optimization. However, that success was on a specific cache size (2048 entries, 32×64 geometry). Cycles 0005–0007 failed to generalize micro-optimizations to other geometries, raising the question: is the 32×64 configuration a robust optimum, or was it finely tuned to the 2048-entry scale and Zipfian workload?

This hypothesis follows Path A (robustness validation) recommended by the cross-cycle insights. By scaling proportionally to 4096 entries, we test whether the latency-efficiency principle (early-exit + cache alignment) remains effective at different scale, or whether the micro-optimization degrades—as micro-optimizations did across cycle 0005 when applied to different geometries. If the scaled variant maintains fitness improvement, cycle/0004/v003 becomes a credible foundation for further refinement; if it regresses, the micro-optimization is confirmed as a narrow local optimum.
