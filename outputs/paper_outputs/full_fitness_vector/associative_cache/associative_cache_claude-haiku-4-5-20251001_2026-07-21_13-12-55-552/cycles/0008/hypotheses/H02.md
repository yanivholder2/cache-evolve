# H02

Add tail-preferential eviction bias to cycle/0004/v003 to improve hit-rate by implicitly preserving recently inserted (hotter) items without the frequency-tracking overhead that degraded cycle/0006/v003.

## Parent

cycle/0004/v003

## Changes

Modify the eviction policy in cycle/0004/v003's ClassicLRU implementation: when selecting a victim from a full bucket, prefer to evict items from the tail of the bucket (end of the items vector) rather than applying strict least-recently-used order. Specifically, iterate the bucket backward from the end and evict the first item that has not been accessed recently (track a simple "recent access" flag per item that resets every N operations where N is tuned empirically, default 64). If all items in the tail are recent, then fall back to LRU eviction. Preserve all other aspects: 32×64 geometry, early-exit optimization, cache-line alignment, and single-vector bucket structure.

## Rationale

Cycle 0006 v003's adaptive hot/cold partitioning achieved 0.6988 hit-rate (best on Zipfian) by implicitly learning that recently-accessed items are more likely to be reused. However, the full implementation (dual indices, frequency counters, decay loops, promotion logic) added per-operation overhead that degraded latency. Cycle 0007 confirmed that deferred bookkeeping does not eliminate cost—it merely redistributes it.

This hypothesis tests a middle ground: instead of explicit frequency tracking like v003, use a simple recency flag and tail-preference in eviction. The intuition is that recently-inserted items are often hot (Zipfian property: accesses cluster temporally), and evicting from the tail naturally preserves them. Unlike cycle/0006/v003, this requires no dual indices, frequency decay, or promotion scanning—just a simple boolean per item.

This follows Path A (refinement of recent winner cycle/0004/v003) by attempting to improve its hit-rate incrementally while preserving the latency efficiency that made it unique. Success would confirm that hit-rate and latency can be jointly optimized through smarter eviction bias rather than structural complexity.
