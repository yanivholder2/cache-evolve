# H03

Test whether bounded-window LRU without any frequency tracking can match hit rate of frequency-aware variants by calibrating the window size to the cache capacity.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Implement pure LRU with a dynamically-calibrated bounded search window (no frequency counters, no adaptive bucketing, no saturation checks). Maintain a simple doubly-linked list of all nodes in recency order (most recently used at tail, least recently used at head). During eviction, scan the first `min(capacity / 3, 512)` nodes from the head and evict the first one encountered. This window size is larger than Cycle 0002's bounded window (`capacity / 4`) to account for the loss of frequency-aware selectivity, and is fixed (not adaptive) to eliminate per-operation decision overhead. Remove all metadata fields related to frequency (`freq`, `freq_buckets`, `min_freq`, `saturation_threshold`, etc.), reducing the per-item node size from 56 bytes (in cycle/0003/v003) to approximately 40 bytes by eliminating the `freq: u32` field. Measure hit rate against the baseline and cycle/0003/v003 to determine whether frequency tracking provides non-eliminable selectivity benefit on this workload, or whether window-size calibration alone captures the necessary recency skew.

## Rationale

Across eight cycles, hit rate has plateaued at 0.69 regardless of whether frequency tracking, recency counting, or adaptive bucketing is employed. Cycle 0007 V002 demonstrated that pure FIFO with a larger fixed window maintains hit rate unchanged from the frequency-tracking baseline. However, Cycle 0008 V002 showed a 4.8% hit-rate drop when frequency was removed with a smaller window, suggesting hit rate is sensitive to window size and selectivity strategy. This hypothesis tests the hypothesis directly: by increasing the bounded-window size from capacity/4 (Cycle 0002 baseline) to capacity/3, can pure LRU match or exceed the hit rate of frequency-aware variants? If yes, then frequency tracking is metadata overhead without benefit on this workload, and the simpler algorithm should become the baseline. If no, it reveals that frequency tracking provides non-eliminable selectivity beyond recency, justifying its overhead. This tests the open question from Cycle 0008: "Is V002's 4.8% hit-rate degradation fundamental to FIFO+window strategies, or an artifact of the specific window-checking cost?" By using true LRU (not FIFO with windows), we isolate the effect of window size from the effect of eviction strategy.
