# H03

Evicting the lowest-frequency victim among a small sample of the oldest entries, instead of the strict LRU head, raises hit-rate above v003's 0.7362.

## Parent

cycle/0001/v003

## Changes

v003 keeps baseline residency: the victim is always the LRU head, and an
incoming key is admitted only if its sketch estimate strictly exceeds
that single victim's. This discards genuinely hot keys that happen to sit
near the head. Change victim selection in `put()` (the full-cache branch)
to a sampled-coldest policy:

- Walk forward from `self.head` along `next` to collect up to `S`
  candidate node indices (introduce `const SAMPLE_VICTIMS: usize = 5;`
  near the other sketch constants). Stop early if the list ends.
- Among the sampled candidates, pick the one whose `sketch.estimate(key)`
  is minimum; break ties toward the older (closer to head) candidate so
  recency still matters.
- Compare the incoming key's estimate against this minimum-frequency
  victim using the existing strict-`>` admission test, then perform the
  same `map.remove` / `unlink` / overwrite / `push_back` / `map.insert`
  sequence on the chosen victim index.

Keep v003's `u8` counters, single-position-per-row hashing, sketch
geometry, and `get()`/promotion logic unchanged. Preserve all inline
tests — with capacity 2 the sample spans the whole list, so
`eviction_prefers_hot_items` and `low_frequency_new_item_is_rejected`
must still pass (the coldest of two is the LRU head when frequencies are
equal). The claim: sampling lets the admission filter protect hot keys
that pure-LRU eviction would have dropped, lifting hit-rate at a small,
bounded per-op cost.
