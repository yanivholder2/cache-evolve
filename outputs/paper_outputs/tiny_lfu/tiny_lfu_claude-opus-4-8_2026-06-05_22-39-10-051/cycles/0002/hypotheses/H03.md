# H03

Quadrupling the count-min sketch width (SKETCH_WIDTH_FACTOR 4→16) reduces collision overestimation and improves the frequency comparisons admission and victim selection depend on, raising hit-rate.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

In the baseline `src/tiny_lfu/mod.rs`, the sketch width is derived as
`(capacity * SKETCH_WIDTH_FACTOR).max(MIN_SKETCH_WIDTH).next_power_of_two()`
with `SKETCH_WIDTH_FACTOR = 4`. At capacity 2048 that is a width-8192 sketch;
with more than ~8k distinct keys in the trace, count-min cells collide and the
`min`-over-rows `estimate` systematically overestimates cold keys. Both the
baseline admission gate (`incoming_freq > victim_freq`) and any
frequency-ranked decision read those biased estimates, so cold residents are
wrongly protected and hot newcomers wrongly rejected.

Make exactly one change: raise `const SKETCH_WIDTH_FACTOR: usize` from `4` to
`16` (width becomes capacity*16, next power of two). Leave `SKETCH_DEPTH`,
`MIN_SKETCH_WIDTH`, the hashing in `position`, the increment/estimate logic,
and the entire LRU/admission path untouched. This isolates estimator
fidelity — magnitude of count-min error — as the single independent variable.

Preserve invariants: width stays a power of two (so the `& (width - 1)` mask is
valid), memory growth is bounded (the sketch is the only structure that scales),
and admission/eviction semantics are byte-for-byte the baseline's apart from the
sharper counts. The testable assertion: a less noisy frequency signal lifts
hit-rate above the baseline's 0.736, validating that this workload's wins come
from spending an accurate signal rather than from new policy mechanics.
