# H02

Replace continuous victim-scoring with frequency-tier-based selection to improve robustness under frequency-estimation noise, improving hit-rate by always preferring victims from the coldest frequency cohort before considering age.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

In the victim-selection loop within the `put()` method (where a victim must be chosen from full cache), implement a four-tier frequency histogram instead of computing a single maximum-score victim across all items.

First, scan all cached items and collect their frequency estimates from the sketch. Compute the 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles of these frequencies. Create four frequency tiers:
- Tier 1 (coldest): frequency <= 25th percentile
- Tier 2: frequency <= 50th percentile
- Tier 3: frequency <= 75th percentile
- Tier 4 (hottest): frequency > 75th percentile

When selecting a victim, iterate through tiers in order (Tier 1 first). For the first non-empty tier, find the oldest item (by position in the LRU list) and select it as the victim. Do not compute the complex frequency-age score formula; instead use frequency-coldness as the primary selection criterion with age as the strict secondary discriminator within the tier.

Keep the admission gate (`incoming_freq > victim_freq`) unchanged. Sketch parameters, counter saturation, and all other logic remain the same.

## Rationale

Cycles 0004–0006 showed that fine-tuning the continuous scoring formula (adjusting exponents, constants, normalization) yields diminishing returns. The victim-selection metric appears locally optimal, but the underlying problem may be that when frequency estimates are noisy (from hash collisions), a single global score can be misled by outliers. Tiering separates the decision into two distinct phases: first, identify which frequency cohort contains victims (frequency matters absolutely), then within that cohort, use age for ranking. This structure is more robust to estimation noise because it doesn't depend on precise score magnitudes—only on which tier a candidate falls into. The approach recognizes that victims should come from genuinely cold items, with age as a tiebreaker, rather than computing a complex formula across all items.
