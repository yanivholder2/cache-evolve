# H01

Enhance victim selection by incorporating access burstiness alongside frequency and age, improving hit-rate by recognizing clustered access patterns as stronger temporal locality signals than sparse old accesses.

## Parent

cycle/0005/v001

## Changes

Extend the `Node` struct to track `burstiness_window` (number of accesses to this item within the last W steps, where W = capacity / 10) and `total_accesses_ever` (cumulative count of all accesses). At each access via `get()`, increment both counters; reset `burstiness_window` every W global access steps.

In the victim-selection loop (the scan through cached items in `put()`), compute a burstiness factor for each candidate: `burstiness_factor = 1.0 + (burstiness_window as f64 / total_accesses_ever.max(1) as f64)`. Modify the victim scoring formula from `score = age / (frequency + 2.0)` to `score = (age * burstiness_factor) / (frequency + 2.0)`. Items with recent clustered accesses (high burstiness_factor) receive proportionally lower eviction scores, preserving the current threshold values while weighting against recently bursty access patterns.

Keep all other logic (admission gate threshold, sketch parameters, counter saturation) unchanged.

## Rationale

Access burstiness captures temporal clustering—when an item is accessed repeatedly in a short window, it signals immediate working-set relevance independent of absolute frequency count. The formula weighs age higher for bursty items (lower eviction score), recognizing that temporal proximity is a distinct signal from long-term frequency. This is grounded in the pattern from prior cycles that frequency and age are orthogonal signals; burstiness adds a third orthogonal dimension (access clustering) that frequency and age do not capture alone.
