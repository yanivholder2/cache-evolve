# H02

Promoting a hit item by a bounded step of `max(1, max_size/8)` slots toward the MRU end, instead of jumping it straight to the tail, builds a frequency gradient inside each bucket and raises MSR hit-rate.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Edit only `src/shared/eviction_policy.rs`, changing the `ClassicLRU` impl of
`EvictionPolicy::get_from_position`. This is the policy the simulator selects.
`name()`, `eviction_candidate_index` (still `0`), and
`promotion_candidate_index` (still `len - 1`) stay unchanged.

New behaviour of `get_from_position(items, position)`:
- Let `len = items.len()` and `step = max(1, len / 8)`.
- Compute `target = min(len - 1, position + step)`.
- If `target == position`, return `position`.
- Otherwise move the item with `items[position..=target].rotate_left(1)`. This
  shifts the item to `target` and slides the items in between down by one, so
  their relative order is kept and nothing is removed or reallocated.
- Return `target`.

Items still enter at the back (MRU) through the unchanged `Bucket::put`.
Eviction still takes index 0. A key therefore needs repeated hits to climb
back to the top after ageing. One-hit items drift to the front and are evicted
first. Items with many hits stay near the tail even after a burst of new
insertions. This is a graded, Swap-like/CLOCK-Pro-flavoured movement, between
ClassicLRU (one jump to MRU) and Swap (one slot per hit).

Invariants: the item set and length are unchanged by a get, there are no
duplicates, and the returned index points at the accessed key.

Tests in `src/shared/traits/bucket.rs` (for example
`classic_lru_get_moves_to_back`, `classic_lru_evicts_least_recently_used`) may
assert the old order on tiny buckets. With `len <= 8`, `step = 1`, which
changes the order. If needed, use `step = len` (full jump to the tail) when
`len <= 8`. That keeps the old behaviour on small buckets, and the new
behaviour only applies to the real 128- and 1024-slot buckets.

## Rationale (optional)

The memmove also gets shorter: it is at most `len/8` elements, compared with up
to `len` for `remove`+`push`. A small per-op-time gain is a possible side
effect, but the target dimension is hit-rate.
