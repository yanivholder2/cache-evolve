# H02

Switching the count-min sketch to conservative update will raise hit-rate, mainly on web_0 at both sizes, because less overestimation from collisions makes the coldest-of-4 victim choice and the candidate-vs-victim contest more accurate.

## Parent

cycle/0005/v003

## Changes

Change only `CountMinSketch::increment`. Its geometry stays the same: depth 4, width `capacity*4` rounded up to a power of two, `u32` counters, no cap. `estimate`, `position`, `heap_bytes`, every climber, `sampled_main_victim` (sample of 4) and the strict `>` admission all stay as they are. Keep the double counting on a miss followed by `put`.

The new `increment` works in two passes:
1. Compute the `SKETCH_DEPTH` row positions once, storing them in a fixed-size array `[usize; SKETCH_DEPTH]`, and read the counters at those positions. Let `m` be the minimum of those counter values.
2. For each row whose counter equals `m`, increment that counter (saturating at `MAX_COUNT`). Leave rows whose counter is already greater than `m` unchanged.

This is the standard conservative-update (minimal increment) rule. Estimates still never under-count (`estimate` stays the min over rows), but a key that collides with a hot key no longer inherits that key's inflated rows. Add a unit test: on a narrow sketch (width 16), increment key A 100 times and key B once. Assert that `estimate(B)` is at most what plain update would give and still at least 1. Also assert that `estimate(A) >= 100`. All existing tests must keep passing.

## Rationale (optional)

The cross-cycle insights ask whether a less noisy victim estimate would sharpen admission better than a wider minimum. Cycle 0006 v002 showed that a minimum over 8 noisy estimates picks residents that only *look* cold. Here the sample stays at 4 and the noise is reduced instead. Cycle 0005 v001's gain on web_0 small came partly from less collision noise in the small sketch, which supports this route. The change does not lower the admission bar the way single counting did, so it should not cost web_0 large its newcomer advantage. Metadata is the same as the parent, and the extra per-op cost is a few compares.
