# H03

Aging the count-min sketch by halving all counters every 10×capacity increments will raise hit rate by letting newly popular keys win admission contests against residents whose high counts are stale.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

In `src/tiny_lfu/mod.rs`, add aging to `CountMinSketch`:

- New fields: `additions: usize` and `sample_size: usize`. Construct with `sample_size = 10 * capacity` (pass `capacity` into `CountMinSketch::new(width, capacity)` from `TinyLfuCache::try_new`; clamp to at least `width` so tiny caches do not reset every few ops).
- In `increment`, after bumping the rows, do `self.additions += 1`; when `self.additions >= self.sample_size`, call a new `reset()` that right-shifts every counter in every row by 1 (`*c >>= 1`) and sets `self.additions /= 2`.
- Optionally (keep it within this hypothesis) cap counters at 15 instead of `u32::MAX` (`MAX_COUNT = 15`) so a single hot key cannot accumulate an estimate that survives many halvings; the storage type stays `u32`, so `heap_bytes()` and the metadata-size dimension are unchanged.

Do not touch the recency list, the admission contest comparison in `put`, or the residency logic — the only movement change is *which* keys win contests, driven by fresher estimates. Preserve: increment on every get/put before residency change; `estimate` remains the row minimum; `sketch_heap_bytes()` still reports `depth * width * 4`. Unit tests use tiny capacities where no reset fires within the test (sample_size ≥ width ≥ 16), so they should pass unchanged.

## Rationale (optional)

The baseline sketch never decays: over the long MSR traces, keys that were hot early accumulate counts that make them unbeatable victims, freezing the resident set (cache pollution by history). Periodic halving (the TinyLFU "reset" operation) bounds the effective window of frequency to recent history, improving adaptation to working-set shifts with O(width·depth) work amortised over 10×capacity ops, so per-op time should be essentially unchanged.
