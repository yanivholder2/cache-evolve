# H03

Raising LIRS's resident-HIR share from 1% to a size-scaled 5–10% and enlarging its non-resident history bound will raise mean hit-rate above v002 by keeping LIRS's lead on hm_0 while closing its web_0 gap.

## Parent

cycle/0002/v003

## Changes

Keep v003's LIRS mechanism (stack S, resident-HIR queue Q, non-resident FIFO N, status transitions, stack pruning, and the free list) unchanged. Change only the sizing parameters set in `try_new` and how the non-resident bound is enforced:

1. **HIR share.** Replace `hir_cap = capacity / 100` (the `lir_cap = capacity - hir_cap` split) with a share that is larger at small capacity: `hir_cap = if capacity / 10 <= 2048 { max(1, capacity / 10) } else { max(2048, capacity / 20) }`. That gives 1638 (10%) at 16384 and 6553 (5%) at 131072. Keep `lir_cap = capacity - hir_cap` and require `lir_cap >= 1`. For `capacity < 2`, keep v003's existing degenerate handling.
2. **Non-resident bound.** Change `nonres_limit` from `2 * capacity` to `3 * capacity`. Leave the eviction of the oldest non-resident entry from N (and its removal from S and `map`) as v003 does it, and keep it O(1).
3. **Correctness check, no behaviour change intended.** Confirm that v003's miss path follows the paper. A non-resident HIR key that is still in S becomes LIR on re-reference, and the bottom LIR is demoted to Q. A key that is no longer in S (pruned or bounded out of N) enters as resident HIR at the tail of Q and the top of S. If v003 deviates from this, make it match. Do not add any other mechanism, so the experiment isolates the sizing change.
4. **Tests.** Update any unit test that hard-codes the 1% split or the `2 * capacity` bound, and recompute the expected victims under the new `hir_cap`. Add a test asserting `hir_cap == 1638` for capacity 16384 and `hir_cap == 6553` for capacity 131072, and that resident count never exceeds `capacity` across a 100k-op random workload on capacity 1000.

## Rationale (optional)

v003 already beat v002 on hm_0 at both sizes (0.474 vs 0.451 small, 0.743 vs 0.740 large). It lost on web_0, mostly at the large size (0.554 vs 0.584). Its insight blames the fixed 1% HIR queue as too narrow a probation window. The cross-cycle notes allow a return to LIRS only as a tuned-HIR test run directly against the current best, and this is that test. Its outcome also shows whether a front-segment share around 5–10% is the right magnitude, which can inform H01's climber bounds.
