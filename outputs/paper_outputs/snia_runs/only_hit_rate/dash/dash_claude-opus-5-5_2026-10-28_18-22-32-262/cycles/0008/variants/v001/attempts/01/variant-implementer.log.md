**What changed:**
1. **Reference bit.** In target and probing buckets, `lfu_counter` now works as a 1-bit "hit since placed" flag. In the stash it is still a hit count.
   - **Set to 0** whenever an item enters a normal bucket: stash-to-target promotions and rescues already did this, and `promote_to_probing` (before `put_at`) and the foreign-resident retry in `demote_from_normal_bucket` now do too.

2. **Victim choice.** I added `NORMAL_VICTIM_SECOND_CHANCE = true` and `make_room_clock`. It only acts when the bucket is full. It scans from the least-recently-used end, clears the bit on each referenced item it passes, and demotes the first unreferenced item through the same path an LRU eviction takes. If every item was referenced, it removes nothing (all bits are now clear) and the usual eviction of the least-recently-used item happens.
3. **Where it runs.** It is called at the top of `promote_to_target` (target bucket) and `promote_to_probing` (probing bucket). The rescue and the foreign-resident retry don't use it.
4. **Tests.** I added `clock_spares_referenced_lru_resident` and `clock_falls_back_to_lru_when_all_referenced`. I also added bit checks to three existing tests (target non-hottest hit, probing hit, foreign retry). No existing expected positions needed changing, because every pre-filled resident starts at bit 0.

**One departure from step 4:** the hypothesis says nothing can refill the bucket after `make_room_clock` removes an item, but that is wrong. If the demoted item overflows its stash bucket, the stash's victim can be rescued into the slot just freed, since rescue fills any free slot of the victim's own target or probing bucket. An assert after that step would fire in debug builds. So I put the `debug_assert!` (bucket not full) straight after the removal, before the demotion runs. If a rescue does refill the slot, the following `put_at` evicts the least-recently-used item as before. The promoted key is placed after that cascade has finished, so it is still guaranteed to end up in target or probing.
