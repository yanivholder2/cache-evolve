**File:** `overlay/src/dash/segments/dash_segment.rs`. This is the parent v003's segment file with one arm changed.

**What changed:** when a key gets its second hottest hit in its target bucket and the probing bucket is full, a new `swap_into_full_probing` function handles it:
- The hot key is removed from its target slot and its counter resets to 0. v003's guard is unchanged.
- The probing bucket's least-recently-used entry is removed, and the hot key goes into probing at the usual position.
- If the removed entry's key belongs to this target bucket, it moves straight into the freed target slot at the target's least-recently-used end, with its counter reset to 0. Only two buckets are touched, nothing goes to stash, and the item count stays the same.
- Promotion into a non-full probing bucket, the stash→target path, put and eviction are all unchanged.

**Where I departed from the hypothesis:**
1. **Entries that can't legally move to the target.** If the probing least-recently-used entry's key belongs to the probing bucket itself, the subject's slot-legality invariant doesn't allow it in the target bucket. Without a fallback the invariant gate would fail. In that case the entry goes to stash with its counter halved, which is where v003 already sent such entries.
2. **Which end of the target.** The hypothesis says to place the moved entry at "the target's LRU end (`Back`)". With the ClassicLRU policy, `Back` is the most-recently-used end and `Front` (index 0) is the least-recently-used end. I followed the stated intent ("the next target victim") and inserted at `Front`.

**This may not change much.** In v003, the guarded promotion had already removed the hot key from the target before evicting from probing. So a moved-back probing entry always landed in a target with a free slot, and the target-LRU → stash step never actually ran on this path. In behaviour, the swap is close to what v003 already did. The real differences are:
- the moved entry's counter now resets to 0;
- the code works directly on the two buckets instead of the generic cascade, which skips repeated hashing, the key-exists scan and the lookup after insert.

Any drop in get p99 will probably be small. The hit-rate change should come only from the counter reset.
