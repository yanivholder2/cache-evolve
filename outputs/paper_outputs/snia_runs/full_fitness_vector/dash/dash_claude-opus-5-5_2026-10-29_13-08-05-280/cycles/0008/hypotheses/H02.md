# H02

Double cycle 0007 v003's `PROBE_WINDOW` to twice the segment slot count so slow-recurring hot keys in small caches still complete their full-probing promotion, recovering hm_0-16384 hit-rate and raising overall hit-rate above 0.3952.

## Parent

cycle/0007/v003

## Changes

In cycle/0007/v003, change only the value of `PROBE_WINDOW`, the per-segment `u32` tick-age bound used in the `is_hottest` / probing-bucket-`is_full()` arm of the target-hit path. Today it equals the segment's total slot count (all buckets plus stash). Set it to `2 * total_slot_count` (computed once at segment construction, or as a constant if the slot count is compile-time; use `saturating_mul` or an equivalent that cannot overflow `u32`).

The comparison stays `age = seg_tick.wrapping_sub(item.timestamp); age <= PROBE_WINDOW`. Do not change the first-hit branch, the stale-hit reset branch, the swap mechanics, the non-hottest arm (which must still not touch `lfu_counter` or `timestamp`), the stash→target guard, put, eviction, how the per-segment tick is bumped (on insert only), or the 24-byte Item.

## Rationale

v003's insight attributes its hm_0 dip (0.3518 vs 0.3520 at 16384, 0.4432 vs 0.4440 at 131072) to the window being short in tick terms, so genuinely recurring keys get their swap reset and stay in target. A wider window lets more of those swaps through while still filtering the very slow recurrences. This is a single-parameter test of the window's hit-rate/latency trade-off on the swap lineage, distinct from changing the arm's mechanics. Confirmation: hm_0 cells return to ≥ 0.3520 / ≥ 0.4440 and summary hit-rate > 0.395225 with get p99 ≤ ~152 ns. Refutation: hit-rate unchanged (the window is not what is costing hm_0) or get p99 rising back toward cycle 0006 v001's 184 ns with no hit-rate gain (the window only acts as a throttle).
