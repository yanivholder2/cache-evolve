  - **Target bucket:** it finds the item the bucket's own eviction policy would evict. It then rotates the slots between that victim and the insert end by one: `rotate_right` over `[..=e]` for `Front`, `rotate_left` over `[e..]` for `Back`. This leaves the victim at the insert slot, in the same order as `remove(e)` followed by `insert(0)` or `push`.
  - **Stash bucket:** it rotates `[item_idx..)` left by one, so the promoted item ends up at stash MRU.
  - **Fused case:** when the victim belongs to this target bucket and hashes to the same stash bucket, one `mem::swap` exchanges the two slots. Both items get `lfu_counter = 0`, as in the old chain. Neither bucket changes length, and no stash eviction is possible.
  - **Fallback case:** the hypothesis assumed both items share a stash bucket, which isn't always true. When the victim belongs to a different target bucket (it was sitting there as that key's probing overflow) or maps to a different stash bucket, the target bucket still gets the single in-place replace. The promoted item is removed from stash, and the victim goes through the existing `demote_from_normal_bucket`. The final state is still identical to the old chain.
