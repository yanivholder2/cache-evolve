# H01

Refine MRU insertion asymmetry by applying back insertion to demotion paths only, preserving v003's proven demotion recovery while testing whether promotion-path back insertion (the new part of v001) is the source of interference.

## Parent

cycle/0004/v001

## Changes

Modify the `promotion_get()` function in `src/dash/segments/dash_segment.rs` to test whether back insertion belongs in demotion paths only.

**For demotion**: Preserve the back-insertion logic from v003 for all demotion paths (target→stash, probing→target→stash cascades). Demoted items continue to use `InsertPosition::Back` to maintain promotion eligibility after surviving tier discipline.

**For promotion**: Change promotion insertions to use `InsertPosition::Front` (LRU) instead of the universal back insertion attempted in v001. Specifically:
1. In `promotion_get()`, when promoting stash hits to target (line ~165), use `InsertPosition::Front` 
2. When promoting hottest target items to probing (line ~177), use `InsertPosition::Front`

This isolates the working part of the asymmetry (demotion recovery) from the part that may be interfering (promotion-path MRU insertion). The hypothesis tests whether v001's saturation at 0.684 is due to back insertion on promotion paths over-accelerating items through tiers.

## Rationale

Cycle 0004 v001 revealed that applying back insertion universally (both promotion and demotion) saturated at 0.684 instead of improving. The cycle insight questions whether the MRU mechanism's asymmetric behavior is rooted in insertion position or in candidacy timing. By reverting promotion paths to Front insertion while preserving demotion-path back insertion, this variant isolates which direction of movement benefits from MRU treatment. If hit rate remains at 0.684, it confirms that demotion recovery (not promotion acceleration) is the mechanism; if it drops, it confirms that both paths contribute equally and the saturation is due to a different cause.
