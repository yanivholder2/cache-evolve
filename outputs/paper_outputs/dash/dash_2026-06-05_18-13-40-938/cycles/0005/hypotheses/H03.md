# H03

Test a fundamentally different lever: occupancy-based promotion gating. Gate stash-to-target promotion on stash occupancy, allowing items to mature in the stash tier when occupancy is high, addressing the filtering discipline problem identified in cycle 0004 v003.

## Changes

Modify the stash-hit path in `promotion_get()` in `src/dash/segments/dash_segment.rs` to implement occupancy-aware promotion gating.

**Occupancy threshold check**: When a stash hit is detected (line ~155), before promoting to target, check the stash bucket's current occupancy ratio relative to its capacity.

**Conditional promotion logic**:
1. If stash occupancy is below 70%: promote the item to target with `InsertPosition::Front` (baseline behavior), allowing natural tier progression
2. If stash occupancy is at or above 70%: reposition the item to Back (MRU) within the stash bucket and return without promoting, keeping it in the stash tier

**Rationale for the threshold**: At high occupancy, the stash is filtering effectively—items that survive 70%+ occupancy have proven value. Promoting them immediately risks pushing marginal items into target/probing. By keeping high-occupancy survivors in stash, we extend their proving period and reduce promotion of unproven items.

**Preserve all other paths**: This change affects only the stash-hit promotion decision. All other tier transitions (target→probing, demotion cascades) follow baseline behavior.

## Rationale

Cycle 0004 v003 failed when combining tier-skip with back-insertion demotion, revealing that "tier-skipping mechanism assumes a filtering discipline that back-insertion undermines by giving demoted items a shortcut." This suggests the filtering discipline—allowing items to survive tier transitions and prove themselves—is the real lever, not insertion position alone. By gating promotion on occupancy, we enforce filtering: items accessed during high-occupancy periods stay in stash longer, while low-occupancy periods allow free tier progression. This tests whether the local optimum at 0.684 is constrained by insufficient filtering, not by insertion position conflicts.
