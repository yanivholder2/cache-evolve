# H02

Investigate promotion candidacy asymmetry by delaying promotion eligibility for newly promoted items, requiring them to prove fitness at their new tier before becoming candidates for further promotion.

## Parent

cycle/0004/v001

## Changes

Extend the MRU asymmetry investigation with an eligibility-delay mechanism in `src/dash/segments/dash_segment.rs`.

**Track promotion recency**: Add a promotion-access counter to items (could use existing metadata or a parallel tracking structure). When an item is promoted to a new tier (target or probing), mark it with promotion-age = 0.

**Gate further promotion**: In `promotion_get()`, modify the hottest-item promotion logic (target→probing) to skip promotion of items with promotion-age < 1 (i.e., items promoted to target in the immediately preceding access cycle). These items must be accessed again at their new tier before competing for further promotion.

**Preserve demotion**: Demoted items land at Back (from v001's parent v003) and immediately have promotion-age = 1, preserving their re-entry opportunity. This differentiates demoted items (which have proven history) from freshly promoted items (which are unproven at the new tier).

**Specific implementation**: 
1. Keep v001's demotion-path back insertion intact
2. When promoting from stash→target or target→probing, record the promotion timestamp or access epoch
3. In promotion-candidate selection for target→probing, exclude items promoted fewer than 1 access-cycle ago
4. This creates a "prove-yourself" window at each tier for newly promoted items, without affecting demoted items' rapid re-entry

## Rationale

The cycle 0004 insight asks whether the promotion-path MRU problem is "eviction cadence, promotion candidacy eligibility, or something else." This variant directly tests the eligibility hypothesis: is the asymmetry caused by items being too quickly eligible for further promotion after arriving at a tier? By making newly promoted items ineligible for one access cycle while keeping demoted items eligible, this separates the two populations and tests whether enforced residency windows resolve the asymmetry without blocking demotion recovery.
