# H02

Displacing an occupancy-aware "settling" victim instead of the blind LRU at each full cascade hop reduces terminal drops and raises hit-rate over v003's cascade.

## Parent

cycle/0007/v003

## Changes

v003's cascade displaces the **LRU resident** of every full alternate bucket
and carries it onward; that displaced resident often has no free home either,
so the cascade burns its hop budget chasing dead ends and still drops a key at
the end. The cross-cycle open question asks explicitly whether smarter-than-LRU
victim selection deepens the seam. This hypothesis answers it by changing only
the victim-choice rule inside the existing cascade.

In `stash_insert`'s cascade loop, when the target alternate `dest` bucket is
full, do not blindly displace its LRU. Instead pick a **settling victim**: scan
`dest`'s residents (via the bucket's `get_items`) and prefer to displace one
whose own `other_stash_home(resident.key, dest, stash_size)` is a *distinct,
non-full* bucket — i.e. a resident that will relocate cleanly in a single
further hop and cannot trigger another drop. Remove that chosen resident (by
position) to seat the homeless item at `InsertPosition::Back`, then carry the
chosen settling victim onward as the new homeless item. If no resident has a
free second home, fall back to v003's current behaviour (displace the LRU /
`promotion_candidate`-opposite resident).

Keep `RELOCATION_MAX_HOPS = 3`, the get-path both-homes search, all promotion
and demotion logic, and the occupancy-conservation invariant (still exactly one
homeless item, at most one drop per `put`) unchanged. The only diff is which
resident the cascade evicts at a full bucket.

## Rationale (optional)

Depth (H01) widens the search; this widens the *quality* of each step —
displacing residents that resolve cleanly should let more of the hop budget end
in a seat rather than a drop, isolating victim-selection as an independent lever
from raw cascade depth.
