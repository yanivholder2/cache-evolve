# Cross-Cycle Insights

## Recurring wins

- **Stash-hit acceleration as a fitness signal**: Cycles 0001–0009 converge on treating stash residents as proven hot items worthy of fast-tracking. Early cycles (0001–0003) tested variants of stash-hit promotion through multi-tier hierarchies; cycles 0008–0009 confirmed that *direct* stash-to-hot promotion (eliminating intermediate tier gatekeeping) is superior to all filtered paths. Treating stash survival as a qualification signal has never degraded hit-rate.

- **MRU demotion back-insertion as asymmetric recovery**: Cycles 0003–0009 consistently show that inserting demoted items at MRU (back of queue) improves hit-rate compared to LRU insertion. Demoted items—those that previously climbed the hierarchy—benefit from immediate re-promotion eligibility. This pattern held even when other mechanisms (promotion paths, occupancy gating, tier-skip) were varied. By contrast, fresh promotion paths do not benefit from MRU insertion; the asymmetry suggests demoted items have proven tier-advancement history that justifies preferential treatment.

- **Architectural simplification over parametric tuning**: Cycles 0002–0007 exhausted insertion position, occupancy gating, eviction priority, cascade structure, and capacity allocation without exceeding 0.684 baseline. Cycle 0008's breakthrough—collapsing three-tier hot-side to unified two-tier architecture—was the first improvement in four cycles. This validates that the bottleneck was structural topology, not parameter values. Cycles 0008–0009 confirm that additional conditional logic (occupancy-gated tier-skip, stricter promotion thresholds) on the simplified architecture adds no value, indicating the two-tier structure itself is near-optimal.

- **Demotion cascades as mandatory safety valve**: Every successful variant preserves multi-step demotion paths. Cycle 0007 v002's attempt to bypass the target-tier retry step for foreign residents (hash collisions, overflow) degraded hit-rate by ~3%, confirming that a "second chance" at target before final demotion is load-bearing. The cascade recovers borderline items that lack stash pedigree, unlocking value without creating churn.

## Recurring failures

- **Occupancy-based gating without fitness coupling**: Cycles 0002 (v003), 0005 (v003), 0006 (v003 expansion), 0008 (v001), and 0009 (v002) all tested occupancy thresholds—promoting selectively when stash > X% or deferring promotion when tiers > Y% full. None improved hit-rate; several degraded it. The pattern is consistent: occupancy alone is not a fitness signal. Occupancy-aware routing that defers acceleration of stash hits during congestion appears to delay identification of genuinely hot items rather than improve discipline. Occupancy becomes relevant only when paired with strong fitness signals (stash survival in cycles 0002 v002's success), not as a standalone gate.

- **Frequency-based eviction on recency-dominant workloads**: Cycle 0001 v002's LFU policy showed no improvement despite correct implementation, validating that temporal locality (recent = reused soon) dominates access count. This workload's recency-heavy pattern makes frequency-based schemes misaligned.

- **Tier-skip without structured recovery**: Cycles 0004 v003 and 0006 v003 showed that direct stash-to-probing bypass (skipping intermediate target filtering) conflicted with demotion back-insertion discipline, flooding hot tiers with unvetted stash hits that lack tier-advancement proof. The conflict proved structural until cycle 0008 v003 introduced mandatory recovery enforcement: items skipped to hot must re-enter lower tiers on eviction, restoring the "second chance" principle. Before recovery structure was added, skip was considered "fundamentally incompatible"; after, it works.

- **Composite strategies that interfere**: Cycle 0004 tested combining stash-hit acceleration, MRU positioning, and tier-skip—all individually successful in prior cycles—and found they degraded hit-rate. Individual mechanisms that work in isolation can create conflicting tier-occupation dynamics or eviction cadence when combined. This led to the observation that simple, singular strategies outperform composite approaches on this workload, a principle that held until cycle 0008's architectural pivot revealed that *architectural simplification* (removing tiers entirely) is orthogonal to *parameter composition* (stacking insertion rules on the same tier structure).

- **Parametric tuning beyond architectural plateaus**: Cycles 0002–0007 all failed to exceed 0.684 despite testing insertion position (Front/Back/MRU), promotion candidacy timing, occupancy thresholds, tier capacity ratios, eviction priority, and cascade structure across independent levers. This plateau defended across six orthogonal dimensions indicates the constraint was not parametric but topological. Only cycle 0008's shift to architectural restructuring (three-tier → two-tier) broke through, validating that further parametric refinement was exhausted.

## Open questions

- **Is the two-tier unified hot tier the true optimum, or can further architectural simplification unlock gains?** Cycle 0009 v002–v003 show that overlay logic (occupancy-gated tier-skip, stricter promotion thresholds) on the two-tier baseline adds no value, suggesting architectural equilibrium. But alternatives like single-tier caches, alternative tier counts, bucket saturation thresholds, or non-LRU eviction orderings remain untested.

- **What is the precise mechanism defending the three-tier hierarchy's bottleneck?** Cycle 0008 identified the three-tier hot-side as a bottleneck, but the exact constraint is unclear: is it eviction cascade timing, tier occupancy balance, bucket saturation interactions, or asymmetric promotion flow between tiers? Understanding this could clarify whether the two-tier simplification addresses the root cause or merely sidesteps it.

- **Does the workload's access distribution naturally suit direct stash-to-hot promotion, or is simplification itself the driver?** Cycles 0008–0009 show that unified two-tier architecture with direct promotion outperforms multi-tier filtering. Whether this is workload-specific (access pattern matches two-tier dynamics) or architecture-specific (simplification always wins) remains unresolved.

- **Are alternative eviction orderings, stash depth variations, or bucket saturation triggers unexplored levers?** Cycles focused on insertion position, occupancy routing, and tier topology, but eviction orderings beyond LRU/MRU, stash filtering window depth (beyond 4–6 buckets tested in 0003, 0006), and bucket saturation thresholds remain largely untested as primary levers.

## Per-policy notes

- **MRU back-insertion (demotion recovery)**: Asymmetric benefit—improves hit-rate for demoted items (proven tier-advancement history) but does not help fresh promotion. Remains the only demotion-path mechanism that consistently succeeds across cycles 0003–0009. Its asymmetry against fresh promotion suggests eviction cadence or promotion candidacy eligibility differs between recovery and forward paths, but the exact mechanism is unresolved.

- **Direct stash-to-hot promotion (tier-skip with recovery)**: Cycles 0001–0002 showed promise; cycles 0004–0007 rejected it as incompatible with demotion discipline. Cycle 0008 v003 reconciled the conflict by enforcing mandatory target re-entry on eviction (recovery flag), proving skip is viable when recovery structure is built into the skip decision, not bolted on afterward. The two-tier unified architecture (cycle 0008–0009) supersedes conditional tier-skip, suggesting simplicity outweighs conditional acceleration.

- **Occupancy-based routing (occupancy thresholds, congestion-aware gating)**: Consistently failed or flat across all cycle variations. Tested in cycles 0002, 0005, 0006, 0008–0009 as occupancy-gated promotion, occupancy-gated demotion, occupancy-aware destination selection, and occupancy-filtered tier-skip. None improved hit-rate; several degraded it. Pattern: occupancy is not a fitness signal and does not improve hit-rate absent strong fitness coupling (e.g., stash-hit survival). Occupancy-aware mechanisms appear to be implementation overhead without discovery of latent utility.

- **Three-tier hot-side hierarchy (target tier filtering)**: Identified in cycle 0008 as an architectural bottleneck. Replacing it with unified two-tier (Stash + unified Hot, 28 buckets allocated to Hot) broke through the 0.684 ceiling. The three-tier structure's intermediate gatekeeping imposed filtering discipline that the workload does not require; direct stash-to-hot access is near-optimal for this access pattern. This represents a shift from treating tiers as filtering stages to treating them as occupancy buffers.

- **Demotion cascade structure (safety-valve retry step)**: Cycle 0007 v002's removal of the target-tier retry step for foreign residents degraded hit-rate by ~3%. The cascade's "second chance" mechanism is mandatory for recovery of borderline items. Attempts to simplify cascades (cycles 0007) have consistently failed, suggesting the structure is well-balanced and inert to further optimization.
