# H03

Push bucket allocation to 75/25 geometry to maximize hash-indexed capacity and test asymptote of geometry concentration.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

In `src/shared/geometry.rs`, modify the `BaselineGeometry::for_settings()` function to allocate buckets even more aggressively toward the normal tier:

- Change the normal bucket allocation to `num_of_normal_buckets = (total_buckets * 3) / 4` (3/4 = 75%)
- Change stash allocation to `num_of_stash_buckets = total_buckets - num_of_normal_buckets`
- Clamp `num_of_normal_buckets` to at least 2 (required for probing) using `.max(2)` to ensure structural validity on small bucket counts

For baseline's total of 32 buckets, this yields 24 normal + 8 stash (75%/25%), pushing further from v002's 50/50 and v001's 62.5/37.5.

Keep `bucket_size` and `segment_size` unchanged. The total capacity remains constant; capacity is reallocated from the stash to the hash-indexed normal tier.

## Rationale

Cycle 0002's insight confirms geometry allocation remains the primary lever with no observed saturation: baseline (25/75) → 50/50 (v002 cycle 1) → 62.5/37.5 (v001 cycle 2) all showed improvements. But v001's hit-rate (0.6991) was slightly lower than v002's (0.7078 with back-insert), suggesting diminishing returns or a composition effect. This hypothesis directly tests whether further geometry concentration continues to yield gains or if 62.5/37.5 represents the asymptote. Pushing to 75/25 sacrifices stash capacity (8 of 32 buckets) in exchange for more hash-indexed slots, which on Zipfian traces should catch cold misses in the primary tier more reliably. The tradeoff is higher demotions for items that overflow from the smaller stash, but the hot-set dominance in Zipfian distributions suggests stash size matters less than primary-tier hit efficiency.
