# H02

Admitting each cold key to the less-full of its two stash homes (power-of-two-choices) raises hit-rate by structurally lowering stash collision pressure so more cold keys survive to a second reference.

## Parent

cycle/0006/v002

## Changes

v002 always inserts the incoming cold key into its *primary* stash bucket and
only relocates the resulting overflow *victim* into the alternate bucket — so
the gain is throttled by the primary bucket filling up while the alternate sits
with spare room. Change `stash_insert` in
`src/dash/segments/dash_segment.rs` to balance load across both homes from the
moment of admission:

- Compute `primary = get_index(&item.key, core.stash_size)` and
  `alt = stash_alt_index(&item.key, core.stash_size)`.
- When `alt != primary`, choose the destination bucket as the one with more
  free room (`get_max_size() - size()`), breaking ties toward `primary` so
  behaviour is deterministic and collapses to v002 when both are equally full.
  Insert the item there at `pos`.
- Keep the existing one-hop overflow relocation as the fallback: if the chosen
  bucket was full and evicts a victim, relocate that victim into *its* other
  home (the home not just used) at `InsertPosition::Back` when that bucket has
  room, else drop it — same single-hop, capacity-conserving rule as today.
- When `alt == primary` (`stash_size < 2`), behave exactly as the baseline
  single-bucket insert.

Leave the get path, `contains_key`, and `check_slot_legality` unchanged — they
already accept either home as legal, so balanced admission needs no new
residency rules. Preserve all promotion/demotion logic from v002.

## Rationale (optional)

The cycle-0006 insight pinned the marginal relocation gain on "the alternate
stash bucket is usually full too." Power-of-two-choices attacks that root cause
from the opposite end: instead of rescuing victims after a collision, it avoids
the collision by keeping both homes evenly filled, raising average dwell time
across the whole cold-key population rather than for a rescued sliver.
