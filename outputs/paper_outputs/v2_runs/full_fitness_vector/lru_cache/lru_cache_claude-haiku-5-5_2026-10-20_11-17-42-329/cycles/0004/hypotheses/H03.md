# H03

Computing one hash per access and deriving both frequency-sketch rows from it makes the admission gate in cycle/0003/v003 cheap enough to keep its hit-rate gain without the per-access SipHash cost.

## Parent

cycle/0003/v003

## Changes

Keep the admission rule unchanged: at capacity, `put()` rejects a new key when its estimated frequency is below the LRU victim's estimate. Replace the per-call `DefaultHasher` use in `record()` and `estimate()` with a single 64-bit hash of the key per access, computed once at the top of `get()` and `put()` and passed down to `record()` and the admission check. Derive the two sketch row indices from that one hash by splitting its bits, instead of hashing with a row seed twice. Compute the victim's estimate once per eviction decision, not once per candidate. Use a cheap multiplicative mix for the single hash rather than SipHash, since the sketch only needs approximate frequency counts. Keep the sketch's two `Vec<u8>` rows, mask, and sample counters at their current size, and do not add fields to `Node`. The `get()` ordering and list behaviour must remain untouched, so hit-rate on workload-a at 2048 should stay close to the parent's 0.695, and the per-op-time regression on `get` and `put` should shrink.

## Rationale (optional)

The cycle 0003 insight says the admission filter gained the cohort's clearest hit-rate but paid for it on every access, through two SipHash calls in `record()` and two more `estimate()` hashes per candidate and victim. The cross-cycle insights say admission gating is worth keeping only if that hashing cost is removed. This hypothesis tests whether a single cheap hash keeps the hit-rate gain while bringing per-op cost near baseline.
