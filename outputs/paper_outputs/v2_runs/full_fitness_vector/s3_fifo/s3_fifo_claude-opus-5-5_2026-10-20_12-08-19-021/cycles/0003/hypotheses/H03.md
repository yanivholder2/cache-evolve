# H03

Replace v003's adaptive split with the paper's fixed 10/90 small/main split and its freq ≥ 1 graduation rule to test whether a static configuration beats the adaptive split's 0.7282 hit-rate.

## Parent

cycle/0002/v003

## Changes

All edits are in `src/s3_fifo/mod.rs`. Keep the hashed slab, `index`, and the stamped ghost from v003 exactly as they are. The point is to isolate the policy question on the fast, faithful base.

1. **Fixed split.** In `try_new`, compute `small_size = (capacity / 10).max(1).min(capacity - 1)` (keep `1` when `capacity == 1`), set `main_size = capacity - small_size`, and set `ghost_cap = main_size`. This follows Yang et al., who use a 10% small queue and a ghost sized to main. Make `adaptive()` return `false` unconditionally, or delete it together with every resize branch:
   - the ghost-hit `small_size -= 1; main_size += 1` block in `put()`, including its fix-up `evict_small_inner(false)` and `evict_main()` calls;
   - the `grow_small` block in `evict_small_inner`.

   The ghost-hit path then just evicts from main if main is full, allocates the entry, and pushes it to the front of main.

2. **Paper graduation rule.** In `evict_small_inner`, change the promotion test from `self.slot(i).freq > 1` to `self.slot(i).freq >= 1`: one access while in small is enough to graduate. This is the paper's rule. On graduation, reset the promoted entry's `freq` to 0 before `main.push_front(i)`, so main's second-chance counter starts fresh. The paper does the same.

3. **Keep everything else.** That means `MAX_FREQ = 3`, main second-chance eviction, and ghost insertion of small victims only. In-module tests: `small_eviction_promotes_to_main_after_multiple_hits` still passes because two hits satisfy `>= 1`. `adaptive_split_keeps_bounds` still holds because the sizes are constant. Add one test asserting that `small_size == capacity / 10` after a workload loop.

## Rationale (optional)

Two open questions from cycles 0001 and 0002 are still unanswered: is the adaptive gain real, or just a move away from a bad 25/75 starting split, and is the adaptation fighting the movement rules? This variant swaps both levers for the published static S3-FIFO configuration, so it differs from the incumbent's whole policy rather than from one movement rule layered on the adaptation, which has failed twice. If it matches or beats 0.7282, the adaptive machinery is unnecessary, and future cycles should tune fixed fractions and graduation instead. If it loses, the adaptation is earning its keep. Per-op-time should match v003 or improve slightly, since the resize branches are gone.
