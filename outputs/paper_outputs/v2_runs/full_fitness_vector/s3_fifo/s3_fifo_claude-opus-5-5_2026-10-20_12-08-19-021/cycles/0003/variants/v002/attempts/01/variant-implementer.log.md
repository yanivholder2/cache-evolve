The live-record counter used by the tests is kept. A fingerprint is the key's FxHash with the lowest bit forced to 1, so it is never 0. Keys are no longer cloned into the ghost.
- **Push.** Writing at the head overwrites the oldest slot and drops that record from the map. If the same fingerprint is already live elsewhere, its old slot is cleared so it isn't counted twice. The function that popped the oldest live record is deleted.
- **Lookup.** Finding a key in the ghost removes it from the map, clears its ring slot and lowers the live count.
- **`get`.** It looks up the slot index once and matches on that slot directly. `get` and `put`, including the trait methods, are `#[inline]`, with no `unsafe`.
- **Tests.** Updated as Change 5 asks: `adaptive_split_keeps_bounds` checks the live count against the cap and the map size, and `index_tracks_residency` checks fingerprints. The physical-bound check and its helper function are removed.

Adaptation triggers, clamps and the graduation rule (`freq > 1`) are untouched.

**Things to watch in evaluation:**
- **Hit-rate may not stay at 0.7282.** The two ghosts age records differently. v003 always kept up to `ghost_cap` *live* records. The ring evicts by *push age*, so a slot cleared by a ghost hit stays empty until the head comes back around to it. The ghost can therefore briefly hold fewer live records than v003's did. The hypothesis expects no change, so if hit-rate moves, this is the most likely cause. Re-pushed keys can't be double-counted.
- **Capacity 1.** The ghost cap is 0 there, so the ring gets one slot to avoid dividing by zero. That matches v003, whose ghost also ended up holding one record at that size.
