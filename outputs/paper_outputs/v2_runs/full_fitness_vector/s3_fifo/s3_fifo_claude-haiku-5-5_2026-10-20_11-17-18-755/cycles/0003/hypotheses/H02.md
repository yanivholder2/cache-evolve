# H02

Decaying the frequency counter of rotated main-queue entries during the bounded victim selection should raise hit rate by evicting stale high-frequency residents sooner, targeting hit-rate.

## Parent

cycle/0002/v002

## Changes

Start from cycle/0002/v002, which keeps the 8-step second-chance budget and selects the lowest-frequency entry from the bounded victim buffer once the budget is spent. Add frequency aging to that path. When the rotation loop passes over a main-queue entry that still has a nonzero frequency counter, decrement the counter by one before re-inserting it at the head, instead of leaving it unchanged. Keep the victim selection rule as v002 has it, except break frequency ties by picking the buffered entry nearest the tail. Do not change the small queue, the ghost queue, promotion thresholds, or `get()`. Metadata size must stay at the v002 layout (Entry 24 bytes). Keep the budget at 8 so the put path does no more rotations than v002 does.

## Rationale (optional)

v002's victim choice keeps recently referenced entries, but nothing lets an entry that was hot long ago lose its frequency, so it can stay resident past its usefulness. Decrementing on rotation is the standard aging step in frequency-based policies and is cheap, since the rotation already touches each entry. The hypothesis is that aging makes the post-budget victim choice more accurate without adding a new data structure. Unlike the promotion threshold tried in cycle 0001, it does not move keys between queues earlier.
