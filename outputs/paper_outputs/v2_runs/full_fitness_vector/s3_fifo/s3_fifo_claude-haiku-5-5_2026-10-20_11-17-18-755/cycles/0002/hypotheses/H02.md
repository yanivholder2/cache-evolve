# H02

Replace the tail-eviction fallback after the MAX_EVICT_SCAN budget with a minimum-frequency victim among the entries already scanned, to recover v003's hit-rate loss while keeping its bounded put cost.

## Parent

cycle/0001/v003

## Changes

Keep the `MAX_EVICT_SCAN` (8) second-chance budget in the main-queue eviction loop of v003 unchanged. The change is only to what happens when the budget is exhausted:

- During the scan, record each popped main-queue entry that has `freq > 0` (and is therefore rotated back to the front) in a small fixed-size buffer of at most `MAX_EVICT_SCAN` slots, along with its frequency and its position.
- When the budget is spent, instead of evicting the current tail unconditionally, select the buffered entry with the smallest `freq` (ties broken by the oldest position, i.e. the one closest to the tail) and evict that one. Re-insert the other buffered entries at the front of the main queue in their original order.
- If the scan finds an entry with `freq == 0` before the budget runs out, evict it immediately as in v003.
- Cost stays bounded: at most `MAX_EVICT_SCAN` rotations plus one linear pass over an 8-element buffer, with no allocation (use a stack array).
- Do not change `evict_small()`, the ghost structures, `get()` or `find_entry_mut()`, so the put-path gains of v003 are retained and the change is confined to victim selection.

Invariants to preserve: each put still performs at most one eviction; the main-queue length and the ordering of non-evicted entries are unchanged apart from the chosen victim being removed. Expected effect: fewer forced evictions of hot entries on workload-a at capacity 2048 relative to v003, with put p99 close to v003's.
