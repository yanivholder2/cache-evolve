# H02

Demoting the least-recent protected entry to the probation MRU when the protected segment is full, instead of capping it, will raise hit-rate above 0.7379 on workload-a while keeping metadata-size at 32 (hit-rate).

## Parent

cycle/0002/v002

## Changes

The parent has a probation/protected split with the protected flag packed into an existing `Node` field. Earlier cycles showed that capping the protected segment without demotion lost hit rate, and that the segmentation gain seems to need the protected set to keep turning over. This hypothesis tests the classic SLRU rule that keeps turnover: when a probation hit would promote an entry and the protected segment already holds its maximum number of entries, first demote the protected tail (least recent protected entry) to the probation MRU position, then promote the hit entry to the protected MRU position.

Concretely, in the promotion branch of `get()` (and of `put()` when it updates an existing probation entry), check a protected-length counter against a protected capacity set to about 80% of the cache capacity. If the counter is at capacity, unlink the protected tail, clear its protected bit, and relink it at the probation head, then continue with the normal promotion. Keep the protected-length counter as a single `usize` on `TinyLfuCache`; do not add fields to `Node`. Leave the packed flag accessors, the sketch, the admission gate, and the victim selection rule (evict from probation head first) unchanged.

Invariants to preserve: total resident count stays at capacity; the demoted entry is not evicted directly by the promotion; the protected bit and the protected-length counter stay consistent on every relink.

Accept only if hit-rate is strictly above 0.7379 on workload-a at size 2048, metadata-size stays at 32, and per-op-time does not rise above the parent's 98 ns by more than noise. Repeat the run at least three times before recording the result.

## Rationale (optional)

The segmentation split is the only change in the lineage that has moved hit rate upward. The open question from cycle 0003 is whether the gain depends on turnover, which the demotion rule provides and the cap-only variant lacked.
