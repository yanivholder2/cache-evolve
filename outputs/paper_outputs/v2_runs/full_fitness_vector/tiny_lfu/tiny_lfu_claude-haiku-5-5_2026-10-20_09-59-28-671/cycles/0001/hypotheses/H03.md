# H03

Splitting the resident list into a probation segment for new admissions and a protected segment for re-referenced keys raises hit rate on the lirs workload-a trace by evicting one-hit wonders before proven residents.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Restructure the resident-set ordering in `src/tiny_lfu/mod.rs` as a two-segment LRU, keeping the arena `nodes: Vec<Node<K, V>>` and the `map` as they are.

1. Add a `protected: bool` field to `Node`, and a second pair of list anchors to `TinyLfuCache`: `prot_head`/`prot_tail` and `prob_head`/`prob_tail`, plus a `prot_len: usize` counter. Set the protected capacity to `capacity * 4 / 5` (at least 1).
2. In `get()`, on a hit in the probation segment, unlink the node from probation and push it to the protected MRU. If protected is already at its limit, first demote the protected LRU (`prot_head`) to the probation MRU, then promote. A hit in protected moves the node to the protected MRU.
3. In `put()`, a new key enters the probation MRU. Insertion while `nodes.len() < capacity` follows the same path. In the full-cache branch, the victim is the probation head when probation is non-empty, otherwise the protected head. Keep the existing `incoming_freq > victim_freq` comparison. On admission, unlink the victim from its segment, reuse its slot, and insert the newcomer at the probation MRU with `protected = false`. On rejection, leave all lists unchanged.
4. Update path in `put()`: if the key is protected, treat it like a `get()` hit on protected. If it is probation, promote it as in step 2.
5. Keep `try_new` and `sketch_heap_bytes` signatures unchanged. Add the new fields only to the struct initializer in `try_new`. The sketch, `CountMinSketch`, and hashing stay as they are.

Expected effect: scan-like and one-hit keys are confined to probation and displace each other, while keys that have been hit at least once survive admission contests. The cost is extra pointer bookkeeping per hit and a few bytes of metadata per node, so the target dimension is hit-rate, with metadata-size and per-op-time as tracked costs.
