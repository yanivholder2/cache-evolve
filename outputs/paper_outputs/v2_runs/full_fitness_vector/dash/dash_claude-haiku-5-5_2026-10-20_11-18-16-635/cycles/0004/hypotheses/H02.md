# H02

Adding a small saturating access counter as a victim-selection bias on top of the cycle 0001 get-path reordering will raise hit-rate without giving back that variant's per-op gain, targeting hit-rate.

## Parent

cycle/0001/v002

## Changes

Keep the get-path reordering from cycle/0001/v002 (skip the stash scan on common target and probing hits) as it is. Add frequency-aware victim selection in `evict_one()`:

- Maintain a small saturating access counter per target-bucket slot, incremented on each target hit and capped at a low value (for example 3) so it stays within existing slot metadata where possible.
- When choosing a victim in `evict_one()`, prefer the slot with the lowest counter, breaking ties by the parent's existing order. Do not add a recency tie-break (cycle 0003's v001 showed it moves no hit rate).
- Do not change insert position. Promotions keep the parent's back-of-queue placement, since cycle 0002 showed front-of-bucket insertion on full buckets costs hit rate.
- Leave the stash scan and the get-path reordering logic untouched.

Measure on workload-a at size 2048. Success means hit-rate above the parent's 0.6787 with get p50 not worse than the parent's 76 ns. Because only workload-a has been measured, also record hit-rate on any second trace available, so the result is not credited on one workload alone.

## Rationale (optional)

Cycle 0002 v003's counter-based victim bias was the only change that reported a hit-rate gain without a latency cost driven by insertion. Cycle 0001 v002 is the cohort's clearest latency gain. Combining them tests whether the two effects stack, which no prior cycle has tried.
