# H03

Add a 2-bit access-frequency counter per slot and have `evict_one()` pick the lowest-frequency slot in the target bucket, so that hot residents survive displacement by one-shot inserts.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Change victim selection rather than insert position. This is the only hypothesis in this cohort that does not touch promotion or insertion paths.

- Add a 2-bit saturating access counter per slot in the segment's slot array. Increment it (saturating at 3) on each successful get that hits the slot. Reset it to 0 when a new key is written into the slot.
- In `evict_one()`, replace the uniform or LRU-only victim choice in the target bucket with a scan that selects the slot with the lowest counter, breaking ties by the existing LRU order. Leave the stash and probing victim logic unchanged.
- Decay: on each eviction call that scans the bucket, decrement the counters of the other slots in that bucket by 1 (floor 0), so that a formerly hot resident can eventually be displaced.
- Keep the demotion cascade and promotion logic exactly as in the baseline. Preserve slot ownership and bucket count invariants.

If the counter bits fit in existing padding of the slot struct, metadata-size should stay at 40. If they do not, metadata-size will increase, and that regression must be reported as a cost on that dimension rather than hidden.

## Rationale (optional)

v001's hit-rate loss came from a one-shot insert displacing a hot target resident. Rather than changing how keys enter, this hypothesis protects resident keys that have been accessed recently or repeatedly. It starts from the baseline so that its effect on hit rate is not confounded with the v002 or v003 changes.
