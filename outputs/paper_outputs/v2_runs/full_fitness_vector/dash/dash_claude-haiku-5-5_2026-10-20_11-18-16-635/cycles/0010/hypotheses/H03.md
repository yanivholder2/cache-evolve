# H03

Breaking ties among equal lowest counters in favour of evicting stash residents keeps hot target-bucket residents longer and raises hit rate on Zipfian traces.

## Parent

cycle/0007/v003

## Changes

In the full-scan victim selection in the eviction routine, keep the lowest-counter rule as the primary key. When two or more candidate slots share the minimum counter, prefer a stash-resident slot over a target-or-probe-resident slot. Use a single pass: track the best candidate as a pair (counter, is_target_resident) and replace it only when the counter is strictly lower, or equal and the candidate is stash-resident while the current best is target-resident. Do not sample, do not early-exit, and do not restrict the scan to the stash. Leave the counter increment, the insert-time value, the get path, and the stash-to-target promotion unchanged. Preserve the 32-byte record layout.

## Rationale (optional)

Cycle 0003 showed that recency tie-breaking added eviction cost without changing hit rate, so tie-breaking alone is not the lever. This hypothesis uses a different tie-break key: residency class rather than age. The insights say hot residents in the target bucket are the source of the retention gain, so preferring stash residents on ties should leave them in place. Expected signature: hit-rate above 0.6679 on workload-a with no change to metadata-size. Put cost may rise slightly because the scan carries one more comparison; report it, but it is not the success criterion. Source of the overlay could not be inspected while drafting, so the implementer should confirm how the stash and target slots are indexed before editing.
