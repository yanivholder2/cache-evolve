# H02

Porting the packed `Item<u64,u64>` record from cycle/0007/v003 onto the cycle 0004 v002 full-scan counter cuts metadata from 40 to 32 bytes while holding hit rate near 0.691.

## Parent

cycle/0004/v002

## Changes

Start from the cycle 0004 v002 overlay: full-scan lowest-counter victim choice in `evict_one()`, saturating access counter, no decay. This is the best hit rate on the leaderboard (0.691) but still uses the 40-byte record.

- Apply the record layout from cycle/0007/v003 so that the item record is `Item<u64,u64>` at 32 bytes instead of 40. Store the access counter in the packed record's spare capacity (or the packed `u8` slot), whichever v003 used.
- Keep the victim scan exactly as in the parent: full scan over the bucket, select the lowest-counter resident, same tie-break order.
- Keep the counter's read path on `get` and promotion as cheap as possible. Cycle 0006 v002 showed that packed-field decode on the get and promotion paths slowed both operations, so the counter should be read only inside `evict_one()` and on the existing hit increment, not decoded on any extra path.
- Do not add the deferred stash-to-target promotion from cycle 0004 v001; keep this change to the record layout and the counter.

Invariants: the record must still hold every field the parent stores. No change to insert position, stash handling, or the decay rule.

## Rationale

Cycle 0007 showed that layout and replacement are separable: v003 packed the record without changing metadata cost on the victim path, and cycle 0004 showed that frequency-aware eviction gives the only consistent hit-rate gain. Combining the two should keep the eviction order that gave 0.691 while reducing the one metadata dimension where the parent is still at 40. The hypothesis is confirmed if metadata-size reports 32, hit rate stays within a small margin of 0.691, and get and put stay within the parent's measured range (get 77 ns, put in the 100s). A regression in hit rate would show the packed counter loses information that the full scan needs.
