# H02

Halving the saturating access counters of a bucket after each eviction in cycle/0004/v002 stops stale hot residents from shielding slots, so hit rate rises on workload-a and holds on a second trace.

## Parent

cycle/0004/v002

## Changes

Keep the full lowest-counter scan in `evict_one()` as the victim choice. After the victim is selected and evicted, right-shift by one (halve) the access counter of every remaining slot in that bucket, so that counters decay each time the bucket evicts. Leave the counter increment on target hits as the parent has it. Do not change the stash-to-target promotion path, the get path, insert placement, or the record layout.

Invariants: the victim is still the minimum-counter slot under the parent's tie-break, so the only behaviour change is the decay. Report the hit rate on workload-a and on at least one non-workload-a trace, since the cycle 0005 and 0006 continuations asked for a second-trace check of full-scan frequency eviction and it has not been run.

## Rationale (optional)

The parent's counter only grows on hits, so a resident that was hot early can keep its slot long after it stops being used. Cycle 0002 and 0004 both pointed at protecting hot residents at eviction. Decay tests whether the parent's retention is too sticky, and it is the first hypothesis in this cohort that changes ranking rather than cost. It may lose hit rate, which would also be a useful result.
