# H02

Replacing ClassicLRU's move-to-back on hit with a two-step promotion (front-half hits move to the midpoint, back-half hits move to the back) raises hit-rate on workload-a by stopping one-touch scan items from displacing re-referenced items.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Overlay `src/shared/eviction_policy.rs`, changing only the `impl EvictionPolicy<K, V> for ClassicLRU` block. Keep `name()` returning `"ClassicLRU"`, keep `eviction_candidate_index()` returning `0`, and keep `promotion_candidate_index()` returning `items.len() - 1`.

Rewrite `get_from_position(&self, items: &mut Vec<Item<K, V>>, position: usize) -> usize` as:

- `let mid = items.len() / 2;`
- if `position < mid`: `let item = items.remove(position); items.insert(mid - 1, item); return mid - 1;` (promote to the midpoint rather than the back);
- otherwise: `let item = items.remove(position); items.push(item); return items.len() - 1;` (unchanged LRU behaviour for back-half hits).

Use the position returned by the function, since `AssociativeCacheBucket::get_from_position` indexes `items` with it. Do not add a new `EvictionPolicyKind` variant; that would change `all()`, `index()`, and the JNI ordinal mapping.

The existing unit test `classic_lru_get_moves_to_back` in `src/shared/traits/bucket.rs` expects a hit at position 0 of a 3-item bucket to land at the back. Under the new rule that hit lands at index 0 (front half, `mid - 1` after removal) and the test must be updated to the two-step expectation rather than deleted.

Expected trade-off: hit-rate should move on workload-a, since scan-like single-touch items are kept out of the protected back half. `get` and `put` cost should be essentially unchanged, because the change is one `remove` plus one `insert` or `push` on a 16-element `Vec`. `metadata-size` should be unchanged.

## Rationale (optional)

Midpoint or segmented promotion is the classic answer to LRU's scan pollution. This hypothesis keeps the eviction candidate and the cost profile of ClassicLRU and changes only where hits land, so any hit-rate movement is attributable to promotion placement.
