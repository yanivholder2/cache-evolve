# H02

Halving the bucket width from 32 slots to 16 shortens both the put-side victim scan and the get-side linear scan, lowering median put and get time at a small hit-rate cost.

## Parent

cycle/0008/v002

## Changes

Change the bucket geometry constant that sets slots per bucket from 32 to 16, and double the number of buckets so total capacity stays at the trace size of 2048 entries. Keep the total slot count, the item layout, and the `AssociativeCache` struct fields unchanged apart from any sizing that follows directly from the bucket count.

The Fibonacci multiplicative bucket index from the parent stays in place. Recompute its shift or mask so it maps onto the new, larger power-of-two bucket count. Confirm the bucket count stays a power of two so the index computation remains a shift.

Keep the exact minimum-counter victim search and the periodic global halving schedule unchanged. The victim scan now covers 16 slots instead of 32, so it needs no logic change. If the parent keeps any per-bucket mask or word-level scan sized to the bucket width, resize it to 16 bits or 16 positions and keep its semantics the same.

Invariants to preserve: total capacity remains 2048 slots; the get path still probes only the one bucket selected by the hash; hit-rate and per-op metrics are measured the same way as the parent.

## Rationale (optional)

Cycles 0004 through 0009 found that every shortcut to the victim search either lost hit-rate or left the put tail unchanged, and no one has tried shrinking the scan itself by changing geometry. Cycle 0001 widened buckets for a small hit-rate gain with a longer get scan, which implies the reverse direction is a plausible timing lever. The hypothesis is that narrower buckets give up a little hit-rate, since fewer slots compete for each key, while lowering the linear-scan cost on both get and put. A flat or small loss in hit-rate with a clear drop in median per-op time would be a Winner on the timing dimension.
