# H01

Caching the minimum-counter slot per bucket will cut the put-side p50 of cycle/0002/v003 below its 168 ns while leaving hit-rate exactly at 0.7278, targeting per-op-time.

## Parent

cycle/0002/v003

## Changes

Keep v003's victim policy (evict the slot with the lowest per-slot saturating access counter) but stop re-scanning the whole bucket on every put. Add a per-bucket cached index of the current minimum-counter slot.

- On a put into a full bucket, if the cached index is valid, evict that slot directly and do not scan. If it is invalid, run the existing full-bucket min scan once and store the result.
- On a get hit, increment the counter as v003 already does. If the hit slot is the cached minimum, mark the cache invalid (its counter just rose, so the true minimum may now be elsewhere). Otherwise leave the cache alone. Do not add a scan on the get path.
- When a slot is filled or replaced by a put, set the cache to that slot if its new counter is at or below the cached minimum's counter, otherwise leave it, or invalidate it when unsure.
- Tie-breaking must match v003 exactly (same slot wins among equal counters) so eviction decisions, and therefore hit-rate, are identical to v003. The cache is an optimisation only.
- Preserve the existing counter width, saturation behaviour, and the non-frequency parts of put and get. Add no bytes to the cache struct beyond the per-bucket index, and do not rely on the metadata-size probe to report it.

Expected test: hit-rate on workload-a must equal 0.7278 to four decimals. Put p50 should drop; get p50 should stay within noise of 71 ns.

## Rationale (optional)

v003's insight says the put-side full-bucket scan is the main cost and that a running minimum is the first thing to try. Because the policy is unchanged, this hypothesis isolates the put-side cost from the hit-rate question.
