# H01

Lower metadata-size below the baseline's 40 by folding the saturating reuse counter into the existing one-bit seen-in-stash marker, holding hit-rate within noise of 0.684.

## Parent

cycle/0008/v002

## Changes

Remove the multi-bit saturating reuse counter from the per-entry metadata and make the one-bit seen-in-stash marker carry all reuse state.

- In the entry metadata definition, delete the 2-bit reuse-counter field introduced by cycle/0006/v001 and keep the seen-in-stash bit as the only reuse flag.
- At every site that increments, saturates, halves, or resets the reuse counter (the hit path, the normal-tier demotion path, and the store-skip branch from cycle/0008/v002), replace the counter operation with the equivalent one-bit operation: a hit on a stash-resident entry sets the marker; a normal-tier demotion clears it.
- Delete the saturated-counter gate from cycle/0008/v001 and the store-skip guard from cycle/0008/v002, since both depend on the removed counter. The second-hit promotion gate reads the marker and otherwise keeps its current form.
- Keep MRU landing of stash-hit promotions, the probing tier, the stash victim order, and the foreign-eviction target retry exactly as in the parent.
- Do not change the tier capacities.

Record metadata-size and per-op-time for this variant alongside hit-rate. The claim is confirmed if metadata-size drops below 40 and hit-rate on lirs workload-a at size 2048 stays within 0.001 of 0.6844.

## Rationale (optional)

The reuse-counter lineage has been flat on hit-rate for four cycles (cycles 6 to 8), so its only remaining value is cost. Its metadata bits are the one cost that is clearly known to exist, which makes them the natural target for a metadata-size experiment. The one-bit marker already carries the second-hit signal that produced the only real hit-rate gain, so a reuse-state encoding that keeps that signal should preserve hit-rate. The overlay sources could not be read in this pass, so the exact function names need confirming against the parent before editing.
