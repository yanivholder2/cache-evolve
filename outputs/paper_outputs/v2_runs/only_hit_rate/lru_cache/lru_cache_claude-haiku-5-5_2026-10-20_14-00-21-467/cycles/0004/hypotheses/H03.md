# H03

Splitting the baseline recency list into a probation segment and a protected segment, with eviction taken from probation first, raises hit-rate on workload-a at size 2048 above the baseline's 0.665 by keeping one-touch keys from displacing re-referenced keys.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Introduce two segments over the existing recency list: a probation segment for newly inserted entries and a protected segment for entries that have been hit at least once. On a miss, insert the new entry at the MRU end of probation. On a hit in probation, move the entry to the MRU end of protected; if protected exceeds 80 percent of capacity, demote its LRU entry to the MRU end of probation. On a hit in protected, move it to the MRU end of protected as the baseline does. In the eviction path, `evict_one()` or the equivalent victim-selection code, take the LRU entry of probation when probation is non-empty, and fall back to the LRU entry of protected otherwise. Keep the key index, the node layout, and the return values of the public operations unchanged. Do not add any ghost or admission filter.

## Rationale (optional)

Every variant so far has either preserved eviction order or tried admission, and none has tested a segmented recency structure. Segmenting the list changes which entry is evicted, so hit-rate is the primary dimension and should move. A scan of one-touch keys then pushes out only probation entries, not the re-referenced working set.
