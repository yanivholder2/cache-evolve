# H03

Sizing the small queue adaptively from the observed ghost-hit rate should beat the fixed small-queue ratio on lirs workload-a at size 2048.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Replace the fixed small-queue capacity with a capacity that adjusts over time, in the style of ARC's adaptive target, bounded to a fixed range.

- Keep the total cache capacity unchanged. The small queue has a target size `small_target`, initialised to the baseline's fixed small-queue size.
- Track a ghost-hit counter. On each hit in the ghost queue, nudge `small_target` toward the queue that would have retained the key: increment it when the ghost hit came from the small-queue side (a key evicted from small that returns soon), decrement it otherwise. Use a step of 1 slot per ghost hit.
- Clamp `small_target` to between 10% and 30% of total capacity. Pick the bounds as named constants so they are easy to change.
- Use `small_target` wherever the baseline compares small-queue occupancy against its fixed size, in the admission and eviction routines. Do not alter the promotion bar, the frequency logic, or the ghost queue's size.
- Initialise the counter and target so that the first run behaves identically to the baseline until a ghost hit occurs.

Before editing, read the baseline admission and eviction paths to find the small-queue size comparison, and change only that comparison.

## Rationale (optional)

Earlier cycles tried single constant changes to the small queue and found no signal (cycle 0001). The open question from cycle 0001 was whether a larger, adaptive change to small-queue sizing moves hit-rate. This hypothesis tests that directly with a bounded, self-correcting sizing rule. It stays off the promotion bar and the ghost window, which earlier cycles found saturated or unproductive. Success means hit-rate above 0.7164 on the same trace and size. Report per-op-time and metadata-size, since the extra counter adds a small cost that must be measured.
