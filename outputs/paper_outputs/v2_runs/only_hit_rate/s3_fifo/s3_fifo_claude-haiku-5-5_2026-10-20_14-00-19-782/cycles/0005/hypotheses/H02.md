# H02

Decaying main-queue frequency by two per reclaim pass instead of one should raise hit-rate by letting hot main-queue entries survive more eviction sweeps.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Change only the main-queue reclaim (eviction) loop, the code path that scans main-queue candidates when a slot is needed and decides whether to reinsert or evict each one.

- When a main-queue candidate has frequency greater than zero, reinsert it at the tail as today, but decrement its frequency by 2 (clamped at zero) instead of by 1.
- Keep the frequency cap, the small-queue promotion bar, the ghost queue, and the small-queue sizing exactly as in baseline.
- Entries whose frequency is already zero are evicted exactly as in baseline.

Before editing, confirm in the baseline source what the current decrement rule is. If the baseline already decrements by a different amount, change it by the same single step (make it a decay of two relative to the baseline rule) and record the baseline rule in the variant's notes.

## Rationale (optional)

The frequency cap was raised in cycle 0002 with no effect, and the promotion bar is saturated, but the decay rate of frequency in the main queue has not been tried. Faster decay makes entries reach zero sooner, so it is a different lever from the cap. The claim is testable on lirs workload-a at size 2048: if hot entries are being evicted after one sweep too many, hit-rate should rise above 0.7164. If hit-rate stays flat or falls, the main-queue decay is not the lever on this trace. Report per-op-time and metadata-size alongside hit-rate.
