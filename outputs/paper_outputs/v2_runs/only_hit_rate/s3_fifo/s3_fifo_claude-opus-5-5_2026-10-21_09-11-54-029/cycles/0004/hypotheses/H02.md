# H02

Raising the frequency cap from 7 to 15 on top of cycle/0003/v003 will raise hit-rate on workload-a by keeping the hottest main-queue keys resident through longer bursts of ghost re-admission evictions.

## Parent

cycle/0003/v003

## Changes

In `src/s3_fifo/mod.rs`, change `const MAX_FREQ: u8 = 7;` to `const MAX_FREQ: u8 = 15;` and update the header comment. The termination bound for `evict_main` becomes `main.len() * 15`.

Make no other policy change:
- `get` keeps clamping with `.min(MAX_FREQ)` in both small and main.
- `evict_small` keeps the `freq > 1` graduation threshold.
- Ghost re-admission stays front-of-main at freq 0.
- The 5% split is unchanged.
- `evict_main` keeps decrementing by 1 per rotation.

Update the tests:
- Rename `main_resident_frequency_saturates_at_seven` so that it asserts freq saturates at 15. Call `get` at least 20 times.
- Rename and update `max_freq_key_survives_seven_main_passes_then_evicted_on_eighth` so that the hot key survives 15 passes and is evicted on the 16th.

Keep the linear-scan structures as they are, so the hit-rate delta can be attributed only to the cap.

## Rationale (optional)

Main-queue retention has been the only new axis to win since the partition curve flattened. Going from 3 to 7 gained +0.0014. This variant checks whether the curve keeps rising past 7 or is already flat, which is an explicit open question from cycle 0003. A flat or negative result would tell us that the frequency signal on workload-a saturates at about 7 reuses, and that further retention work should target aging rather than the cap. The expected cost is longer `evict_main` sweeps when most of main is hot. That cost should show up in per-op-time and is worth recording.
