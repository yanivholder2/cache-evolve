# H01

Stacking the cycle-0005 eviction-next miss-insert position on the shared-frequency admission gate raises workload-a hit-rate above the incumbent's 0.7427.

## Parent

cycle/0005/v001

## Changes

Keep the shared-frequency admission gate in `AssociativeCache::put()` (`src/associative_cache/mod.rs`) and the probationary-first eviction in `AssociativeCacheBucket` exactly as the parent has them. Change only where a newly admitted miss is placed inside the bucket. In `AssociativeCacheBucket::put()` (`src/associative_cache/associative_cache_bucket.rs`), the new-item branch currently inserts at `self.items.insert(self.prob_len, item)` and increments `prob_len`. Replace that placement with the eviction-next position used by the cycle-0005 v003 overlay (`cycles/0005/variants/v003`), so that a newly admitted key is ranked as the next eviction candidate among the probationary items rather than at the back of the probationary slice. Use the same placement rule that v003 used, not the victim-end placement tested in cycle 0007 v001. Leave updates of resident keys, `promote()`, `touch_protected()`, and `evict_victim()` unchanged, and keep `prob_len` consistent with the region split. Do not add per-entry fields.

The experiment is a direct test of the open question from cycle 0005: whether the admission gate and the eviction-next insert position are additive. A gain over the parent on workload-a supports stacking. A loss or no change says the cycle-0005 v003 gain was redundant with the gate.
