# H02

Inserting keys admitted by the shared-frequency gate at the eviction-next position instead of MRU raises workload-a hit-rate because the admission filter and the insert position act on different decisions and should stack.

## Parent

cycle/0005/v001

## Changes

Start from the cycle 0005 v001 incumbent. Leave `put()`'s admission comparison, the `freq` counter, and the probationary-first victim selection in `eviction_candidate_index()` unchanged.

Change only where an admitted key is placed in its bucket:

- Read the cycle 0005 v003 overlay first and reuse its definition of the eviction-next insert position exactly. That variant placed misses at the eviction-next slot instead of MRU. Confirm the slot semantics from that overlay before implementing, so that the change matches it and does not reproduce the victim-end placement that lost hit-rate in cycles 0007 and 0008.
- Apply the placement only to inserts that pass the admission gate in `put()`, i.e. the path that reaches `bucket.put(Item::new(key, value))` after the bypass check. Keys inserted into a non-full bucket use the same placement, since they are also admitted.
- Keep the bucket's `is_full()`, `get_position()`, and `eviction_candidate_index()` logic intact. The only new behaviour is the slot the admitted item occupies.
- Do not change the `freq` update in `get_and_update_item()` and do not add metadata.

## Rationale (optional)

Cycle 0005 v003 reported a gain from eviction-next placement alone, and cycle 0005 v001 reported a gain from the admission gate. The ledger's open question is whether these two one-shot filters stack or are redundant. Cycle 0005 never tested them together. This hypothesis pairs the admission gate with the placement from v003 and keeps the gate's decision fixed, so any change in hit-rate is attributable to placement on top of the gate.
