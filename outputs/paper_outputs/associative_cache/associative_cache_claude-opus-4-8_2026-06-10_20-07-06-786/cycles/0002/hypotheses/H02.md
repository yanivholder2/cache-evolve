# H02

Aging the frequency counters on every eviction prevents once-hot keys from becoming immortal under v002's pure-LFU victim rule, raising hit-rate on the shifting working set.

## Parent

cycle/0001/v002

## Changes

Start from v002's `AssociativeCacheBucket`, which evicts the
minimum-`lfu_counter` item and bumps `lfu_counter` on every hit. The weakness
of this pure-frequency rule is staleness: a key that was hot early accumulates a
high count and then resists eviction forever even after it goes cold, crowding
out newly hot keys. Add frequency aging by overriding the bucket's `evict_item`
method (currently the trait default): after selecting and removing the
min-frequency victim via `eviction_candidate_index`, apply a decay pass over the
*remaining* items, setting each survivor's `lfu_counter = lfu_counter >> 1`
(halving, saturating at 0). This is the classic "aging LFU" / window-LFU decay:
recent activity keeps counts high, but counts erode each time the bucket is
under eviction pressure, so a long-idle former-hot key gradually becomes
evictable. Preserve v002's `get_from_position` (bump + back-promote) and
`eviction_candidate_index` (min-by-frequency, first-on-tie) unchanged. Keep the
evict-exactly-one invariant (P1) — decay only mutates surviving counters, never
the resident set or capacity. Leave the `EvictionPolicyKind` enum and JNI
ordinal mapping untouched.

## Rationale (optional)

Cycle-0001 asked whether the dominant pressure is scan traffic or capacity
collision; aging directly targets the failure mode where stale high-frequency
entries pin capacity, a hypothesis distinct from H01's admission-side change.
