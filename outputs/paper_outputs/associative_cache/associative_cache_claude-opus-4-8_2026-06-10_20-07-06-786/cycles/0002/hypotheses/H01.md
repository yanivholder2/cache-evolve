# H01

Layering v003's probationary front admission on top of v002's frequency-first victim selection composes their scan-resistance and lifts hit-rate further than either alone.

## Parent

cycle/0001/v002

## Changes

Start from v002's `AssociativeCacheBucket` (frequency-first victim selection:
`get_from_position` bumps `lfu_counter` then ClassicLRU-promotes to the back,
and `eviction_candidate_index` returns the `min_by_key(lfu_counter)` index with
first-on-tie ordering). Add a `put` override on the bucket — mirroring v003 —
so that a *new* key is admitted at index `0` (via `self.items.insert(0, item)`)
instead of pushed to the back: the freshly inserted item carries `lfu_counter = 0`,
making it both the front (recency-oldest) and minimum-frequency entry, i.e. the
immediate next eviction candidate until it earns a hit. Keep the existing-key
update branch identical to the trait default (overwrite value in place, then
`get_from_position` to promote per the frequency rule). Preserve the
evict-exactly-one-when-full invariant (P1): call `self.evict_item()` before the
`insert(0, …)` when `is_full()`. Do not touch the `EvictionPolicyKind` enum, its
JNI ordinal mapping, bucket geometry, or routing. The hypothesis is that
one-hit-wonders and scan traffic now die at the front before accumulating any
frequency, while genuinely reused keys both climb toward the back and build a
protective `lfu_counter`, combining both levers' working-set protection.

## Rationale (optional)

The cycle-0001 insight flagged "do the orthogonal levers compose?" as the
central open question; v002 (0.7231) and v003 (0.7182) both attacked scan
traffic from different ends, so stacking them is the highest-leverage test of
whether their gains are additive or overlapping.
