# H03

Deriving the bucket split directly from the total capacity to hit an explicit target associativity — rather than repeatedly halving the harness-provided split — raises hit-rate by landing on collision-set widths the power-of-two halving sequence skips over.

## Parent

cycle/0006/v001

## Changes

Replace the relative "halve the harness split" mechanism in
`src/shared/geometry.rs` with an **absolute** associativity target computed from
the budget. In `BucketGeometry::layout(&self, total_capacity)`:

1. Pick a target per-bucket associativity `T` (start with `T = 8`; this is the
   tunable under test).
2. Choose `bucket_size` as the **divisor of `total_capacity` closest to `T`**:
   iterate candidate divisors `d` of `total_capacity` (those where
   `total_capacity % d == 0`), and select the one minimising `|d - T|`. Set
   `bucket_size = d`, `num_of_buckets = total_capacity / d`.
3. Because the chosen `bucket_size` divides `total_capacity` exactly,
   `num_of_buckets * bucket_size == total_capacity` holds for **every** budget
   (including primes, where the only divisors are 1 and the budget itself), so P4
   `capacity_conservation` passes across the gate's full `nb∈1..32, bs∈1..128`
   sweep. Guard the empty/degenerate case by falling back to the identity split
   if no divisor is found (cannot happen for `total_capacity >= 1`, since 1 always
   divides).

Leave the segmented bucket logic untouched — this only changes how the fixed
budget is factored. Unlike H01 (which doubles associativity in power-of-two
steps relative to the swept `num_of_buckets`), this targets an absolute width and
can reach non-power-of-two splits (e.g. a 6- or 10-way set) that the halving
sequence cannot express, letting the cohort triangulate the true associativity
optimum rather than only the powers of two around the parent.

## Rationale

The parent's win came from one power-of-two halving, but the geometry sweet spot
may sit between halving steps. Parametrising by an absolute divisor-based target
explores the geometry axis at resolutions the relative-halving strategy cannot
reach, while keeping capacity exactly conserved and the proven SLRU behaviour
intact.
