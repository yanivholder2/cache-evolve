# H01

Enlarging the protected segment from 80% to 90% of capacity raises hit-rate on the reuse-heavy LIRS trace by giving genuinely hot keys more residency.

## Parent

cycle/0001/v001

## Changes

In `try_new`, change the protected budget from `let protected_cap = (capacity * 4) / 5;`
to `let protected_cap = (capacity * 9) / 10;` (floor of 0.9 * capacity). The
probationary budget remains `capacity - protected_cap`, so the two segment
budgets still sum to exactly `capacity` and the P1 capacity bound is unchanged.
Update the `log::info!` line in `try_new` accordingly (it already prints
`protected_cap` and the derived probationary cap; no wording change needed
beyond the new value flowing through).

Touch nothing else: keep promote-on-first-hit in `get`/`put`, keep
`enforce_protected_budget` demoting the protected LRU node to the probationary
MRU end, and keep the probationary-first eviction order in `put`. The sole diff
is the ratio constant.

Preserve all invariants: `prob_len + prot_len == map.len()`, residency never
exceeds `capacity`, and slot reuse in place on eviction. Note the edge case at
capacity 2048 (the eval size): `protected_cap` becomes 1843 with 205
probationary slots — confirm the probationary segment stays non-empty so the
probationary-first eviction path still has a victim under normal load.

## Rationale (optional)

Cycle 0002 proved every "loosen the parent" lever (wider probationary, two-hit
gate, ghost-to-protected) regressed, and its continuation explicitly asks
whether pushing the ratio the *other* way (85/15, 90/10) keeps winning. This is
the lowest-risk, highest-confidence probe of that open question and isolates the
protected-residency lever from every other change.
