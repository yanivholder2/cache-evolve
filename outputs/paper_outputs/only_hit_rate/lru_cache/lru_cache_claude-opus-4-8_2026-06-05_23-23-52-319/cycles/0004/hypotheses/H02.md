# H02

Adding a bounded ghost list of evicted keys that re-admits a returning key directly into the protected-LRU end, layered on the 90/10 winner, recovers the thin warm-but-evicted reuse band and lifts hit-rate.

## Parent

cycle/0003/v001

## Changes

Add reuse-distance memory on top of the existing 90/10 SLRU. Introduce a
ghost structure that stores only keys (no values, no arena slots): a
`HashMap<K, ()>` plus a `VecDeque<K>` (or an intrusive key-only LRU list)
bounded to at most `capacity` keys.

- In `put`'s eviction branch (the `self.nodes.len() >= self.capacity`
  arm), after computing `evicted_key` and removing it from `map`, push
  `evicted_key` onto the MRU end of the ghost list; if the ghost list now
  exceeds its bound, pop its LRU key. Slot reuse in the arena is
  unchanged.
- Still in `put`, when inserting a genuinely new key, first check the
  ghost list. On a **ghost hit** (key present in the ghost structure):
  remove it from the ghost list and insert the new entry at the
  **protected-LRU end** — i.e. call a new helper `push_front_protected`
  that links the node just *before* `prot_head` and sets
  `protected = true`, then run `enforce_protected_budget`. A ghost hit
  signals the key churned out of probation but is genuinely reused, so it
  earns *weak* protection (front/LRU of protected), not the strong
  MRU placement that regressed in earlier cycles. On a **ghost miss**,
  keep the parent behaviour: enter at the probationary MRU end via
  `push_back_probationary`.

Preserve all existing invariants: `prob_len + prot_len == map.len()`,
`map.len() <= capacity`, the protected budget enforced by
`enforce_protected_budget`, and that a full cache always has a victim
(ghost entries hold no slots, so they never affect the resident count).
A key that is currently resident must never also sit in the ghost list —
remove a key from the ghost structure the moment it is re-admitted.

## Rationale (optional)

Cycle 0003's weak-protection ghost (v003) was directionally right but
tested against the original parent and barely moved. The cross-cycle
open question is whether it contributes once stacked on the
maximally-protected 90/10 winner rather than in isolation. This layers
exactly that mechanism onto the incumbent.
