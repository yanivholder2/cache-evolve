# H01

Improve hit rate by combining unified two-tier hierarchy (stash + hot) with occupancy-aware demotion routing to test whether tier simplification paired with load-sensitive eviction flow enables breakthrough beyond the 0.684 ceiling.

## Parent

cycle/0008/v001

## Changes

Implement a merged architecture combining v002's tier simplification with v001's occupancy-aware demotion logic:

1. In `src/shared/geometry.rs` and `src/dash/segments/segment_core.rs`, modify the tier structure to use two tiers (Stash and a unified Hot tier) instead of three (Stash, Target, Probing). Allocate all 28 normal buckets to Hot tier instead of splitting 14+14.

2. In `src/dash/segments/dash_segment.rs`, modify `promotion_get()` to insert stash-hit items directly into the Hot tier (not a two-stage target→probing advancement). Remove the intermediate target-tier filtering step.

3. Modify `demote_from_normal_bucket()` to implement v001's occupancy-aware demotion logic: 
   - Before demoting from Hot tier to Stash, check Hot tier occupancy
   - If Hot tier occupancy < 70%, demote directly to Stash with back insertion (MRU) as in cycle 0003's proven mechanism
   - If Hot tier occupancy ≥ 70%, trigger accelerated cascade: demote to Stash with back insertion but also mark in the item to trigger faster re-demotion if accessed again before reaching equilibrium, effectively reducing cascade depth under congestion
   
4. Preserve demotion-path back insertion (cycle 0003's proven mechanism) on all demotions to maintain MRU positioning for recovered items.

This tests whether the bottleneck defending the 0.684 ceiling is the intermediate filtering overhead (v002's insight) combined with load-aware routing decisions (v001's insight), and whether their synergy can achieve breakthrough when both are applied to a unified architecture.
