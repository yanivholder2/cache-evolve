Tighten the small-queue admission ratio from 1/12 to 1/13 while maintaining MAX_FREQ=5 to test whether more aggressive structural selectivity compounds synergistically with proven frequency protection at the saturation boundary.

## Parent

cycle/0007/v002

## Changes

Modify the `try_new()` method in `/src/s3_fifo/mod.rs` to further reduce the small-queue capacity. Change the line containing `(capacity / 12).max(1).min(capacity - 1)` to `(capacity / 13).max(1).min(capacity - 1)` when computing `small_size`. Keep MAX_FREQ at 5 (do not change `const MAX_FREQ: u8 = 5;`). All other logic remains unchanged: frequency increment on access, freq > 1 threshold for small-to-main promotion, main-queue eviction strategy identical. This isolates the structural dimension to determine whether tighter selectivity at the saturation boundary can unlock incremental gains when paired with the proven frequency depth.

## Rationale

Cycle 0007 v002 (1/12+MAX_FREQ=5) won with 0.727 hit-rate by pairing structure and frequency for the first time. However, cycle 0007 v001's 1/13 in isolation yielded only +0.0003 gain over 1/12, a marginal improvement. The open question asks: "Can 1/13+MAX_FREQ=5 unlock greater gains when paired with MAX_FREQ=5 than 1/13 achieved alone?" This hypothesis tests whether the frequency protection enables tighter structural selectivity to deliver larger compounding returns at the saturation boundary, or whether the structure dimension has truly saturated at 1/12.
