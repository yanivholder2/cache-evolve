# H02

Implement three-level tiering (hot/warm/cold) to better match workload's Zipfian re-access distribution and reduce hot-tier churn from oscillating items.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Extend the `AssociativeCache<K, V>` structure from a two-tier (hot/cold) stash to a three-tier (hot/warm/cold) hierarchy.

**Tier allocation**: Replace the current hot/cold partition with hot/warm/cold allocation:
- Hot tier: 10% of total cache capacity (was 20% in the baseline two-tier design)
- Warm tier: 10% of total cache capacity (new intermediate tier)
- Cold tier: 5% of total cache capacity (unchanged from two-tier)
- Total stash: 25% (unchanged)

**Tier fields**: Add `warm_stash: Vec<Item<K, V>>` and `warm_stash_capacity: usize` to the cache struct, computed similarly to hot and cold tiers based on total capacity.

**Promotion rules**:
- **Cold-to-warm**: When an item in the cold stash is accessed (on a cold-tier hit during `get_and_update_item()`), promote it to the warm tier if warm tier has space. If warm is full, evict the LRU item from warm to cold (pushing the least-recently-used warm-tier item down).
- **Warm-to-hot**: When an item in the warm stash is accessed (on a warm-tier hit), promote it to the hot tier if hot tier has space. If hot is full, evict the LRU item from hot to warm (creating space in hot for the warm-tier hit).
- **Bucket-to-cold**: Items evicted from buckets enter cold tier directly (unchanged from two-tier mechanism).

**Eviction within tiers**: Apply LRU (last-accessed-time-based) eviction when a tier is at capacity and a new item needs to enter from below (or from promotion). This includes cold→warm overflow eviction.

**Lookup and access path**: During `get_and_update_item()`, follow the lookup sequence: primary bucket → hot stash → warm stash → cold stash. On hit at any tier, update `last_accessed_time` and apply promotion rules accordingly.

Preserve the Item struct's `last_accessed_time` field tracking from v002. All other cache mechanics (geometry, total capacity, FIFO insertion into stash tiers) remain unchanged.

This tests whether an intermediate tier buffers items that oscillate between frequent and infrequent access, reducing the churn of items being constantly promoted/demoted between hot and cold, and better matching the Zipfian distribution of the workload's re-access patterns.
