# H03

Detecting workload phase transitions via collision-distribution changes and adaptively adjusting collision-counter reset frequency will improve hit rate by allowing the cache to respond dynamically to access-pattern shifts.

## Parent

baseline

## Changes

Modify `src/associative_cache/mod.rs` to add phase-aware collision management. Implement operation counting and per-phase collision tracking:

1. Track the distribution of collision counts across all buckets over each 1000-operation phase window (e.g., compute mean and standard deviation of collision_count per bucket)
2. Every 2000 operations, compute the coefficient of variation (std_dev / mean) of collision distribution. If the current phase's CV differs from the prior phase's CV by > 20 percentage points (indicating a change in collision clustering pattern), classify this as a **phase transition** and set `in_transition = true`
3. During normal operation, reset collision counters every 1000 operations. During detected phase transitions, increase reset frequency to every 500 operations for 2000 operations (4 resets) to accelerate cache tuning to the new access pattern
4. After 2000 operations of active transition, revert to normal 1000-operation reset windows

Retain the 0.71 geometric factor and 25% stash FIFO from the baseline v001 design. Do not modify stash insertion or eviction logic.

The hypothesis expects this adaptive tuning to improve hit rate on workloads with distinct access phases by preventing stale collision history from interfering with recovery decisions during transitions, allowing the cache to re-tune more rapidly when workload characteristics change.

## Rationale (optional)

Cycle 0005's continuation guidance explicitly suggests "workload phase detection and dynamic adaptation" as an escape path from the local optimum. Cycles 0003–0005 repeatedly tested static filtering signals (occupancy, collision count, capacity) without improvement, suggesting that adaptive responsiveness to changing patterns may be more productive than static thresholds. This hypothesis implements phase detection through collision-distribution analysis (orthogonal to prior signals) and couples it with aggressive collision-counter resets during transitions to enable rapid re-tuning, a fundamentally different optimization axis than geometry or capacity scaling.
