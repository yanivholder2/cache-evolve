# H01

Replacing mid-point insertion with O(1) back-append in the incumbent cold-first stack reduces per-op-time without regressing hit-rate, because cold-first eviction and the warm-lock fallback at `remove_at(0)` are both position-independent for newly inserted cold items.

## Parent

cycle/0004/v001

## Changes

In `put()` in `src/associative_cache/associative_cache_bucket.rs`, replace the
mid-point insertion block:

```rust
let mid = self.items.len() / 2;
self.items.insert(mid, item);
(&self.items[mid], evicted_item)
```

with a back-append:

```rust
self.items.push(item);
let back = self.items.len() - 1;
(&self.items[back], evicted_item)
```

No other changes. The cold-first linear scan in `evict_item` (`items.iter().position(|item| item.lfu_counter == 0)`) and the warm-lock decay + `remove_at(0)` fallback are left exactly as in the parent.

**Reasoning for correctness**: New items have `lfu_counter == 0` regardless of insertion
position. The cold-first scan finds the *first* cold item by scanning from index 0
forward; since older items that have not been hit cluster at the front (positional LRU
from `get_from_position`), those front-cold items are evicted before the newly appended
back-cold item. The warm-lock fallback evicts `items[0]`, which is the oldest item;
new items at the back are maximally distant from index 0, so they are equally (or
more) protected from warm-lock eviction than they were at mid-point. The O(n/2)
average shift cost of `Vec::insert` is eliminated; `Vec::push` is O(1) amortised.

**Invariants to preserve**: capacity conservation (no change), key-uniqueness (no
change), eviction before any insert when `is_full()` (no change). The existing-key
update path at the top of `put()` is untouched.

## Rationale

The O(n) cost of `vec.insert(mid, item)` on a 64-slot bucket requires shifting ~32
elements on every new insertion. Per-op-time is the one fitness dimension that has
been consistently unmeasured across all cycles; this change makes the expected saving
concrete and measurable without altering eviction semantics. If per-op-time parity
with the baseline is confirmed, the combined hit-rate + latency picture determines
whether the incumbent stack's complexity is justified.
