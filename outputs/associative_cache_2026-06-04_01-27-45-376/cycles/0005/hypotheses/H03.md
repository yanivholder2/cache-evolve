# H03

Periodically sacrificing the freshly-inserted slot instead of v003's victim provides scan-resistance that protects long-resident hot items during one-shot floods, raising hit-rate without per-item state.

## Parent

cycle/0001/v003

## Changes

Add a per-bucket eviction counter to `AssociativeCacheBucket` and use it to alternate between v003's normal pick and a "newest-slot" sacrifice on a fixed cadence:

1. In `src/associative_cache/associative_cache_bucket.rs`, add `evict_count: Cell<u32>` to the struct, initialised to `0` in `new`. (Same metadata-neutral vehicle that cycle/0004/v002 already validated — bucket-side state does not show up in the `Item<u64,u64>` or `AssociativeCache<u64,u64>` measurements.)
2. Override `Bucket::evict_item` on `AssociativeCacheBucket`. Inside:
   - If the configured policy is not `ClassicLRU`, delegate to the trait default (so non-`ClassicLRU` policies see baseline behaviour).
   - Otherwise, increment `evict_count` and read its current value `n`. Choose the cadence `K = self.max_size` (the bucket capacity — i.e., once per bucket-worth of evictions on average).
   - If `n % K == 0` AND `self.items.len() >= 2`: call `self.get_items_mut().remove(self.get_items().len() - 1)` to evict the newest (back) slot directly, bypassing v003's filter. Return that item wrapped in `Some`.
   - Otherwise: fall through to the existing v003 logic — `self.eviction_candidate_index()` then `self.remove_at(idx)`.
3. Leave `eviction_policy.rs` exactly as it is in the parent (v003's two-pass scan on `ClassicLRU::eviction_candidate_index`); the entire change lives on the bucket so direct trait callers keep seeing v003.

The behavioural claim: under move-to-back, ClassicLRU is vulnerable to one-shot scan floods that insert K never-re-referenced keys, each of which evicts a useful long-resident hot item from slot 0. v003 mitigates this on the eviction side (preferring `lfu_counter == 0` slots), but once a scan has inserted a few un-hit slots that all sit untouched, the next eviction targets the OLDEST of them — which may itself be a genuine working-set item that just happened to land in front. Forcibly sacrificing the *newest* slot once per bucket-worth of evictions guarantees that scan-pattern keys (which arrive at the back and never get a hit before being shoved further back by the next scan-insertion) are pruned at their freshest, before they crowd out items that have already earned protection.

Distinction from prior cohorts: cycle/0004/v003 changed scan *direction* (reverse from the back) and applied that change to *every* eviction; this change keeps v003's scan direction intact and only fires the newest-slot sacrifice on a deterministic cadence (1 in K). Cycle/0004/v001 inserted at the victim's index (gap-fill on the access path); this change leaves insertion alone and targets eviction only.

## Rationale (optional)

Scan-resistance via periodic newest-slot sacrifice is the one mechanism the cohort has not exercised — every prior attempt to spend information on protecting hot items has either touched the access path (lost) or replaced v003's filter (lost). Spending one in K evictions on the assumption that scans dominate the bucket gives the LRU front a defensive grace it currently lacks.
